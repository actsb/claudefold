## D. How to find the model number and what owners should do
- JKMAX: back of the remote, plus white label on packaging (CPSC 26-778 page). Owners who threw out the box: look at the remote back; also check Amazon order history for the product title (that part is a general suggestion, UNVERIFIED as official guidance).
- OCTROT: model number is on the packaging; controller cord with 2 or 4 small holes at the lower connection point (CPSC 26-652 page).
- Bedsure (2023 recall): white label on back of blanket/pad, back of controller, order history (CPSC 23-735 page).
- Sunbeam 2023 recall: wash label on the blanket (CPSC 23-157 page).
- MaxKare: attached tag (CPSC 25-351 page).
- Bedsure's own troubleshooting says the model number on the blanket label should match the one on the controller back, and to use only the included controller; F-codes or black screen: check controller not covered, unplug/reconnect, try another outlet, contact support (search-result summary of https://bedsurehome.com/blogs/products/heated-blanket-guidebook, accessed 2026-11-04; the page itself came back truncated on fetch, so the exact wording is UNVERIFIED).
- Steps per CPSC pages: stop using, unplug, contact the firm for a refund; for OCTROT, MaxKare, Sunbeam 2023 the firm requires proof of destruction (cut cord/photo). JKMAX page lists refund only. Check the CPSC SaferProducts database by brand and model (State Farm, Sep 18, 2026: https://www.statefarm.com/simple-insights/safety/the-dos-and-donts-of-electric-blanket-safety).

## F. Failure modes (what fails first)
- Controller or its connector is the recurring weak point in recalls and owner anecdotes: Bedsure 2023 recall hazard was "the controller can malfunction" (CPSC 23-735); OCTROT 2026 hazard is the cord detaching at the connection, with holes at the controller cord's lower connection (CPSC 26-652); Biddeford 2001 recall was the plug connecting the detachable control becoming loose and overheating (CPSC 02-003, Oct 4, 2001). Sunbeam/Walmart owner reports are dominated by blinking/F2 controller faults (Section E, dates UNVERIFIED).
- Internal heating wire overheating is the JKMAX, MaxKare and Melinora hazard (CPSC pages above).
- Wire kinks/folding: fire services and State Farm advise not to fold, bunch, tuck under a mattress or place items on a heating blanket, and to keep it flat or loosely rolled (State Farm, Sep 18, 2026, URL above; Maine State Fire Marshal PDF https://www.maine.gov/dps/fmo/sites/maine.gov.dps.fmo/files/inline-files/electric_blanket_safety.pdf, date UNVERIFIED). The 2007 Beautyrest-branded throw recall (07-276) cited bunching/folding/tucking as the overheating trigger.
- Inspection signs: fraying, scorch marks, exposed wires, lumps, cord cracks, controller damage; plug directly into the wall, no extension cord or power strip, test auto shutoff while awake (State Farm, same page).
- No hard statistic on "which part fails first" was found; the statement above is a qualitative pattern from recalls and anecdotes.
