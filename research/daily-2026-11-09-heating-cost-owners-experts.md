## 0. Headline findings for the post

1. Wattage is the weak spot. Makers rarely print watts in web copy. Only two of the five picks have a maker-quality wattage line: the Dreo Atom One (1500 W, manual) and the oil-filled heater (De'Longhi TRH0715 1500 W on the maker's PDF; Pelonis HO-0279 1500 W with 600/900/1500 settings). Mattress pad, queen blanket and Bedsure throw wattage all come from retailer text or forum measurement. Say "rated" vs "typical" in the post.
2. Dreo recall (new, matters): CPSC recall 27-054, 2026-10-29, Dreo DR-HSH015 ceramic heater (about 338,800 US units, made Apr 2025 to Jan 2026, sold at Costco, Dreo.com, Best Buy, Kohl's, Lowe's, QVC, Home Depot). Our blog's Dreo review is the Atom One DR-HSH004 (per posts/2026-10-dreo-space-heater), which is NOT on the recall. The post must name the model number and tell readers to check the underside label. Our earlier Dreo post said no Dreo recall was found as of Oct 15; the recall postdates it. The existing post may need a correction note.
3. Bedsure throw: model BS-HB5060 was in the March 2023 Bedshe/Bedsure recall (23-735, about 350,000 units). Current throw listings are a different build; whether a current unit is a redesigned controller is UNVERIFIED. Must disclose.
4. Energy math (my arithmetic, label as such): cost = kWh x price. EIA-cited US residential average is about 17.5 to 18.8 cents/kWh in 2026 (see section C). Consumer Reports uses $2.10 per 8 h for a 1,500 W heater on high (national average, page updated 2025-10-12).

## C. Experts and measured numbers

- Consumer Reports, "Will Using a Space Heater Save You Money?" (updated 2025-10-12): "Most use 1,500 watts when on the highest heat setting, which comes to about $2.10 per 8-hour day or about $64 a month, based on the average cost of electricity nationally." Heaters "should be used as supplemental heat, not as a substitute for a central system"; one "could be less expensive than cranking up your central heating system" for "a small, drafty room." No mention of electric blankets. Method given: wattage x hours x rate / 1,000. https://www.consumerreports.org/appliances/space-heaters/will-using-a-space-heater-save-you-money-a2378783171/ (read today).
- Consumer Reports, "How to Use Your Space Heater Safely" (updated 2025-10-05): "Turn the heater off when you leave the room or go to bed." "A smart sensor that automatically shuts off a heater when it overheats is a must." Never in a child's room. "Nearly half of all home heating fires happen during the months of December, January, and February." https://www.consumerreports.org/appliances/space-heaters/space-heater-safety-tips-a1096367334/ (read today). Snippet also says keep 3 ft from bedding, plug straight into the wall (not read on page).
- NC State Extension, Camden County, "Space Heater Safety," Kristi Willard, 2026-01-26: attributes to NFPA/CPSC that space heaters and heating stoves cause nearly half of home heating fires, about 1,600 fires, 70 deaths, 160 injuries a year; 3 ft clearance; wall outlet, no extension cord; tip-over and auto shut-off; off when sleeping. Does not mention electric blankets. https://camden.ces.ncsu.edu/2026/01/space-heater-safety (read today).
- NFPA (via fire-department/state pages, SNIPPET): 3 ft from anything that can burn, including bedding; wall outlet; "Turn off your space heater and unplug it before leaving the room or going to bed"; 2018 to 2022 heating equipment about 46% of reported home fires (one fire department citing NFPA). NFPA tip-sheet page https://www.nfpa.org/downloadable-resources/safety-tip-sheets/heating-safety-tip-sheet returned only the title; no NFPA electric-blanket text found.
- Electric-blanket safety checklist (Texas electric co-op, not NFPA/CPSC): check cord for frays, do not tuck under mattress or pile covers on top, keep pets off, keep flat (https://texascooppower.com/space-heater-safety/, SNIPPET). Old CPSC pages (2001 to 2008 archives) say discard blankets with dark/charred spots. For a UK view only: Electrical Safety First says consider replacing blankets at 10 years and reports 31 accidental electric blanket fires in England in 2024/25 (https://www.electricalsafetyfirst.org.uk/media-centre/press-releases/2026/03/concerns-as-electric-blanket-fires-hit-five-year-high). Do not present UK data as US.
- Energy.gov: no current page on electric blankets or heated pads was found. An archived DOE EnergySavers page (2009 capture) says zone heating can save more than 20% vs heating the whole house (https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20090506055429/http://www.energysavers.gov/your_home/space_heating_cooling/index.cfm/mytopic=12520, SNIPPET). DOE Indian Energy winter guide: layer clothes and blankets so you can lower the thermostat (https://www.energy.gov/indianenergy/articles/six-simple-steps-save-energy-and-money-winter, SNIPPET, ordinary blankets). energy.gov/energysaver/portable-heaters returned 404.
- Wirecutter / Good Housekeeping: not reachable (blocked). No claims taken. Good Housekeeping wattage numbers: none found.
- Electricity price: EIA-based figures through third parties: about 18.83 cents/kWh residential in April 2026 (electricchoice.com), 17.65 in June 2026, EIA STEO forecast 18.2 for 2026 (SNIPPETS; EIA tables not opened). Verify at eia.gov Electric Power Monthly Table 5.6.A before publishing a single number. Post is "at your electricity price", so give a formula plus a table at 12, 18 and 30 cents.
- Cost per 8-hour night (my arithmetic, full-wattage ceilings; thermostats cycle so real use is lower for blankets/pads and for any heater that reaches set temperature):
  - 1,500 W: 12.0 kWh = $2.16 at 18 c ($1.44 at 12 c, $3.60 at 30 c).
  - Oil-filled on high 1,500 W: same ceiling. Measured 1,400 W on high by an owner = 11.2 kWh = $2.02.
  - Bedsure throw 100 W (listing): 0.8 kWh = 14.4 c. (But auto-off is 3 h on most listings; an 8 h night would need re-starting - flag.)
  - Queen blanket: unknown; at an assumed 100 W = 14.4 c, at 200 W = 28.8 c. Label assumption.
  - Queen pad, both sides on high: 160 W (80 W/side) = 1.28 kWh = 23 c; 260 W (130 W/side) = 2.08 kWh = 37 c. Forum measurement of 30 to 65 W per side would be about 5 to 20 c.
- Secondary claims seen, not authoritative: blanket 50 to 150 W vs heater 750 to 1,500 W; about $1.80 vs under $0.20 per night at 15 c; NOVEC says 1,500 W heater about $0.15/hour (that figure is a utility's, equals 10 c/kWh).
- Oil-filled vs ceramic: same wattage = same cost while on; oil heaters cycle less and hold heat, so can use less over a night (search summary of commercial sources; no measured test found).
