## 4. Running cost per night

EIA Electric Power Monthly Table 5.6.A (https://www.eia.gov/electricity/monthly/epm_table_grapher.php?t=epmt_5_6_a): US average residential price 18.16 cents/kWh for August 2026 (preliminary), vs 17.57 in August 2025. This is the latest month in the table as of Nov 4, 2026 (the fetch summary did not state a release date; confirm the table still shows August).

Math at 18.16 c/kWh, 8 h per night (blanket draws full wattage only while heating; thermostat cycling cuts real use, so these are ceilings):
- 100 W (Bedsure throw per listings): 0.8 kWh = 14.5 cents/night; 1.8 cents/hour.
- 60 W: 0.48 kWh = 8.7 cents. 200 W: 1.6 kWh = 29 cents.
- 1,500 W space heater: 12 kWh over 8 h = $2.18/night; 27 cents/hour at full on.
- Typical range per sources: 60-200 W blankets, about 1-3 cents/hour (Homes & Gardens; others, not authoritative); a 1,500 W heater runs 750-1,500 W (one source's $2.55/hour claim is wrong).
- Thermostat: DOE says setting back 7-10 F for 8 hours a day can save as much as 10% a year on heating; about 1% per degree F (energy.gov Home Upgrades https://www.energy.gov/save/home-upgrades). Savings depend on the home; heat pump caveat applies. No home-wide dollar figure given here; do not invent one.
- A throw only warms one person; do not claim it replaces whole-home heat.
