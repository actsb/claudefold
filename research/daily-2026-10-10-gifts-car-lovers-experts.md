  - Jump starter: compact packs under $50 that can also charge phones.
  - Tire gauge: a digital Accutire is the top pick; the Intercomp dial gauge appeals to enthusiasts.

  - Timed 0-35 psi and 30-35 psi. The fastest full inflation took about 4 minutes.
  - CR's pick was the DeWalt, for performance, quiet operation and hose storage.
  - CR says they suit topping off and roadside kits, not heavy work.
  - 2017 test: Ryobi was fastest; the Ideaworks was least accurate with a hard-to-read dial. Continuous use on a fully flat tire can overheat handheld units.

  - Accuracy was tested against a calibrated master gauge at cold, room and hot temperatures.

  - The test data is about six years old.

### Safety / how-to authorities
- **NHTSA tire page.** https://www.nhtsa.gov/vehicle-safety/tires
  - Check pressure at least monthly, with tires cold (driven less than about 3 hours earlier), and check the spare.
  - Use the vehicle maker's pressure from the Tire and Loading Information label on the driver's door jamb (B-pillar), not the number on the tire. The label may be on the rear edge of the driver's door if there is no B-pillar. The owner's manual also lists it.
  - Warm tires can read about 4-6 psi higher. TPMS is not a substitute for regular checks.
  - Related: 2002 Federal Register tire-label rule, https://corpora.tika.apache.org/base/docs/govdocs1/480/480205.html (placard location rule).
- **AAA how to check tire pressure.** https://www.aaa.com/autorepair/articles/how-to-check-tire-pressure-everything-you-need-to-know
  - Look at the door-jamb placard. The sidewall number is the tire's maximum, not the recommended fill. On older cars the decal may be in the glove box or fuel door.
  - Check cold; warm tires can read up to about 5 psi high. Press the gauge firmly on the valve stem with no hiss.
  - Inflate in short bursts and recheck. Release over-inflation with the valve pin.
  - Check monthly and before long trips.
  - See also https://www.aaa.com/autorepair/articles/what-is-the-ideal-car-tire-pressure-and-how-to-maintain-it
- **AAA TPMS study, Oct 2023.** https://newsroom.aaa.com/2023/10/dont-let-your-tires-pressure-monitoring-system-leave-you-feeling-flat/ (report PDF: https://newsroom.aaa.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Report-Tire-Pressure-Monitoring-Systems-October-2023.pdf)
  - 11 vehicles tested. Displayed readings were accurate (max error 3.3%).
  - The warning light came on at about 70-75% of posted pressure in most vehicles, so a tire can be 20%+ low before the light.
  - AAA advises a manual gauge monthly.
  - A 65%-before-warning single-vehicle result was seen only in a secondary write-up; omit it.
- **Edmunds.** https://www.edmunds.com/how-to/how-to-check-tire-pressure-and-inflate-tires.html and https://www.edmunds.com/how-to/how-to-create-your-own-roadside-emergency-kit.html
  - Check monthly. The TPMS light only warns at about 25% below spec. Keep a roughly $10 digital gauge in the glovebox. The pressure sticker is on the driver's door jamb or in the manual.
  - Roadside kit: jumper cables or lithium battery pack, flares or triangles, first aid, blanket, flashlight, tools, inflator with sealant, tire gauge, water and snacks.
  - These pages are old; use for general support only.
- **Tire Rack tech.** https://www.tirerack.com/tires/tiretech/techpage.jsp?techid=8
  - The right pressure is the vehicle maker's figure, found on the placard and in the manual. It is almost always below the tire's sidewall maximum. Placards have been standardized since 2003 and list the spare.
  - Tiresafety.com (Bridgestone/Firestone) is quoted elsewhere saying the same (secondary).
- **State DOTs (winter).**
  - PennDOT: https://www.penndot.pa.gov/TravelInPA/Safety/TrafficSafetyAndDriverTopics/Pages/Winter-Driving.aspx - clear windows, mirrors, lights, hood and roof; a driver can be ticketed if falling snow or ice causes injury; stay with the vehicle if stranded.
  - WSDOT: https://www.wsdot.wa.gov/publications/fulltext/winter/winter-driving-guide.pdf - clear snow and ice from all windows and the roof; kit includes first aid, phone charger, water, warm clothes.
  - Michigan: https://www.michigan.gov/msp/divisions/ohsp/safety-programs/winter-driving/be-prepared - kit has blanket, flashlight, jumper cables, tow rope, shovel, sand or cat litter, radio, map, snacks. Michigan State Police: a fine is possible if snow or ice from your vehicle hits other drivers' windshields. Keep the exhaust pipe clear, and run the engine only briefly each hour with a window open.
- **AAA winter tips (regional AAA clubs).** https://www.mwg.aaa.com/via/car/must-have-winter-items
  - Keep an ice scraper and snow brush, shovel, traction aid, jumper cables and warm clothing.
  - Never pour hot water on a frozen windshield (it can crack); use the defroster. Avoid metal scrapers.
  - Wording comes from several AAA-affiliated pages; check the exact page before quoting.
- **Cal OES vehicle kit.** https://www.news.caloes.ca.gov/building-an-emergency-kit-for-your-vehicle/ - store items in airtight bags in one or two containers (secondary mention).

## 3. Manuals and official instructions
- **Tire placard (official).**
  - NHTSA: https://www.nhtsa.gov/vehicle-safety/tires. Look for the Tire and Loading Information label on the driver's door jamb, with the owner's manual as the backup. Inflate cold to that number, not the sidewall maximum.
  - AAA: https://www.aaa.com/autorepair/articles/how-to-check-tire-pressure-everything-you-need-to-know
  - Tire Rack: https://www.tirerack.com/tires/tiretech/techpage.jsp?techid=8
- **DeWalt DCC020IB (the CR-recommended inflator).** Official product page: https://www.dewalt.com/product/dcc020ib/20v-max-corded-cordless-air-inflator. It lists an instruction-manual PDF (NA738844_DCC020i_NA.pdf, Type 3). The PDF itself was not opened.
  - From the product page: runs on a 20V battery, 12V DC or 110V AC (batteries and chargers are sold separately); digital preset pressure with auto-shutoff; a deflation port.
  - The 10-minutes-on / 20-minutes-off duty cycle is unverified (it comes from a video snippet). Check the manual.
- **Milwaukee M12 inflator 2475-20.** The manual is hosted at https://www.manualslib.fr/manual/179821/Milwaukee-2475-20.html and https://www.notice-facile.com/en/manual/578448/milwaukee+m12-247521xc. It was not opened; the snippet shows only the standard "read the operator's manual" warning.
- **Avid Power.** No official manual was found. Listings say you preset the pressure and it auto-stops, with stated accuracy of about ±2 psi. Models vary, so confirm the model number.
- **NOCO GB40 user guide.** PDF copies: https://attachments.priuschat.com/attachment-files/2021/02/201443_noco_genius_boost_gb40_user_guide_english.pdf. Official support: www.no.co/support.
  - Manual override is for batteries below about 2V. Hold the override button for 3 seconds; it disables the protections, so the clamps must not touch and polarity must be correct. Press the button again or the power button to exit.

## 4. Gift-guide angles the evidence supports
- A digital tire gauge (CR and Edmunds) pairs with an inflator. AAA's TPMS study shows why a manual gauge still matters.
- Cordless inflator: CR picks the DeWalt (needs a battery). C&D picks the Avid Power (loud, slower). Outdoor Life picks the Milwaukee M18 (large, costly). Say which battery platform each needs.
- Jump starter: the NOCO GB40 is The Drive's and AutoGuide's top pick. Note the override caveats and the cold-weather limits in CR's test.
- Winter kit: CR and the state DOTs agree on brush/scraper, shovel, traction material, blanket, light, and jump starter or cables. Clear the whole roof. Never use hot water on glass.
- Gaps: no expert testing found for trunk organizers, seat-gap organizers, LED work lights, or ice scraper practices beyond AAA's advice. Describe these as "useful picks" without claiming test results.
