## 1. Jump starter vs jumper cables

Sources and dates
* Tahoe Yukon Forum, "Best jump box?" thread, Jan 25 to Feb 17, 2020 (https://www.tahoeyukonforum.com/threads/best-jump-box.116722/). Posters report a cheap box that could not turn over an engine from a dead battery; a poster whose smaller units stranded him and who now trusts a Clore JNC660 (said to have jumped 30+ vehicles on one charge); NOCO GB150 started a 6.7L diesel F-350 at 10 F per a friend; a GB40 kept in a Tahoe console "worked well so far". Posters also doubted inflated "4,000 amp" labels.
* Garage Journal, "Car Jump starter recommendation, avoid NOCO please", Sep 26-27, 2023 (https://www.garagejournal.com/forum/threads/car-jump-starter-recommendation-avoid-noco-please.522260/). Complaints: NOCO units losing capacity or not turning on (GBX55 named), a GB40 that swelled after a jump and was refused repair out of warranty, sensitivity to heat in a parked car, deep-discharge death. Defenders: NOCO called one of the better small lithium brands, but all small lithium units eventually fail. Most recommended alternative: Clore Jump-N-Carry JNC660 (lead-acid); JNC770 reported fine after 3 and 5+ years; Topdon JS3000 reported 25-30 uses, started a 6.0 Powerstroke; HULKMAN Alpha 85 reported good. Disputed: Clore charging port wear and $50 vs $200 replacement batteries.
* Garage Journal, cold-season jump pack thread (https://www.garagejournal.com/forum/threads/portable-jump-starter-going-into-the-cold-season.433159/) and NOCO thread (https://www.garagejournal.com/forum/threads/noco-battery-jumpers.434940/), read via search summaries, no dates captured. Reports: a pack cold-soaked a week in single-digit F started three vehicles; a GB40 started a 2014 Silverado 5.3L at 20 F; one owner guesses lithium NOCOs stop working near -10 C; a GB150 on a diesel failed once in cold and needed two packs; several note diesels may fail from gelled fuel, which no pack fixes.
* Walmart, NOCO Boost GB40 listing (https://www.walmart.com/ip/NOCO-Boost-Plus-GB40-1000A-12V-UltraSafe-Portable-Lithium-Jump-Starter/46605770), snippet snapshot, date not captured: strongly positive; one owner says it started a hybrid SUV three times below 10 F; one 1-star says the unit arrived only partly charged and failed on a truck (jumped with cables instead); another reports NOCO told them the smaller GB20 is for gas engines up to 4.0L and the GB40 up to 6.0L. Charge it fully on arrival.
* Costco, Michelin High-Capacity jump starter and power bank (https://www.costco.com/michelin-high-capacity-portable-jump-starter-and-power-bank.product.100763903.html): a search summary reports strong member ratings and at least one cold-weather problem at -20 F, and a connection warning on a fully dead battery. Snapshot undated; 500 peak amps, 10,000 mAh per the listing.
* The Drive, "Best Jump Starters", updated Feb 11, 2025 (https://www.thedrive.com/reviews/27487/best-jump-starters): best overall NOCO Boost Plus GB40 (writer owns one), rated to 6.0L gas / 3.0L diesel; notes portable units lose charge in the same cold that drains car batteries, so check them periodically; advises buying more power than you need.
* Jumper-cable sizing: only retailer and blog guides found (no forum consensus). They lean toward 4 gauge and 16-20 ft for cold and big engines; 8 gauge is borderline in cold. Treat as weak.

What this means
* GB40 is the most-named small unit and is fine for cars, small SUVs and V6s; owners of V8 trucks and diesels split between GB70/GB150 and lead-acid Clore JNC660 (heavy, around a few dozen pounds is typical for lead-acid; verify weight on maker page before stating).
* Failure modes: arrived uncharged, never topped up, swelled battery from heat, dead battery too flat for the unit to detect, cold-soaked lithium.
* Practical rule: keep the pack in the cabin (heat and cold both hurt), top it up every month or two, match it to engine size.

## 2. Folding shovel

* Home Depot, Suncast Telescoping Auto Shovel SCS4506PK (https://www.homedepot.com/p/Suncast-Telescoping-Auto-Shovel-SCS4506PK/302369673): search snapshot says 4.4 from 196 reviews; one reviewer says the blade flew off within seconds because a single screw holds the thin head; another says it works about as well as a metal shovel. Target listing (https://www.target.com/s/sled+shovel) snapshot said 4.6 from 92 ratings. Dates not captured.
* Home Depot, Michelin Ultra Compact 40 in. Folding Utility Snow Shovel 19188-2 (https://www.homedepot.com/p/Michelin-Ultra-Compact-40-in-Folding-Utility-Snow-Shovel-for-Car-Emergency-Telescoping-Handle-19188-2/318607007): snapshot 3.9 from 27; split, one reviewer says plastic snapped in hard icy snow, another says it kept up.
* Lowe's, BirdRock Home 34 in folding aluminum shovel (https://www.lowes.com/pd/BirdRock-Home-34-8221-Folding-Emergency-Snow-Shovel-8211-Rugged-Compact-Tool-for-Car-Snowmobiles-or-ATV-8211-Compact-Winter-Survival-Gear-for-Camping-Mud-Colla-PSIble-Multifunctional-Red/5001505621): 2 reviews only, one reports the plastic locking sleeve broke, no replacement parts. Too thin to cite as a pattern.
* Walmart folding-shovel review pages (examples: https://www.walmart.com/reviews/product/21587866 Coghlan's, https://www.walmart.com/reviews/product/51094998 Ozark Trail Heavy Duty Steel Folding Shovel), via search summaries: recurring complaint is the lock (twist knob loosens, awkward lock release); sample-to-sample variation noted; some reviews are marked incentivized.
* Cold advice: a buying guide warns all-ABS sliding sections can seize or shatter in extreme cold (https://thewearify.com/best-collapsible-snow-shovel-for-car/; low-authority site).
* Forum: Rick's Free Auto Repair Advice says minimum gear is a full-size shovel rather than a flimsy folder (https://ricksfreeautorepairadvice.com/what-to-pack-in-your-car-emergency-kit/); Consumer Reports says good kits include a collapsible shovel, useful for digging out and clearing around the tailpipe (https://www.consumerreports.org/emergency-preparedness/winter-car-emergency-kit-be-prepared/; paywalled, read via summary).
* Not found: owner threads on Garant, Voile, Gerber, Estwing folding shovels for car use. Do not claim anything about them.

Pattern: the failure is almost always a plastic joint, lock sleeve, or single-screw head, not the blade. Tell readers to wiggle-test the lock in the store and favor a metal blade and metal lock.

Two anecdotes; use cautiously.
* Size that matters: long reach (about 26 in plus; 48 in or more for SUVs/trucks) vs trunk space; non-telescoping is more reliable.

## 4. Traction mats

* Overland Site, MAXSA vs MaxTrax (https://www.overlandsite.com/recovery-gear/maxsa-vs-maxtrax/): pros price and ease of use, non-directional; cons 90-day warranty and the polypropylene may flex more; not designed as a bridge; some off-roaders doubt durability.
* The Drive, best snow traction mats, updated May 31, 2023 (https://www.thedrive.com/reviews/27070/best-snow-traction-mats): they did not test in snow; MAXTRAX MKII best overall; Maxsa Escaper Buddy honorable mention. Use only for what it is: market research.
* Costco, MAXSA Escaper Buddy 2-pack (https://www.costco.com/.product.100540718.html): snippets showed about 4.5-4.6 from 36-44 reviews, undated; I could not read the written reviews.
* Home Depot, MAXSA Escaper Buddy 20333 (https://www.homedepot.com/p/MAXSA-Escaper-Buddy-Traction-Mats-Set-of-2-20333/309649258): only about 2 reviews, both positive; too few to quote. Home Depot MAXSA Foldable Traction Mats 20025 (https://www.homedepot.com/p/MAXSA-Foldable-Traction-Mats-20025/309649167): four reviews, one says the mat shot out from under the tire. Retailer prices reported by the snippets for the 2-pack ranged from about $120 to $280 across stores; check live before using a band.
* Expedition Portal, MAXTRAX Extremes fail in the snow (https://expeditionportal.com/forum/threads/maxtrax-extremes-fail-in-the-snow.223659/): owner says boards got sucked under the front tires and buried, almost no grip on snow, though they did not crack. Related thread (https://www.expeditionportal.com/forum/threads/soooo-i-got-stuck-any-recovery-advice.155668/): posters bring MAXSA pads and shovels; one says shovels and knock-off Maxtrax did the job. No MAXSA verdict was posted.
* Failure modes reported at other sellers (via search summaries): mats cracking or snapping under heavy trucks (F350 on cheap tracks), mats flying out 5 ft in powder, need something solid beneath. Best on a dug-out, compacted surface.
* Minnesota State Patrol "Ask a Trooper" column, Sgt. Jesse Grabow, Feb 9, 2026 (https://www.echopress.com/news/minnesota/ask-a-trooper-alternative-winter-traction-aids) and Feb 16, 2026 (https://pctribune.com/opinions/ask-a-trooper-traction-advice/): lists sand, tire chains, snow socks, and traction mats or boards; says dig out first and put the aid in the path of the drive wheels; floor mats work in a pinch. (A reader, not the trooper, called cat litter a slippery mess.)

Pattern: mats are a small-vehicle, plowed-lot or driveway fix. For a heavy truck or deep powder, owners trust rigid boards (MaxTrax-class) or chains.

## 5. Blanket, mylar

* Blade Forums (https://www.bladeforums.com/threads/wool-or-survival-blanket.1017692): via search summary, one poster carries both (space blanket as ground cloth or shelter, wool for warmth); another favors a heavy wool bedroll in the car. Date not captured.
* Field & Stream emergency-blanket guide (https://fieldandstream.com/outdoor-gear/survival/survival-gear/best-emergency-blankets): wool or fleece suits blankets you will not carry far; mylar's strengths are weight and price. Outdoor Life: wool keeps warming when wet (https://www.outdoorlife.com/gear/best-emergency-blankets.md).
* REI, SOL Escape Lite Bivy (https://www.rei.com/product/891011/sol-escape-lite-bivy): snapshot 4.2-4.3 from 18-25 reviews; enthusiasts like it, critics say it is not truly water resistant. REI, SOL Emergency Bivvy with whistle and tinder cord (https://www.rei.com/product/199053/sol-emergency-bivvy-with-rescue-whistle-and-tinder-cord): snapshot 4.7 from 60; top critical review is condensation. Walmart SOL Escape Bivvy with Hood (https://www.walmart.com/ip/5378381016): about 4.5 from 18. Trailspace reviewer says the bivvy can feel like a heat sink and sleeping-bag insulation compressed in it (https://trailspace.com/gear/sol/escape-bivvy).
* Graywolf Survival says mylar will not keep you warm like a real blanket and tears easily (https://graywolfsurvival.com/?p=218008; low-authority).

Verdict: a wool or fleece blanket (or an old sleeping bag) is the real warmth; a mylar sheet or SOL bivvy is a small add-on for wind and ground cold.

## 6. Headlamp / flashlight

* REI, Black Diamond Astro 300 (https://www.rei.com/product/202772/black-diamond-astro-300-headlamp): snapshots show 4.5-4.6 from 225-307 reviews; Backcountry lists mostly 5-star reviews from about 37 (https://origin-c1.backcountry.com/black-diamond-astro-300-headlamp). Runs on AAA; the Astro 300-R is the rechargeable version and has a few durability and service complaints per Backcountry.
* REI, Petzl Tikkina (https://www.rei.com/product/203909): split, 3.4-3.8 across a few snapshots; complaints about build quality and a flimsy battery door, praise for value. CleverHiker names it best budget headlamp (https://www.cleverhiker.com/backpacking/petzl-tikkina-headlamp-review).
* Cold batteries: ToolGuyd explains alkaline cells fall off sharply in cold and Energizer Ultimate Lithium is rated to -40 F, effective to about -4 F by its chart (https://toolguyd.com/flashlight-tool-batteries-cold-weather/). Candlepower Forums has extended cold-flashlight discussion (https://www.candlepowerforums.com/threads/extreme-cold-effects-on-flashlights.216561/).
* A headlamp beats a hand flashlight when changing a tire or digging; keep it in the cabin.

## 9. Hand warmers

* Walmart HotHands listings (https://www.walmart.com/ip/HotHands-Large-Body-Hand-Super-Warmers-10-Pack/33486551 and https://www.walmart.com/ip/746434073): reviewers keep them in cars and report they last hours; the larger body/hand "Super" warmers are listed to 18 hours and one reviewer calls that about right; another says at least 12 hours. ## 10. Kitty litter / sand

* Reviewed ice-rink test, updated Feb 12, 2024 (https://reviewed.com/home-outdoors/features/sand-salt-and-ice-melt-prevent-ice-falls): puck slid 52 in untreated, 26 in on kitty litter, 10 in on sand, 19 in on Blue Heat ice melt. Lab-style, not a car test.
* Clay litter turns to a slick paste when damp, per a CBC piece (https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.3921988) and Mankato Free Press coverage; coarse sand (not fine beach sand) is the norm.
* A bag is heavy and messy; mats do the same job and reuse.

Verdict: skip litter as a plan; if you want a granular aid, coarse sand in a small bag. NWS and Alaska lists still say "sand or cat litter" (https://www.weather.gov/media/top/prepare/CarWinterSurvivalKitChecklist.pdf).

## 11. Candle-in-a-can

* Minnesota Department of Public Safety lists a 3-lb coffee can with candle stubs and matches, per CBS Minnesota (https://cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/good-question-what-do-you-need-in-your-car-to-survive-winter-storms). Official, so do not call it a myth; call it dated and low-yield.
* Carbon monoxide: Minnesota materials cover engine idling; a repair blog says crack a window about 1 in with a can-heater (https://ricksfreeautorepairadvice.com/what-to-pack-in-your-car-emergency-kit/). No official candle-heater test found; no tea-light heat test found. We cannot back "candles do not work" with data. The defensible claim: open flame in a closed car adds fire and oxygen risks, and a blanket plus hand warmers are safer. State that as our judgment.
* Engine idling rules (about 10 min per hour, clear the tailpipe, crack a window): Jalopnik citing NWS (https://jalopnik.com/1848338972); Minnesota DPS blog (https://dps.mn.gov/blog/Pages/20221110-winter-driving.aspx).

## Picks the evidence supports (6 to 8)

1. NOCO Boost Plus GB40 jump starter. Most-named in forums, The Drive best overall (Feb 2025), Walmart owner reports of sub-10 F starts. Cons: partly charged out of box, a swelling and lifespan complaint thread (GJ Sep 2023), engines above 6.0L gas / 3.0L diesel. Skip if you drive a big diesel.
2. Clore Jump-N-Carry JNC660 (lead-acid). Pick for V8 and diesel owners and deep cold; the forum favorite (2020 and 2023 threads). Cons: heavy, bulky, charging port wear claims, costly battery replacements. Grade B (forum only; confirm weight/price on Clore or a retailer).
3. MAXSA Escaper Buddy traction mats (2-pack, 20333). Top value mat; The Drive honorable mention (2023), Overland Site cons, Costco rating snapshot. Cons: flex, 90-day warranty, thin Home Depot reviews, mats can shoot out in powder; not for heavy trucks. Grade B.
4. Hopkins SubZero 26 in snow brush and ice scraper. Family Handyman top pick (Nov 2024); Lowe's 26 in Hopkins snapshot 4.6 from 130. Cons: bristle shedding, foam grip spinning on larger brooms. Grade B.
5. Suncast Telescoping Auto Shovel (SCS4506PK). Best-reviewed car shovel with real volume at Home Depot (snapshot 4.4 from 196) and Target. Cons: single screw holding the head, plastic. Grade B-; say "check the lock and screw" and pair it with a note that cheap folding shovels fail at joints. Alternative to mention: a metal-blade entrenching style (Ozark Trail steel folding shovel at Walmart) for people who don't mind a smaller, heavier tool.
6. Black Diamond Astro 300 headlamp (with Energizer Ultimate Lithium AAAs). REI snapshot 4.5-4.6 from 225-307; ToolGuyd on lithium in cold. Cons: AAA cells not included in the -R model, 300 lumens only. Budget alternative: Petzl Tikkina (mixed REI feedback).
7. SOL Escape Lite Bivy (or SOL Emergency Bivvy) plus a wool blanket. REI snapshot 4.2-4.7; critics cite condensation and water resistance. Grade B. Do not sell mylar alone.
8. HotHands Super Warmers (body and hand, 18 hr). Walmart owner reports; cheap; slow to start; 4-year shelf life. Grade B.

Left without a named pick: first-aid kit (C), jumper cables (guides only), power bank (pack covers it).

## Overkill, or skip

* Candle-in-a-can (see 11). Optional at best.
* Kitty litter (sand or mats instead).
* MaxTrax-class recovery boards for a sedan or crossover that stays on roads; they are for deep-powder and off-road recovery.
* Buying both a big jump starter and a heavy 4-gauge cable set: pick one, carry cables only if you drive a diesel or big V8 or want a backup when the pack is flat.
* Folding shovel and mats if you never leave a plowed city with a garage. A scraper, a charged phone and a small jump pack cover most calls.
* Mylar as the only blanket.
* Full-size axe/crowbar/tow chain from Alaska trooper-style lists unless you drive remote roads.

## Questions owners keep asking (from the forum threads above)

* Which jump starter for a V8 or diesel? (match to engine size; GB40 tops out at 6.0L gas / 3.0L diesel.)
* Does a lithium jump pack work in the cold, and where should I store it? (cabin, topped up.)
* Will traction mats work in deep snow? (best when dug out and compacted; powder can launch them.)
* Does cat litter work? (sand beats it; clay turns to mush when wet.)
* Should the kit go in the trunk or the cabin? (a trunk can freeze shut or be inaccessible after a crash; source: Guide Auto guidance and other winter kit pages, e.g. https://mobile.guideautoweb.com/en/articles/64504/does-your-emergency-car-kit-include-what-you-need-for-winter.)
