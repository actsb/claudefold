### 2. Health and medical evidence

* **Cleveland Clinic, "Power Wash: Why Using a Bidet Is Sanitary and Safe"** (gastroenterologist Christine Lee, MD) [S]: more sanitary than toilet paper; water beats "a few squares of dry TP" at removing trace fecal matter; gentler on skin (wiping can chafe and cause tiny cuts); can be more comfortable with hemorrhoids or fissures. Cautions: spray front to back, right pressure and angle so water isn't forced into the vagina, to avoid vaginal or urinary infections.
* **Mayo Clinic Press, "Hemorrhoid Care Expert"** (Sarah Umar, MD, gastroenterologist) [S, paraphrase]: bidets help people with symptoms after a bowel movement and cut down on wiping; "a Mayo Clinic expert" says bidets won't harm or aggravate hemorrhoids (confirm the speaker before attributing it to Dr. Umar).
* **Harvard Health** hemorrhoid pages [S]: repeated wiping worsens itching; sitz baths and patting dry; **no Harvard statement on bidets found**.
* **Baig et al., "Be Kind to Your Behind" systematic review** (Evid-Based Complement Altern Med, May 2022; doi:10.1155/2022/1633965) [S]: habitual use had no impact on the odds of hemorrhoids; habitual use **may increase** the odds of anal itching; 2 case series of perianal burns or anterior anal fissures; 1 RCT: bidets non-inferior to sitz baths for post-hemorrhoidectomy pain.
* **Asakura, Nakano & Omae** (Epidemiol Infect, 2018; 10,305 Japanese respondents, 2013–2016) [S]: habitual use did not significantly raise hemorrhoids or urogenital infections; male habitual users more often reported new irritated skin around the anus (adjusted RR 1.36).
* **Ryoo et al.** (J Korean Med Sci 2011) [S]: low or medium pressure, warm water and a wide stream lowered anal resting pressure, much like a warm sitz bath.
* **BIDLOW randomized trial** (BMC Surgery; hemorrhoidectomy; electronic bidet at 38 °C and very low force vs warm sitz baths, 7 days) [S]: day-7 pain 38.3 vs 42.0 (no difference), but non-inferiority not proven.
* **AJOT 75(5), automated bidet intervention** [S]: feasible and reduced the physical help needed with toilet hygiene (caregiver burden).
* **Tsunoda A** (J Anus Rectum Colon, Oct 2021) [S]: excessive use may cause anal itching and anal incontinence; immunocompromised hospital patients should be careful with shared bidet toilets.
* **Ogino et al.** (J Obstet Gynaecol Res 2010) [S]: among 268 women with increased discharge, Lactobacillus absent in 42.86% of habitual users vs 8.77% of non-users (observational, symptomatic women). **Obstet Gynecol, June 2013** (2,545 Tokyo births) [S]: preterm birth 15.8% vs 16.0% (adjusted OR 1.04); normal use poses no clinical risk for preterm birth or bacterial vaginosis.
* **Nozzle hygiene** [S]: Tokyo Medical University Hospital hematology ward (2020–2021): nozzles may harbor multidrug-resistant Pseudomonas aeruginosa (hospital setting); warm-water tanks lose residual chlorine, raising bacterial counts. Consumer cleaning frequency UNVERIFIED — point to the manufacturer's instructions.
* UNVERIFIED (vendor/lay sites only, do not publish): bidets reduce UTIs in Japanese nursing homes.

### 3. DIY installation facts

* Steps (non-electric attachment) [S, vendor/retailer how-tos]: close the valve behind the toilet and flush; disconnect the supply hose; remove the seat, set the attachment over the bolt holes, refit the seat bolts ("firmly but do not overtighten"); fit the T-adapter on the fill valve, the supply hose on the T and the bidet hose on the branch; open the valve slowly and check every joint for leaks.
* Time [S]: "about 15 minutes with one wrench" (vendor how-to); TUSHY claims 8.5 minutes (via Bob Vila); GH testers: TUSHY met its "10 minutes or less" promise.
* Warm-water kits screw a female 3/8" T-adapter onto the sink's hot-water valve [S]; the extra hose is usually 9–10 ft; usually one small hole through the vanity. 7/8" toilet fill-valve thread: UNVERIFIED this session.
* Common mistakes [S]: overtightening damages fittings; not checking for leaks; cheap brands that leak (Mahoney). Teflon tape: follow the manual.
* Compatibility [S, fitment guides]: hinge bolt spacing normally about 5.5 in (155 mm); one guide says under 5.5 in or over 7.5 in won't fit; allow at least 1.5 in between the bolt holes and the tank; one-piece toilets are OK if the tank meets the bowl at roughly 90°; skirted toilets usually work but may need extra parts; hidden hinges UNVERIFIED.
* Electric seats [S]: NEC 210.8(A) requires GFCI protection for bathroom receptacles (local adoption varies); no extension cords or power strips (vendor guidance); seats draw about 500–1,400 W; cords often about 4 ft, outlet within about 3–4 ft of the toilet centerline. **Electrician costs** [S]: Angi (2026 data) GFCI outlet installed $130–$300, average about $210; HomeGuide/HomeAdvisor new GFCI install $150–$350; a Fort Worth electrician $350–$600 for a new 20-amp circuit with GFCI outlet incl. permit.
* Renters [S]: non-electric attachments need no permanent changes; warm-water models may need a small hole in the vanity (check the lease).
* Backflow [S]: ASME A112.4.2/CSA B45.16 "Personal hygiene devices for water closets" covers bidet seats, sprayers and retrofit devices; look for that certification.

#### 5. Fifteen questions buyers ask before buying
Best-supported answers; E-numbers point to the evidence above.

1. **Will it fit my toilet?**
   - Buyers ask this directly: "Will this device work on an elongated toilet seat" ([Best Buy Q](https://www.bestbuy.com/site/questions/luxe-neo-120-non-electric-self-cleaning-nozzle-universal-fit-bidet-toilet-attachment-blue/6408499/question/4ec6e165-2634-393a-9974-586743f932eb)).
   - Attachments like the NEO 120 are sold as "universal fit". Owners still report a front gap from thicker attachments (NestAlpha), T-adapter mismatches and short hoses (E52), bulky seats that raise height and shrink the opening (E53), and a small round C2 seat (E54).
   - One-piece, skirted and hidden-hinge toilets: **UNVERIFIED**.
2. **Can I install it myself, and how long does it take?**
   - Buyers ask "How easy to install" ([Best Buy Q](https://www.bestbuy.com/site/questions/luxe-neo-120-non-electric-self-cleaning-nozzle-universal-fit-bidet-toilet-attachment-blue/6408499/question/7a27ffe2-6b22-3649-9a0f-f172204c60e2)).
   - Owners report 10–30 minutes (E1) or under 30 (E3). Warm models take longer and may need drilling (E7).
   - Watch for cross-threading (E39) and a stubborn old supply line (E32).
3. **Will it leak?**
   - Leaks are the most common complaint (E30–E39), but many owners report none after a year or more (E31).
   - Fixes owners used: plumber's tape (E30) and new supply lines (E39).
   - Slow drips can show up at 14 months to 2 years (E36, E38).
4. **How cold is the water?**
   - There's a shock at first; after a few weeks it feels "cool" (E66, E68), though some call it "icy" (E69). Some owners wish they'd bought a warm model (E67).
   - Winter temperatures: **UNVERIFIED**.
5. **Can I get warm water without electricity?**
   - Yes: the NEO 320 and TUSHY Spa tap the sink's hot line.
   - You need a sink within about 6 feet, and you often have to run the hot tap first (E75, E76).
6. **How long does the warm water last on an electric seat?**
   - Tank-heated seats: about 15 seconds on the SE600 (E70), 40–60 seconds on the C5 (E71), and "quickly" on the C2 (E72).
   - Instant-heater seats like the Swash Thinline run continuously (editorial).
   - Alpha JX: **UNVERIFIED**.
7. **Do I still need toilet paper?**
   - Owners report much less paper (E21, E22).
   - Dryers are slow or missing (E62–E65), so most owners still pat dry with a little paper or a towel.
   - Graying with Grace argues you shouldn't need to wipe at all [editorial] ([link](https://www.grayingwithgrace.com/wipe-after-using-bidet/)).
8. **Is it clean water, or tank or bowl water?**
   - Buyers ask whether it uses bidet water or flush water ([Best Buy Q](https://www.bestbuy.com/site/questions/luxe-neo-120-non-electric-self-cleaning-nozzle-universal-fit-bidet-toilet-attachment-blue/6408499/question/aae728a0-a168-355d-a381-009a8d824d9d)).
   - The LUXE units are sold as "Fresh Water" bidets that tee off the supply line ([Amazon listing](https://www.amazon.com/Luxe-Bidet-Neo-120-Non-Electric/dp/B00JG2DETM); T-adapter in E52).
9. **Are the nozzles hygienic?**
   - The NEO 120's nozzle self-cleans and retracts behind a guard gate (listing per snippet).
   - The Slim Two's stainless nozzle is easier to clean (Best Buy).
   - Hard water can leave a nozzle looking "funky" (E79), and a few C2 owners notice a smell (E80).
10. **Is there a front (feminine) wash, and is it safe for women?**
    - Buyers ask "what does this clean?" ([Best Buy Q](https://www.bestbuy.com/site/questions/luxe-neo-120-non-electric-self-cleaning-nozzle-universal-fit-bidet-toilet-attachment-white/6408504/question/7d5570a0-995b-3a11-a0d7-f4c6c96b14f6)).
    - The NEO 120 is rear-only. The NEO 185 adds a separate feminine nozzle with a softer spray aimed further forward ([Amazon 185](https://arcus-www.amazon.com/product-reviews/B00P2XZDGG/ref=pd_sim_cr_sccl_2_4/) / [Best Buy 185](https://www.bestbuy.com/site/reviews/luxe-neo-185-non-electric-self-cleaning-nozzle-universal-attachment-bidet-blue/6410339) cluster).
    - The TUSHY Classic has one nozzle with angle control (NestAlpha). The C2's nozzle doesn't reach far enough forward for some owners (E73).
    - UTI concerns: **UNVERIFIED**.
11. **How strong is the pressure?**
    - Non-electric attachments run on line pressure, and owners call them strong and adjustable (E13–E16).
    - Electric seats tend to be gentler (E58, E73, E74).
12. **Will it work for an elderly parent or with a raised toilet seat?**
    - Buyers ask "Will it work with an old people's raised toilet?" ([Best Buy Q](https://www.bestbuy.com/site/questions/luxe-neo-120-non-electric-self-cleaning-nozzle-universal-fit-bidet-toilet-attachment-white/6408499/question/7d35fa29-7444-31b3-9be7-058f7ff48ecc)). The answer wasn't captured (**UNVERIFIED**).
    - The E820 is valued for limited mobility (E28).
    - Editorial view: no cords to trip on, but the knobs may confuse someone with dementia.
13. **Does an electric seat need an outlet?**
    - Yes; for example, the E930 is listed as plug-in.
    - Distance and GFCI requirements: **UNVERIFIED**.
14. **I rent, or I'm replacing my toilet. Can I move it?**
    - One owner asked after replacing their toilet ([Best Buy Q](https://www.bestbuy.com/site/questions/luxe-neo-180-non-electric-self-cleaning-nozzle-universal-fit-bidet-toilet-attachment-white/6410338/question/ca908fa8-45a2-3c2f-97ce-e1a09e44c46b)). The answer wasn't captured.
    - Renters: **UNVERIFIED**.
15. **Will it raise my water bill? Will kids and guests manage?**
    - **UNVERIFIED** (not captured).

  - Choose an electric seat if there's an outlet. Expect tank seats to run out of warm water within seconds to a minute and dryers to be slow. Long-life reports exist for the BB-2000 and Swash 1000, alongside a scatter of 1–2 year failures across brands.
