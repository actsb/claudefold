## A. Studies and data on fake reference prices

### Consumers' Checkbook "Sale Fail" 2025 (the main US study; press release fetched and read in full)
- Method: starting February 2025, researchers checked prices once a week for 24 weeks at 25 national chains, 25-plus items per store. They picked on-sale items typical of each store (for example power tools at Home Depot, clothing and housewares at Kohl's, big-ticket electronics at Best Buy) — Consumers' Checkbook press release — https://www.checkbook.org/pdfDocs/doc/Sale_Fail_2025_press_release.pdf — Nov 13, 2025
- Headline number: 21 of 25 stores advertised sale prices more than half the time. In the 2018 project only six retailers did (JCPenney, Kmart, Kohl's, Macy's, Neiman Marcus, Sears) — same press release — Nov 13, 2025
- Worst offenders: Bass Pro Shops, Bed Bath & Beyond, Dick's, Foot Locker, Gap, JCPenney, Michaels, Nordstrom, Old Navy, Wayfair. "Most of the items tracked at those stores were always or almost always on sale." — same press release — Nov 13, 2025
- Honest stores: "Only Apple, Costco, and Dell consistently conducted legitimate discounts." — same press release — Nov 13, 2025
- Borderline: Walmart. 10 of its 24 tracked items were on sale at least 50% of the time, and its items overall were on sale 48% of the time — same press release — Nov 13, 2025
- Outliers: Banana Republic and Lululemon. Most of their tracked items were marked "Final Sale." The other 19 retailers labeled items "on sale" about 76% of the time on average — same press release — Nov 13, 2025
- Quote from Kevin Brasler, executive editor: "By constantly offering items at sale prices—and rarely if ever offering them at regular prices—retailers are engaging in deceptive advertising to boost their profits." Also: "Seeing 50 percent or 60 percent off a regular price doesn't mean anything. The only way to make sure you're getting the best deal is to shop around." — same press release — Nov 13, 2025
- Earlier rounds: 2015, 2018 and 2022, each with 40 or more weeks of tracking — same press release — Nov 13, 2025. Full report: https://www.checkbook.org/national/sale-fail/
- More numbers from the same study, as reported by WKMG: at 12 retailers, more than half the tracked products were at false discounts every or nearly every week. Nine retailers were rated "often misleading" and three "sometimes misleading." Some prices swung by as much as 100% over several months — ClickOrlando/WKMG (Donovan Myrie), "Dollars & Sense: Fake sales, the discount deception" — https://www.clickorlando.com/news/local/2026/10/30/dollars-sense-fake-sales-the-discount-deception/ — Oct 30, 2026
- Do not mix up the studies. Older coverage of an earlier round (24 retailers, 33 weeks) named Costco, Apple and Target (borderline) as the honest stores. "Usually misleading" stores were Amazon, Gap, Banana Republic, Nordstrom, Dick's, Old Navy, Foot Locker, Wayfair and Williams Sonoma — CBS Boston/WBZ (Courtney Cole) — https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/boston/news/consumer-expert-sale-prices-major-retailers-misleading — page updated Nov 21, 2023. Use the 2025 lists (Apple/Costco/Dell, Walmart borderline) for 2026 copy.
- No 2026 Checkbook study was found as of Nov 20, 2026 (one search). UNVERIFIED whether a new round exists.

### Groundwork Collaborative report on Amazon deal pages (Nov 2026, the newest study found)
- Released Nov 16, 2026 by the Washington think tank Groundwork Collaborative. It covered about 351 products from Amazon's own Black Friday and Cyber Monday deal pages in 2024 and 2025. Finding: 29.1% were "priced the same or higher during the sale event than the month leading up to it." ### Visualping Black Friday 2025 report (fetched)
- Visualping tracked 2,620 products daily at 9 retailers (named: Amazon, Best Buy, Staples, Canadian Tire, plus others) from Oct 1 to Dec 1, 2025. 1,659 of them had at least one price change. The "Black Friday price" was compared with the lowest price since Oct 1 — Visualping blog, "Black Friday 2025 Deals: Real or Fake?" — https://visualping.io/blog/black-friday-2025-fake-deals-report — updated Nov 28, 2025
- Results: 33% were "fake deals" (higher than the season low) and 67% were genuine. Fake deals averaged 37% above the lowest observed price — same — Nov 28, 2025
- By category: Travel and Games & Hobbies had the most fake deals (43%). Home & Appliances and Tech & Electronics had the fewest (33%). By product: luggage 61%, microwaves 50%, video game consoles 14%, backpacks 4% — same — Nov 28, 2025
- Caveat: Visualping sells the monitoring tool. The intro says "over 1,500" products while the methodology says 2,620. The sample mixes US and Canadian retailers.

## B. Law and regulation

### Federal: FTC Guides Against Deceptive Pricing, 16 CFR Part 233 (fetched via Cornell LII)
- §233.1(a): a former price is legitimate when it is "the actual, bona fide price at which the article was offered to the public on a regular basis." If the former price is "not bona fide but fictitious," the advertised bargain is false — https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/233.1 — regulation in force (originally adopted 1964)
- §233.1(b): the price must have been openly and actively offered for a "reasonably substantial period of time," "honestly and in good faith," in the recent, regular course of business. A former price "is not necessarily fictitious merely because no sales at the advertised price were made." — same
- Enforcement gap: Monica Vaca, former deputy director of the FTC Bureau of Consumer Protection, says inflating an original price and advertising a fake list price violate the FTC Act. She also says that since 2021 the FTC "can't get money back" and can only seek injunctions. Brasler says the FTC hasn't enforced its own deceptive-pricing rules in more than 40 years — Hawaii News Now / InvestigateTV (Gray TV syndication) — https://www.hawaiinewsnow.com/2026/07/27/how-some-retailers-inflate-original-prices-create-fake-discounts/ — Jul 27, 2026. The FTC did not comment.
- Brasler, in an earlier segment: "The FTC has just chosen, basically, not to enforce its own rule on this" — CBS Boston — https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/boston/news/consumer-expert-sale-prices-major-retailers-misleading — updated Nov 21, 2023
- No 2025–2026 FTC enforcement action against a retailer's reference pricing was found, and no revision of Part 233 — law-firm search results, 2026 — UNVERIFIED (absence of evidence).

### State rules
- California Bus. & Prof. Code §17501: no price may be advertised as a former price unless it was the prevailing market price within the three months before the ad, or unless "the date when the alleged former price did prevail is clearly, exactly and conspicuously stated in the advertisement." — https://california.public.law/codes/business_and_professions_code_section_17501 — statute (fetched)
- California's appellate decision in People v. Superior Court (J.C. Penney), 2019, rejected vagueness challenges. "Prevailing market price" is read as the market price, not only the retailer's own former price — Tushnet blog — https://tushnet.com/2019/04/29/prevailing-price-regs-not-unduly-vague-but-commercial-speech-doctrine-may-still-defeat-them/ — Apr 29, 2019 (snippet)
- Massachusetts 940 CMR 6.05(3): a seller's former price counts as bona fide if it was offered openly and in good faith for 14 days right before the ad, or for at least 28 of the 90 days before it (or within another 90-day window in the prior 12 months). Words like "regularly" or "originally" mean the seller's own former price — https://law.cornell.edu/regulations/massachusetts/940-CMR-6-05 — snippet. Verify against the current mass.gov text.

### Retailer definitions of reference prices
- Amazon (from aboutamazon.com, Amazon's corporate news site, fetched; amazon.com itself not opened): "List Price" is also known as MSRP, "a validated price customers can find on Amazon and at other retailers." "Typical" or "Was" Price is the median price paid on Amazon in the last 90 days, excluding limited-time-deal prices — https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/retail/how-amazon-offers-genuine-deals-to-customers — published Nov 20, 2024, updated Aug 13, 2026
- Amazon seller rule (snippet): from Apr 23, 2026, a seller's List Price must meet validation criteria, such as a recent offer at that price by another retailer or recent Featured Offer sales — https://sell.amazon.com/blog/amazon-list-price — UNVERIFIED (not fetched)
- Walmart: Comparison Price is "defined by an item's List Price or Was Price." List Price is the MSRP. Was Price is "the 90-day median price paid by customers for the item on Walmart.com," or the median offered price for at least 28 of the last 90 days. Both exclude holiday campaigns, limited-time deals, rollbacks and clearance — Walmart Marketplace Learn (seller-facing) — https://marketplacelearn.walmart.com/guides/Policies%20&%20standards/Product%20listings/Comparison-Price-rules — fetched Nov 20, 2026, undated
- Target: the strikethrough "Was" price is based on "the 90-day median price offered for the item on Target.com and the Target app," excluding sales, clearance and limited-time promotions. MSRP is "the suggested retail price provided by the manufacturer, supplier or seller" and may not reflect the actual selling price. "New Lower Price" is a price after a reduction — Target Help — https://www.target.com/help/article/000194850 — fetched Nov 20, 2026, undated. No Target definition of "Reg" was found (UNVERIFIED).
- Best Buy: "Best Buy and its marketplace sellers may identify reference prices from a number of sources." "Comparable Value" (Comp. Value) is "based on prices at which the product, or a comparable item, was" offered by Best Buy, marketplace sellers, manufacturers, suppliers or other retailers (paraphrase of the rest). No "Was" definition was found on the page — Best Buy pricing policy — https://www.bestbuy.com/pricing — effective 1/24/25
