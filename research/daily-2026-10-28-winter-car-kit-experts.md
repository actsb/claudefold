# Experts, video, photos brief: Winter Car Emergency Kit 2026 (research date 2026-10-28)

Rules applied: no Amazon ratings, review counts, review quotes or Amazon prices (Car and Driver's scraper table prices are omitted on purpose). Quotes are only from pages WebFetch or curl actually returned. Items from a search snippet only are tagged SNIPPET. Anything not confirmed is UNVERIFIED.

## 0. Biggest findings
- Every government and club checklist agrees on the core: flashlight with spare batteries, blankets or sleeping bag, warm clothes, first aid, water and snacks, phone and charger, shovel, ice scraper and brush, sand or cat litter, jumper cables or jump starter, flares or reflective triangles. The guide can say "official lists agree" and then separate "everyone says" from "what is overkill".
- Overkill candidates the lists themselves hint at: tire chains (CDC lists them, CR pairs them with traction boards, Ready.gov says only "if your area requires"), canned sealant (CR: "may not work well in extreme cold"), tow chains, a fire extinguisher, a compass, a 7-gallon water jug, fuel cans (all from the NBC Select shopping list, see 1.9). This is our reading, not a quote.
- Storage: Minnesota DPS and Synchrony say keep the kit in the passenger area because the trunk can freeze shut or be crushed (SNIPPET only for both; I could not open the DPS PDF). Title says "trunk", so address this in the guide.
- Stranded: NWS says stay in the vehicle, run the motor about 10 minutes each hour, crack a window, clear snow from the exhaust pipe (fetched, 2.1). CDC says a blocked tailpipe lets carbon monoxide "build up quickly inside your vehicle" (fetched, 2.2). The "100 yards" Red Cross rule is SNIPPET only (not on a page I could open).
- Not obtained: Wirecutter, NYT, Popular Mechanics (nytimes.com and popularmechanics.com are blocked for the search crawler). NHTSA pages return 403 to every fetch. Do NOT cite their texts. The only NHTSA line I have is relayed by New York's DMV (1.5).
- Consumer Reports: the winter-kit page loaded (1.4). CR's jump-starter ratings page is SNIPPET only: it says CR is no longer testing jump starters (UNVERIFIED, the fetch 404ed).
- Product photos: NO brand offered a quoted editorial-use sentence. Nothing downloaded (section 4).

## 1. Expert checklists: what each actually says

### 1.1 Ready.gov, "Be Prepared for a Car Emergency" (page text fetched)
- URL: https://www.ready.gov/car, page shows "Last Updated: 03/12/2026".
- Vehicle kit items: cables for jump-starting; flares or reflective warning triangle; tool for scraping ice off the windshield; car charger for your phone; warm covering; paper road map; cat litter or sand to improve tire grip.
- Winter notes: winter tires "plus any chains or studs your local area requires"; a full gas tank keeps the fuel line from freezing; turn off cruise control on icy roads; keep the kit in the car in case you get stranded.
- It does not list a shovel, water, food or a flashlight. Do not attribute those to Ready.gov.

## 2. How-to: official stuck-in-snow instructions

### 2.1 NWS, "Winter Weather: During a Storm" (fetched)
- URL: https://www.weather.gov/safety/winter-during (no page date returned).
- "Stay in the vehicle!" Leaving "will become disoriented quickly in wind-driven snow and cold."
- "Run the motor about 10 minutes each hour for heat." Dome light on at night while running.
- "While running the motor, open the window a little for fresh air to avoid carbon monoxide poisoning."
- "Clear snow from the exhaust pipe to avoid gas poisoning."

### 2.2 CDC, "Clear Snow from Tailpipes" (fetched)
- URL: https://www.cdc.gov/natural-disasters/psa-toolkit/clear-snow-from-tailpipes.html; first published May 15, 2024, updated Aug 26, 2026.
- "Before you drive after a winter storm, check to be sure that your vehicle's tailpipe is clear of snow."
- Carbon monoxide is "an odorless, colorless, and deadly gas produced by your engine" that can "build up quickly inside your vehicle, poisoning anyone inside" if the tailpipe is blocked.
- Also (fetched, CDC CO page, updated Oct 26, 2026, https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/about/index.html): "Have a mechanic check the exhaust system of your car or truck every year." No idling guidance on either page.

### 2.3 AAA, getting unstuck (SNIPPET, the aaa.com page 404ed; steps relayed by a search summary)
- URLs seen: https://www.aaa.com/autorepair/articles/how-to-get-unstuck-from-snow (404 on fetch), https://info.oregon.aaa.com/aaa-offers-tips-for-motorists-stuck-in-snow/ (not opened).
- Steps as summarised (UNVERIFIED until the page is opened): do not spin the wheels; turn off traction control; clear snow in front, under and behind the car; start gently in low gear; rock the car between drive and reverse, stopping fully before shifting (transmission risk); keep helpers out from directly behind the wheels; sprinkle sand, litter or rock salt under the tires; if still stuck, call for help; AAA cannot provide snow removal.
- Second SNIPPET: a Washington State DOT-sourced brochure on rocking says check the owner's manual first because rocking can damage some transmissions (https://www.plu.edu/safety/wp-content/uploads/sites/133/2014/10/plu-winter-driving-brochure.pdf, not opened).

### 2.4 Staying with the vehicle
- NWS: stay in the vehicle (2.1). NY State Police advice via search summary: stay in the vehicle and call 911 (SNIPPET). Minnesota State Patrol: "Stay in the vehicle and call 911" (SNIPPET).
- Red Cross "stay unless help is 100 yards away or less": SNIPPET from a news site, not the Red Cross page.
- Snopes (https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/carbon-monoxide-snowed-in-car/) returned 402, nothing read.

## 5. Notes for the writer
- Supported structure: a "core kit" every official source names (about 12 items, under $100 if you skip the jump starter), a "worth adding" layer (jump starter, traction boards, folding shovel, headlamp, power bank), and an "overkill" layer (chains, sealant, tow chains, fire extinguisher, fuel cans) with CR and CDC as the cited lists.
- Cold-weather caveats with a source: the GB40 manual forbids jump-starting a frozen battery and rates -20 C to 50 C; The Drive says jump starters lose charge in the same cold; CR says sealant "may not work well in extreme cold"; power banks lose output below 0 C (Anker's own blog, vendor source).

- Shovel evidence: no independent test. Present as "every list names one", pick on size and length with our own analysis.
- Do NOT claim Wirecutter, Popular Mechanics, NHTSA or Red Cross lists. Anything marked SNIPPET above needs opening before it becomes a quote.
- Count: about 60 search and fetch lookups, a number returned errors (403, 404, 503) as noted.
