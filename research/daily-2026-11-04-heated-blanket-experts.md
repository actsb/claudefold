## 2. Expert sources (what each says; "opened" = page fetched)
1. State Farm, "The dos and don'ts of electric blanket safety" https://www.statefarm.com/simple-insights/safety/the-dos-and-donts-of-electric-blanket-safety (opened; page showed Sept 18, 2026). No single replacement age; replace if stiff, crushed, lumpy, label missing, or history unknown; stop use if recalled or damaged. Do not tuck tightly under mattress; keep flat, avoid folds and bunching. Do not pile heavy bedding or objects on top unless manufacturer allows. Plug into wall outlet, no extension cord or power strip. Test heat settings and auto shutoff while awake before overnight use. Stop if controller gets unusually hot. Extra caution for young children, some older adults, people with reduced heat sensitivity or limited mobility. Its related page says to check CPSC for recalls (snippet).
2. UL Solutions, "Electric Blankets Are Cozy, But Are They Safe?" https://www.ul.com/insights/electric-blankets-are-cozy-are-they-safe (opened; no date shown on page; image path hints 2020-05, UNVERIFIED). Says only use blankets approved by nationally recognized testing agencies such as UL; UL "performs independent tests"; the logo "means a representative sample of the blankets has been tested for safety." Does not mention UL 964. Tips: preheat about 30 minutes then switch off before getting in; check for worn areas, loose plugs, cracks, charred spots and discard damaged; keep pets away; do not fold while in use or layer bedding over it; not on sofa beds or adjustable beds; roll to store; no secondhand blankets. Avoid use by infants, young children, paralyzed people, people with incontinence; diabetes raises burn risk (quotes Derek E. Bell, MD, Kessler Burn Center, Univ. of Rochester).
3. UL 964 "Electrically Heated Bedding" (snippet only via standards catalogs, UNVERIFIED). Scope includes heated blankets, throws, mattress pads. Many retail listings claim "ETL certified" or "passes UL 964"; that is not the same as a UL Listed mark. Verify in UL Product iQ or Intertek directory.
4. ESFI (Electrical Safety Foundation International) https://www.esfi.org/heating-pads-and-electric-blankets-safety/ (fetch returned 403; snippet only, UNVERIFIED wording). Says around 500 fires a year from heating pads and electric blankets, almost all involving blankets over 10 years old; keep items and pets off while on; do not fold or tuck while on; no extension cord or power strip; replace if charred, frayed or cracked cord; never sleep with heating pad on. Also https://www.esfi.org/electric-blanket-fire-prevention/ (not opened).
5. Fairfax County Fire and Rescue https://www.fairfaxcounty.gov/fire-ems/node/194 (opened, no date). Quotes: "Make sure your blanket has been approved by a nationally recognized testing agency." "Turn your blanket off when not in use." "Don't sit or lay on top of an electric blanket." "Never pile toys, pillows, or stuffed animals on top." "Don't wash an electric blanket." (conflicts with the Sunbeam and Bedsure manuals, which allow washing; use the manual). Most models lack internal temperature control. Internal damage cannot be inspected; if in doubt, stop using.
6. Healthline https://www.healthline.com/health/are-electric-blankets-safe (opened; written Feb 28 2020, medically reviewed Feb 28 2020, Deborah Weatherspoon). Buy tested (UL) blankets; inspect for tears, scorch marks, frayed cord, hold to light for shifted wires; off before sleep if no timer; do not tuck under mattress, lie or sit on, or pile on; roll to store. Cites Columbia University: 99 percent of electric blanket fires involve blankets 10 years or older. People with diabetes: neuropathy can reduce heat sensitivity; Mayo Clinic suggests warming the bed then removing the blanket before getting in. Poor circulation: consult doctor. Pregnant: cites WHO advice to discontinue (as stated by Healthline).
7. Medical News Today https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/are-electric-blankets-safe (opened; written Mar 9 2021, reviewed by Deborah Weatherspoon). Replace blankets over 10 years; check for damage before each use; off before sleep; caution for children (under 5 generally should not use), pregnancy, diabetes or circulation problems, older adults, dementia. Modern blankets are safer due to features such as automatic shut-off.
8. Mayo Clinic Care Network FAQ "Diabetes: are electric blankets off limits" https://mymlc.com/health-information/faqs/d/diabetes-are-electric-blankets-off-limits (page 404 on fetch; snippet only, UNVERIFIED). Snippet: warm the bed beforehand, then switch off or remove the blanket before getting in.
9. Consumer Reports. No electric-blanket guide found. Only found: crib article advising no electric blanket or heating pad to warm a baby's crib (https://www.consumerreports.org/cro/news/2011/12/keep-baby-cozy-in-the-crib-without-blankets/index.htm; snippet, 2011). Do not cite CR for heated blanket testing.
10. NFPA. No electric-blanket page. NFPA home-fire statistics table (snippet): heating pads about 77 fires a year and electric blankets about 57 per year, 2019 to 2023, UNVERIFIED. NFPA heating tip sheet is about space heaters; do not cite for blankets.
11. CPSC. No general electric-blanket tips page found. Cite CPSC recall pages above and SaferProducts.gov for incident reports (snippet).
12. Wirecutter, Good Housekeeping: could not search either domain. Bedsure's own press page claims a Good Housekeeping Seal and "Best Overall Heated Blanket 2026" (https://bedsurehome.com/pages/press, opened) which is brand self-reporting; GH page itself NOT verified. ## 3. Manuals
- Sunbeam heated throw instruction booklet (Sunbeam's own manual, hosted by Costco): https://www.costco.com/wcsstore/CostcoUSBCCatalogAssetStore/Attachment/Sunbeam_HeatedBedding_IB_Throw.pdf (opened with pdftotext; warranty names Jarden Consumer Solutions, so an older, generic booklet). Rule 1 (caps): do not use with "AN INFANT, A CHILD, AN INCAPACITATED OR IMMOBILE PERSON, A PARAPLEGIC, A QUADRIPLEGIC, A DIABETIC, ONE INSENSITIVE TO HEAT SUCH AS A PERSON WITH POOR BLOOD CIRCULATION, OR ANYONE WHO CANNOT CLEARLY UNDERSTAND THE INSTRUCTIONS AND/OR OPERATE THE CONTROLS." Do not tuck in, pinch, flex; all of product and controllers visible; no damaged cords; no extension cords, generators or inverters; heat increases where bunched or folded; keep pets away; loop cord loosely. Washing: disconnect power cord from wall BEFORE detaching control cord; presoak 15 min in mild soap and cold water; gentle cycle about 2 min; no bleach, dry cleaning, ironing or wringer; low dryer 10 minutes (no laundromat dryers); drape to finish, no clothespins; completely dry before reconnecting. Warranty 5 years for Sunbeam-branded. No auto-shutoff hours stated in the pages I read.
- Sunbeam care page https://www.sunbeam.com/Sunbeam-Heated-Bedding-Care-and-Cleaning.html (403 on fetch; snippet agrees with the booklet). Sunbeam LoftTec throw product page with instruction booklet: https://www.sunbeam.com/sunbeam/sunbeam-lofttec-heated-throw-walnut/TRL8WS-R470-25A44.html (not opened).
- Bedsure Heated Blanket Guidebook https://bedsurehome.com/blogs/products/heated-blanket-guidebook (opened). Connect coupler securely before plugging in; 10 to 15 minutes to warm; lay flat over flat sheet, heated area on top not tucked, controller outside the covers and visible; check MODEL NUMBER on product label matches controller back; use only the included Bedsure controller; if controller too hot: off, unplug, cool 20 to 30 min, make sure it is not wrapped. Wash: unplug first, disconnect coupler, cool 20 to 30 min, presoak 15 min cold with mild soap, gentle cycle, household dryer only on low, plug in only when blanket and coupler completely dry. Do not use with other heated products at once; do not sit on or put heavy objects on it. Error code starting with F: unplug, disconnect coupler, check 120V outlet, reconnect. Other Bedsure guides: https://bedsurehome.com/blogs/products

## 5. Suggested guide takeaways
Check label model number against the six recall tables above; only OCTROT and JKMAX (plus Melinora warning) are 2026; Sunbeam and Bedsure recalls are 2023 and model-specific. If recalled: stop, unplug, cut cord, photo, refund. General: replace at about 10 years or on damage; no tucking, no pile of covers, no extension cords; inspect cord and controller; do not use for infants, immobile or incapacitated people, diabetics or people with poor circulation (Sunbeam manual); preheat then switch off (UL, Mayo snippet).
