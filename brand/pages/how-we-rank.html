Short version: we rank on evidence, we weight what matters to owners after month three, and we tell you who each product is not for.

1. What goes into a ranking

InputWeightWhat we look at
Independent lab tests35%Measured performance from outlets that publish their methods (pickup percentages, measured suction, battery run time, noise, obstacle-avoidance scores).
Owner reviews30%Recurring themes in Amazon reviews (sorted by most recent, all star levels) and long-term owner threads. A theme counts when it shows up repeatedly across independent reviewers, not once.
Value20%Typical street price, sale history, and 3-year cost of ownership including consumables.
Support and longevity15%Warranty terms, parts availability, firmware/app track record, company stability.

2. What does not affect a ranking

  • Affiliate commission rates. Amazon pays the same category rate regardless of brand.
  • Review units. We do not currently accept products from manufacturers; if that changes, the post will say so.
  • Star ratings alone. A 4.6-star product with 800 reviews that all mention the same failure ranks below a 4.4-star product whose complaints are scattered and minor.

3. How to read our verdicts

  • Best Overall — the product we would buy for a typical US home if money were no object within the category's normal price range.
  • Best Value — 80–90% of the flagship experience at a much lower price.
  • Budget Pick — the cheapest product we would actually recommend, with its compromises named.
  • Use-case picks (pets, carpet, large homes, quiet, etc.) — the winner for that one priority.
  • Skip — popular products we think you should avoid, and why.

4. Corrections and updates

Guides are refreshed at least every six months or when a new generation ships. The update date is shown under the title. If you spot an error, contact us.
