The 14 best robot vacuums of 2026

Ranked by use case rather than by a single score, because the right robot for a 3,000 sq ft carpeted house with two Labradors is the wrong robot for a 700 sq ft hardwood apartment. Every pick lists its list price and the sale price we've seen this year; buy at the latter.

Best overall Roborock Saros 10R

Runtime: 180 min (about 2,500–3,000 sq ft)
Mop: dual spinning pads, extending arm, lifts on carpet

The Saros 10R is the robot every lab keeps ranking first, and after reading its owner reviews we understand why: it's the one that generates the fewest "it got stuck / it ate a cable / it missed a room" complaints. Vacuum Wars scored it a perfect 24 of 24 on its obstacle course — the first robot to do that — and named it the best robot vacuum it has tested; Tom's Guide measured a 98.5% cleaning score. The turret-free StarSight system is the reason it stands 3.14 inches tall, which in practice means it cleans under sofas and beds that every 4-inch LiDAR robot skips. Noise is a civilized 65 dB in normal mode, the app is the best in the category, and the dock does everything short of dumping its own dirty water.

The honest caveats: the mopping is pad-based, which is very good for daily grime and merely fine for dried-on messes — the roller-mop robots below beat it there. Its measured intake suction is below the Vacuum Wars average even though its pickup results are excellent (the brush design and airflow do the work), which is the whole point of our suction section. And the list price is fantasy: it has spent most of 2026 between $885 and $1,000, and that is the price at which it is the best robot you can buy.

Pros

  • Best obstacle avoidance tested (24/24)
  • 3.14-inch body fits under almost everything
  • Best app; per-room control, remote camera view
  • Full omni dock with hot-water washing and drying
  • Quiet; anti-tangle brush and side brush

Cons

  • Pad mop loses to roller-mop rivals on stains
  • List price is $500+ above the real price
  • Auto-empty port can clog in heavy pet-hair homes

Most common owner complaint: the dock's auto-empty port clogging after a few months in multi-pet homes — check it monthly. Who should skip it: heavy mopping households (get the Narwal Flow 2), and anyone who can only get it at list.

Best mopping Narwal Flow 2

IMAGE SLOT — Amazon SiteStripe → Image → Narwal Flow 2.
List price: $1,499
Seen on sale: $1,099 (recurring promotion)
Navigation: LiDAR + dual HD cameras with a visual-language model
Claimed suction: 31,000 Pa
Mop: FlowWash roller, self-rinsing, extends to the edge
Dock: auto-empty (120-day bag), 212°F hot-water roller wash, heated dry, auto-refill

If the reason you're buying a robot is the floors — paw prints, kitchen grime, a toddler's dinner — the Flow 2 is the one. In The Hook Up's 2026 side-by-side, the Narwal left the test floor visibly clean where the eufy Omni S2 left noticeable mud; Vacuum Wars' three-month test found strong mopping, strong pet-hair pickup and strong obstacle avoidance. A 120-day dust bag is nearly double the eufy's 68 days. The cameras run a model that estimates how dirty each area is and re-cleans it, and unlike most "AI" features it does change behavior in a useful way.

Best for long hair & no camera iRobot Roomba Max 705 Vac

IMAGE SLOT — Amazon SiteStripe → Image → iRobot Roomba Max 705 Vac (ASIN B0DWG3C3ZF).
List price: $899.99
Seen on sale: $500–$650
Navigation: LiDAR, no camera
Brush: dual rubber anti-tangle rollers
Suction: "180× the Roomba 600 series" (no Pa figure)
Dock: AutoEmpty (bagged); Combo version adds mop + AutoWash

Ignore the corporate drama for a moment and the Max 705 is a very good vacuum-first robot. Consumer Reports, RTINGS and Tom's Guide all found strong pickup — one- to two-pass removal of long hair, crumbs and coffee grounds — and the dual rubber rollers remain the best design for long human hair, shedding it instead of gripping it. There's no camera, which some owners prefer, and the app is simple and dependable. The Combo version trades a little carpet suction for integrated mopping and an AutoWash dock.

Now the drama: iRobot went through Chapter 11 and is owned by its manufacturer as of 2026, with eight new models replacing this generation. Owners report mixed support experiences during the transition. Our advice is specific: buy the 705 only at a deep discount, only through Amazon (its return window is your warranty backstop), and only if your priorities are long hair, hard floors and no camera — because without obstacle avoidance it will run over the cable and the pet accident that a $700 Roborock steers around.

Most common owner complaint: support response since the bankruptcy. Who should skip it: pet owners whose pets have accidents, and anyone with a cable-strewn floor.

Best for carpet under $350 Shark PowerDetect Self-Empty

Height / width: 2.7 in / 13 in
Navigation: LiDAR; DirtDetect, FloorDetect, EdgeDetect sensors

For a carpet-heavy house on a tight budget, the PowerDetect is the robot to beat. Vacuum Wars found it one of the best in its price range on carpet thanks to a high deep-cleaning score, with solid hard-floor pickup too; its sensors ramp power on dirt, carpet and edges, which is where budget robots usually coast. Keep cords and rug fringe off the floor and it's a workhorse.

Most common owner complaint: getting stuck on cords and rug tassels; the dust puff from the bagless bin. Who should skip it: homes with loose cables, and large homes over 2,000 sq ft.
