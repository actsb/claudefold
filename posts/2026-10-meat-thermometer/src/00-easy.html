Vera's 10-second verdict

Why is the ThermoPop 2 our first pick?

Because it is the model both a test kitchen and ThermoWorks-forum owners reach for when the question is value, and it does the one job a Thanksgiving cook needs: a trustworthy number in a few seconds.

America's Test Kitchen's June 2025 test (The Best Instant-Read Thermometers of 2025) names the ThermoPop 2 its Best Buy: 2.6 seconds on average, within 0.2 °F at 32 °F and 0.3 °F at 125 °F, and it survived the lab's 30-minute dunk in 39 inches of water. Serious Eats' 2023 test of 18 thermometers (syndicated copy) also called it the best inexpensive pick at $35 with a ±1 °F claim. Amazing Ribs awarded it a Gold medal (summary read October 2026; review).

What owners praise

  • It is enough for most home cooking. Regulars on the TVWBB grilling forum call it all most cooks need; one reported several Pops working reliably and bought them as gifts (TVWBB, December 2022).
  • The thin probe. A SmokingMeatForums owner of both a Pop and a Thermapen says the Pop's thinner probe is the one he uses on ribs because it fits between bones (thread undated).
  • The big rotating display is easy to read from either hand.

What they complain about

  • It is slower than the ONE. Prudent Reviews (October 2025) timed 4 seconds in boiling water against 1 for the ONE, and called the Pop's fixed probe a drawback; ATK's 2.6 seconds is kinder.
  • A short start-up delay and a probe cover that goes missing (two reviewers).
  • A coin cell. It takes a CR2032; ThermoWorks' troubleshooting page says a coating on some fresh cells can stop the contact, so scrub the faces if it will not power on. Reviewers on ThermoWorks' own store page mention swapping it several times over years, which is manufacturer-hosted and anecdotal.
  • Water. ATK dunked it and it survived, but ThermoWorks' own help page tells owners to wipe it clean and not submerge it. Follow the maker.

How do the five compare on speed, accuracy and price?

The ONE is fastest and the TP19H slowest, but the three in the middle sit within a second of each other in ATK's test, which is why price and warranty decide it.

Two cautions. First, read times depend on how they are measured: Prudent Reviews timed the ONE at 1 second in boiling water but 4 seconds in an ice bath, and the ThermoPop 2 at 4 and 8 seconds, while ATK averaged 1.1 and 2.6. Second, Serious Eats found the ONE at 2 to 3 seconds in its own test (2023). The order stays the same in every test; the exact number does not. ATK's accuracy figures are for the first four models; the TP19H was not in its list, and Consumer Reports' paywalled category page (consumerreports.org) lists the TP19H and no ThermoWorks model, so we could not compare scores.

What do owners say after months and years?

ThermoWorks models earn multi-year praise on cooking forums; the cheaper TempPro units split owners, with some lasting years and others failing in the first couple.

Thermapen (ONE and older)

  • Long life. TVWBB owners describe an original Thermapen after about ten years, another nearly twenty, and a Mk4 since 2018 with no battery trouble (December 2021 to December 2022). A Slickdeals owner reports about three years on one battery.
  • The hinge is the weak spot. ThermoWorks' troubleshooting page says a unit that will not power on until the probe is fully open usually has debris or moisture in the rotating hub. The warranty covers repair for 5 years; out of warranty ThermoWorks lists $40 including return shipping.
  • Brightness and backlight. One TVWBB owner (2023) found the display less bright than he wanted; the backlight is triggered by ambient light and may stay off in bright rooms, per ThermoWorks.

TempPro / ThermoPro TP19H and Lightning

  • Value. The TP19HW has 4.7 stars from 74 reviews at Target (October 23, 2026), with praise for fast reads, backlight, magnet and price. Slickdeals commenters (2020 to 2026) report units lasting "a few years".
  • Failures. A TVWBB owner described "lines of death" on the display after about 18 months (December 2021, thread); a Target reviewer reported water under the screen after hand-washing; a couple of dead-on-arrival units; a stiff probe release. TempPro offered a replacement in one Target reply.
  • Lightning. TVWBB owners (November 2023, thread) say it survived drops and weather and is good enough next to a Thermapen; it is IP65, not for immersion.

How do you use an instant-read thermometer on a turkey?

Check the innermost part of the thigh, the thickest part of the breast and the wing, away from bone, and pull the bird only when all of them read 165 °F.

  1. Run an ice-bath check. ThermoWorks' method: fill a glass with crushed ice, add water to about half an inch below the top, stir, and hold the probe in the middle, off the walls. The Thermapen ONE's tolerance is ±0.5 °F; ThermoWorks lists ±2 °F around 32 °F for the Pop. ThermoPro models have a calibration button (hold it five seconds, per the manual summary).
  2. Probe the thigh. USDA's turkey roasting guide says the innermost part of the thigh, away from bone.
  3. Then the breast and wing. Thickest part of the breast and the wing. USDA's safe temperature chart lists 165 °F for all poultry and stuffing.
  4. Wait for the reading to settle. One second for the ONE, a few for the others. Reading early is how people under-cook.
  5. Wipe and store. Wipe the probe, close it, and do not leave it in the oven. The ONE's manual says not to rotate the probe while it is submerged and that it is not dishwasher-safe.

We could not read USDA's pages directly (the site blocked our tools), so the spots and the 165 °F figure come from search summaries of those pages; confirm on the USDA pages before the holiday.

Quick answers

What is the best meat thermometer for Thanksgiving?

For most cooks, the ThermoWorks ThermoPop 2, $35 to $39: America's Test Kitchen's Best Buy (June 2025), 2.6 seconds, within 0.3 °F. If you cook meat weekly, the Thermapen ONE, $74 in closeout at ThermoWorks on October 23, 2026.

ThermoPop 2 or Thermapen ONE?

The ONE reads in about 1 second against 2.6 to 4 for the Pop, is waterproof, and has a 5-year warranty against 2. For a turkey and weeknight chicken the Pop is enough; for weekly grilling the ONE is worth the extra.

Is the Thermapen ONE still available?

ThermoWorks showed it in colour closeout at $74 on October 23, 2026, and a deal site says few colours remain. A successor, the NANO, was announced in August 2026 (price and ship dates unconfirmed).

What temperature is a turkey safe at?

USDA says 165 °F, checked in the innermost part of the thigh, the thickest part of the breast and the wing. Stuffing needs 165 °F in its centre.

Is a $18 ThermoPro (TempPro) TP19H good enough?

It is a fair budget buy at $17.99 for the TP19HW at Target, but Prudent Reviews timed it at about 8 seconds in boiling water, the slowest in its test, and owners report some display and water failures.

Did ThermoPro change its name?

Yes. It became TempPro, announced April 8, 2026, with models and warranties unchanged according to the company. Listings may say either name.

How do I check if my thermometer is accurate?

Use an ice bath: crushed ice, a little water, stir, probe in the middle. It should read about 32 °F. ThermoWorks quotes ±0.5 °F for the ONE and ±2 °F near freezing for the Pop.

Can I put an instant-read thermometer in the oven?

No. ThermoWorks says not to leave the Thermapen ONE in the oven, and the Pop is not meant for oven or grill use. Use it to check, then take it out.

Can I wash it or put it in the dishwasher?

Not in the dishwasher. The ONE is waterproof to 39 inches for 30 minutes; ThermoWorks tells ThermoPop 2 owners to wipe it, and the TempPro units are splash-proof (IP65).

Should I wait for Black Friday?

Black Friday is November 27, the day after Thanksgiving, so it is too late for the bird. ThermoWorks has taken 30 to 37 percent off the ONE in past sales, so a $74 closeout is already close to that.

The bottom line: start with the ThermoPop 2

The ThermoPop 2 is not the fastest, and it asks you to wipe it rather than wash it. It is still the thermometer we would hand a friend before Thanksgiving: a test kitchen's Best Buy at $35 to $39, a 2-year warranty, and a number in a few seconds. If you grill every week and the Thermapen ONE is still $74, buy that instead and stop thinking about it. If $18 is the limit, the TP19H will do, slowly.

Whichever you buy, do the ice-bath check once, probe the thigh, breast and wing, and trust the number, not the pop-up timer.
