Before you buy: settings and privacy

Every TV here asks to track what you watch, every maker lets you refuse, and none of them lets you refuse everything.

The tracking is called automatic content recognition, or ACR. A UC Davis, UCL and UC3M study presented at the ACM Internet Measurement Conference in 2024 found Samsung and LG sets capture screenshots as often as every 10 milliseconds and send a fingerprint to the maker, for linear TV and anything on HDMI such as a game console, but not for Netflix or Prime. UC Davis news, December 18, 2024 quotes its lead researcher: it is easy to opt in, and opting out is an extensive process.

Our related research on the owner's own site covers the step-by-step menus: Prof H Lab's how to turn off AI on a Samsung TV and its guide to turning off AI on a TV (published August 30, 2026 and updated September 28, 2026; they cover Samsung, LG, Roku, Fire TV and Vizio, so this guide fills in TCL and Sony). Prof. H runs Prof H Lab; this page earns commissions, that one does not.

Samsung: Viewing Information Services

Samsung's support page says ACR runs only if you opted in and that you can change that at any time in the TV's Settings, but it gives no path. Consumer Reports (updated October 19, 2025) gives it as Settings, All Settings, General & Privacy, Terms & Privacy: uncheck Viewing Information Services and Interest-Based Advertisements. Turning it off still leaves generic ads. Under a judgment signed February 27, 2026 in Collin County, Texas, Samsung must get express consent before using viewing data on Texas sets; the order lasts five years and does not name other states.

LG: Live Plus and User Agreements

Consumer Reports gives Settings, All Settings, Support, Privacy & Terms, User Agreements: switch off Viewing Information, and use "Do Not Sell My Personal Information". LG's own newsroom statement, dated September 12, 2026, says ACR is optional and off by default. The Copilot tile is separate: it arrived by webOS update, and PCWorld (December 2025) reported LG would "allow users to delete the shortcut icon". We could not confirm the update shipped, so check your TV rather than trust a headline. LG settled with Texas, reported May 11, 2026.

TCL and Sony: Google TV

On Google TV the switch you will find is the advertising ID: Settings, Privacy, Ads, reset or delete it. TCL's support page adds a flat "You can't turn off ads". No TCL page we found names ACR. Sony is clearer: its support articles (regional versions, read November 2026) say to open System Preferences, choose Samba Interactive TV and disable it, then reset the advertising ID; Samba applies only to certain Bravia models. Gemini is the open question on both brands. Gemini can change settings by voice on the QM7L (9to5Google, June 11, 2026), and we found no page explaining how to switch it off. Texas sued Sony and TCL in December 2025; no settlement had been reported in the sources we found.

Roku: Smart TV Experience

Roku's support article (updated June 23, 2026): Home, Settings, Privacy, Smart TV Experience, then uncheck ACR. It covers antenna, set-top boxes and HDMI inputs, not streaming apps. Roku keeps the data it collected while ACR was on. Roku's ad-preference page (June 18, 2026) says video ads and certain ads shown to everyone, such as themed backgrounds, cannot be controlled, and its home-screen page (August 25, 2026) says the new layout is now the standard experience with no setting to switch back.

Does switching ACR off end the data collection?

No. Consumer Reports' James K. Willcox wrote in 2021 that even with ACR off, a smart TV keeps sending information to its maker, and that the only complete stop is to disconnect it from the internet (we read that article through a Yahoo copy; the current Consumer Reports page is paywalled). We found nothing newer that contradicts it, so the honest options are to switch off what you can, or to keep the TV offline.

What do the experts say?

Privacy researchers agree that ACR is on by default or easy to accept, that it can be switched off, and that switching it off is not the end of data collection.

  • Consumer Reports (updated October 19, 2025): ACR tries to identify every show you watch, and you cannot stop all data collection. It lists the menu path for Samsung, LG, Roku, Sony and Google TV.
  • Anselmi et al., ACM IMC 2024 (November 2024): an audit of Samsung and LG sets in the UK and US found ACR traffic on linear TV and HDMI, none on streaming apps, and that opting out stops the traffic.
  • The Markup (December 12, 2023): ACR is "a Shazam-like service constantly running in the background", and on Roku it exists only on Roku TVs, not sticks.
  • Electronic Frontier Foundation (Opt Out October 2025): opting out of ACR can be a chore, but is possible on most smart TVs.
  • BleepingComputer (March 1, 2026) and National Law Review (May 11, 2026): the Samsung and LG settlements with Texas; both are framed around Texas consumers.

How do you do the ten-minute privacy pass?

Decline at setup, then switch off the viewing-data setting before you sign in to anything.

The privacy pass for a new TV, from Consumer Reports (October 2025) and the makers' support pages. The step people skip is the ad ID reset.
  1. Decline the first-run agreements. Say no to viewing information and interest-based ads when the TV asks; it is easier than reversing later.
  2. Delay the account. Sign in to the maker's account only when an app needs it.
  3. Switch off ACR using your brand's path above.
  4. Reset the advertising ID. Settings, Privacy, Ads on Google TV; Settings, Privacy, Advertising on Roku.
  5. Review voice. Turn off the voice assistant or microphone if you never use it; on Sony, disabling Google Assistant turns off voice control.
  6. Go offline if in doubt. Unplug Ethernet and skip Wi-Fi, and use a streaming box.

Quick answers

Is there a TV with no AI?

Not a mainstream smart TV in 2026. The Roku Select Series is the set with the fewest AI features we could find, and a TV kept offline with a streaming box is the closest to none.

Which TV brand doesn't spy on you?

None, entirely. Every brand has a viewing-data feature you can decline, and Consumer Reports says turning ACR off does not stop all data going to the maker. LG says its feature is off by default; Roku documents its switch on a page updated June 23, 2026.

Do all smart TVs spy on you?

All five platforms here offer ACR or an equivalent. A 2024 study found Samsung and LG sets capture screenshots as often as every 10 milliseconds for live TV and HDMI content.

How do I turn off ACR on a Samsung TV?

Per Consumer Reports: Settings, All Settings, General & Privacy, Terms & Privacy, then uncheck Viewing Information Services and Interest-Based Advertisements. Samsung's own page gives no path, and menus vary by year.

How do I turn off ACR and ads on an LG TV?

Settings, All Settings, Support, Privacy & Terms, User Agreements, then switch off Viewing Information. LG says ACR is off unless you accept it. Home-screen promotions are a separate setting that varies by model.

Can I remove Copilot from an LG TV?

LG said in December 2025 it would let owners delete the shortcut. We could not confirm that update shipped, so long-press the tile on your TV and see whether a delete option appears.

Does a TCL Google TV have Gemini, and can I turn it off?

The QM7L has Gemini and it can change settings by voice. We found no maker page explaining how to turn it off; the Voice Assistant setting under Privacy may only revert to the older assistant.

How do I stop my Roku TV from tracking what I watch?

Home, Settings, Privacy, Smart TV Experience, then uncheck ACR. Roku keeps data it already collected, and video ads and themed backgrounds cannot be controlled.

Can I use a smart TV without internet?

Yes. Skip Wi-Fi at setup and use a streaming box. You lose firmware updates and built-in apps, and Consumer Reports says disconnecting is the only full stop.

Is the Samsung Frame TV available without AI?

We did not check the Frame in this guide. Samsung's 2026 sets ship with Vision AI features, so the answer is settings, not a model.
