What do owners say after months and years?

Owners praise the small size and the first jump, then split on durability: the complaints are swollen or dead packs after a year or two and weak starts in the cold.

What they praise

  • The first jump works and the pack is glovebox-sized. The Drive's Hank O'Hop (updated February 2025) says he has used his own GB40 on a 440 cubic-inch V8 many times.

What they complain about

  • Cold starts.   • Packs that die or swell. A September 2023 Garage Journal poster reported a swollen GB40 after a jump and said NOCO would not replace the battery out of warranty; a 2022 Expedition Portal poster found a two-year-old GB40 dead, possibly from buttons pressed in a bag; a Home Depot reviewer reported a GB70 that swelled after about three years. These are single reports, not failure rates.
  • Fulfilment. Low ratings on Walmart's GB70 page mention wrong items and used replacements (search summary, October 2026), which is a reason to buy from NOCO or a major seller.

The questions people ask before buying

  • Will it start a completely dead battery? Usually, yes, using the manual override: with the clamps correctly connected, NOCO's guide has you hold the override button for three seconds. That turns off the spark-proof and reverse-polarity protection, so double-check the clamps first.
  • How often do I recharge it? NOCO's guide gives no interval we could find; Family Handyman says the GB40 holds a charge up to a year, and owners report topping up anywhere from monthly to every six months. GOOLOO says every three months. Two to three months is the safe habit.
  • Can I leave it in the car? The makers' operating ranges stop at about -4°F and 122°F to 140°F, AAA says not to store packs in a hot car, and lithium ages faster in heat. A pack on the passenger seat in a heat wave is not a safe storage plan.

Which size works when it is below freezing?

Take the size up one step if your mornings regularly go below 0°F, and keep the pack indoors until you need it.

Consumer Reports' 0°F tests found that none of the packs it tried could start weak or dead batteries at that temperature, though most could start a frozen battery if the pack itself was at room temperature. Battery University's page on low-temperature discharge says all batteries lose capacity in the cold, with some self-warming under load. The practical rule: put the pack in the cabin overnight in a cold snap, and treat the maker's low-end temperature (-4°F for the GOOLOO; NOCO's sources disagree, with -4°F in its FAQ summary and -22°F on a datasheet) as a limit, not a promise.

How do you use a jump starter right?

Connect red to positive, then black to a bare metal ground, check the light, and start within 30 seconds.

Steps paraphrased from the NOCO GB40 user guide (a 2021 copy) and the GOOLOO GP4000 manual. The step people skip is the light check before cranking.
  1. Read your car's manual first. A few hybrids and EVs have a designated jump point, and the 12-volt battery is what you jump.
  2. Red clamp to positive, black clamp to negative or bare metal. The pack's light tells you if you reversed them (a red error light on the GB40; solid red and beeping on the GOOLOO).
  3. Plug the clamps into the pack, turn off the lights and radio, and wait for the ready light. The GB40's white Boost light means go.
  4. Crank for no more than a few seconds. NOCO's guide allows up to 30 seconds connected, then a 20 to 30 second rest between tries and no more than five tries in 15 minutes. GOOLOO says no more than four starts in two minutes.
  5. Once the engine runs, remove the clamps and unplug the pack. Do not leave it connected.
  6. Recharge it and get the car battery tested. A battery that needed a jump once usually needs another one.

Quick answers

Which NOCO jump starter do I need?

Match it to your engine: the GB40 for gas engines up to 6.0L and diesel up to 3.0L, the GB50 for 7.0L and 4.5L, the GB70 for 8.0L and 6.0L (NOCO's published ratings, October 2026). Most four- and six-cylinder vehicles are well inside the GB40.

What is the difference between the NOCO GB40 and GB50?

The GB50 adds engine headroom (7.0L gas and 4.5L diesel against 6.0L and 3.0L) and costs about $50 more ($149.95 against $99.95 list). Buy it for a V8, a small diesel or hard winters; skip it for a compact car.

Is the NOCO GB70 worth the money?

Only for a large diesel or a heavy V8. It is the only one of the three rated for a 6.0L diesel, but it costs about twice the GB40 and has the same one-year warranty.

Is the GOOLOO GP4000 as good as NOCO?

On paper it claims more, with 4,000 peak amps and engines up to 10L, but peak amps have no common test and GOOLOO lists 800A of cranking current. We found fewer dated owner reports and less independent testing for it, so buy from its own store.

Will a jump starter work in the cold?

Less well. Consumer Reports found none of the packs it tested started weak or dead batteries at 0°F, though most started a frozen battery when the pack was at room temperature. Keep the pack indoors in a cold snap and consider stepping up a size.

How often should I recharge a jump starter?

GOOLOO says every three months; NOCO gives no interval we could find, and owners report anywhere from monthly to every six months. Check it every two to three months and after each jump.

Can a jump starter start a completely dead battery?

Often, with the manual override: on the GB40 you hold the override button for three seconds with the clamps connected correctly. The override turns off the spark and reverse-polarity protection, so be certain of the clamps.

Is it safe to leave a jump starter in the car?

Not in a hot car. AAA advises against storing a pack in a hot vehicle, and the makers' operating ranges stop at 122°F to 140°F. Keep it in the cabin in summer and indoors in a freeze.

How much does a jump starter cost compared with calling for a jump?

A NOCO pack costs $99.95 to about $199.95 at list. Third-party estimates put a non-member roadside jump at about $50 to $120, so a pack pays for itself after one or two calls.

Have NOCO or GOOLOO jump starters been recalled?

We found no CPSC recall for either brand on October 30, 2026, though we could read only the first page of the CPSC list. CPSC did warn about other brands in 2026, so check any pack on Recall Check and cpsc.gov.
