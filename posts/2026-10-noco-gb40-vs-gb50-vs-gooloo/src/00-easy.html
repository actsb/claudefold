A dead battery on the first cold morning is the one time a jump starter earns its price, and the box that fits a four-cylinder Civic may not turn a diesel pickup. NOCO sells three sizes that differ mainly in engine rating: the GB40 (6.0L gas, 3.0L diesel) at a $99.95 list price, the GB50 (7.0L, 4.5L) at $149.95 and the GB70 (8.0L, 6.0L) at about $199.95. GOOLOO's GP4000 claims 4,000 "peak" amps and gas engines up to 10L for roughly $120 to $150 at the stores we saw. We read the makers' specs, three published guides, owner reports from forums and retailers, and CPSC's lists, then matched each size to an engine. We did not test the packs ourselves.

Key takeaways

  • Match the pack to the engine, not to the biggest number. NOCO rates the GB40 for gas up to 6.0L and diesel up to 3.0L, the GB50 for 7.0L and 4.5L, the GB70 for 8.0L and 6.0L (NOCO's published ratings, as summarized in search results, October 2026).
  • Most households should buy the GB40. A four- or six-cylinder gas vehicle is well inside its rating, and The Drive named it Best Overall (updated February 2025). Buy the GB50 for a V8 or a small diesel, the GB70 only for a big diesel.
  • GOOLOO's 4,000A is not twice NOCO's 2,000A. Peak amps are a marketing figure with no common test; GOOLOO's own manual lists 800A of cranking current. Compare engine ratings, warranty and evidence instead.
  • The catch is the cold and the calendar. Lithium packs lose strength in the cold and self-discharge, so a pack in a freezing glovebox or a forgotten drawer is the usual way owners end up stranded. Check the charge every two to three months.

Vera's 10-second verdict

Which jump starter size does your engine need?

A four- or six-cylinder gas vehicle: the GB40; a V8 or a diesel up to 4.5L: the GB50; a diesel above 4.5L: the GB70. These are NOCO's ratings with a margin, not guarantees.

Three published sources give three different pictures, and the gaps are the useful part. NOCO rates by engine volume. Family Handyman (Bob Lacivita, updated April 2025) uses amps: 400 to 600A is enough for a four-cylinder, while a large V8, a diesel or anywhere below 0°F calls for 1,000 to 2,000A or more. Consumer Reports' buying guide (updated October 15, 2026; CR says it has stopped testing jump starters) quotes a maker's table, from Lectron, that is much more generous than NOCO's: about 800 to 1,200 peak amps up to 2.0L, 1,200 to 2,000 up to 3.0L and 3,000 or more for diesels. We use NOCO's own ratings for the NOCO packs and leave the margin to you.

How do the four jump starters compare?

The GB40 is the value pick; the GB50 and GB70 buy engine headroom; the GP4000 buys the biggest claimed number with the thinnest dated evidence.

The first pick appears above. The two bigger NOCOs share a price ladder and a one-year warranty, so choose by engine and by how cold your mornings get.

Where the GB50 and GB70 differ from the GB40

  • Engine rating. Each step up adds about one litre of gas and 1.5 litres of diesel to the rating (GB40 to GB50 to GB70: 6.0, 7.0, 8.0L gas; 3.0, 4.5, 6.0L diesel).
  • Price. $99.95, $149.95 and about $199.95 at list: roughly $50 per step. The GB70's NOCO-store price could not be confirmed; Home Depot and Walmart show $199.95 and O'Reilly $199.99 (search results, October 2026).
  • Where you can buy it. We found the GB40 at NOCO's store, O'Reilly, Bass Pro Shops and Walmart; the GB50 at NOCO's store, Best Buy and Walmart; the GB70 at Home Depot, Walmart and O'Reilly. The GB50 has the narrowest distribution.

Is the GOOLOO GP4000 a better deal?

Only if you buy from GOOLOO's own store and accept less evidence: its 4,000 peak amps are not comparable with NOCO's ratings.

GOOLOO's manual and product page (read October 30, 2026) give 4,000A peak, 800A of cranking current for three seconds, 500A for five seconds, 99.2 Wh, gas and diesel up to 10L, an operating range of -4°F to 140°F, and "60+" starts per charge. Its warranty is shown as "up to 30 months"; 9to5Toys reported on August 6, 2026 that 18 months is included, and other listings say 12, so confirm the term at checkout. GOOLOO's store also lists the GP4000 with an inflator at $149.99 against a crossed-out $349.98 that is not a real price. The 800A cranking figure is the one to put beside NOCO's 1,000 to 2,000 peak, and even that is not a like-for-like test.

What do owners say after months and years?

Owners praise the small size and the first jump, then split on durability: the complaints are swollen or dead packs after a year or two and weak starts in the cold.

We could not open every retailer page, so the counts below are search-snapshot figures from October 2026 and the forum reports are dated. Where we only saw a summary, we say so.

What they praise

  • The first jump works and the pack is glovebox-sized. The Drive's Hank O'Hop (updated February 2025) says he has used his own GB40 on a 440 cubic-inch V8 many times.
  • Safety circuitry. The NOCO guide says the pack will not power the clamps if they are reversed (a red error light comes on instead), and The Drive praised the reverse-polarity protection.
  • The power-bank function and long cables, which The Drive also liked.

What they complain about

  • Cold starts. In a 2018 Garage Journal thread, one GB40 could not turn a 4.7L Tundra at about 10°F, and another barely turned a 2.4L Honda CR-V at 0 to 5°F until it was warmed; a GB70 owner in the same thread had no trouble in several cold tests. The thread is old and about an earlier GB40, so it is a pattern to respect, not a measurement.
  • Packs that die or swell. A September 2023 Garage Journal poster reported a swollen GB40 after a jump and said NOCO would not replace the battery out of warranty; a 2022 Expedition Portal poster found a two-year-old GB40 dead, possibly from buttons pressed in a bag; a Home Depot reviewer reported a GB70 that swelled after about three years. These are single reports, not failure rates.
  • Fulfilment. Low ratings on Walmart's GB70 page mention wrong items and used replacements (search summary, October 2026), which is a reason to buy from NOCO or a major seller.

The questions people ask before buying

  • Will it start a completely dead battery? Usually, yes, using the manual override: with the clamps correctly connected, NOCO's guide has you hold the override button for three seconds. That turns off the spark-proof and reverse-polarity protection, so double-check the clamps first.
  • How often do I recharge it? NOCO's guide gives no interval we could find; Family Handyman says the GB40 holds a charge up to a year, and owners report topping up anywhere from monthly to every six months. GOOLOO says every three months. Two to three months is the safe habit.
  • Can I leave it in the car? The makers' operating ranges stop at about -4°F and 122°F to 140°F, AAA says not to store packs in a hot car, and lithium ages faster in heat. A pack on the passenger seat in a heat wave is not a safe storage plan.

Which size works when it is below freezing?

Take the size up one step if your mornings regularly go below 0°F, and keep the pack indoors until you need it.

Consumer Reports' 0°F tests found that none of the packs it tried could start weak or dead batteries at that temperature, though most could start a frozen battery if the pack itself was at room temperature. Battery University's page on low-temperature discharge says all batteries lose capacity in the cold, with some self-warming under load. The practical rule: put the pack in the cabin overnight in a cold snap, and treat the maker's low-end temperature (-4°F for the GOOLOO; NOCO's sources disagree, with -4°F in its FAQ summary and -22°F on a datasheet) as a limit, not a promise.

How do you use a jump starter right?

Connect red to positive, then black to a bare metal ground, check the light, and start within 30 seconds.

  1. Read your car's manual first. A few hybrids and EVs have a designated jump point, and the 12-volt battery is what you jump.
  2. Red clamp to positive, black clamp to negative or bare metal. The pack's light tells you if you reversed them (a red error light on the GB40; solid red and beeping on the GOOLOO).
  3. Plug the clamps into the pack, turn off the lights and radio, and wait for the ready light. The GB40's white Boost light means go.
  4. Crank for no more than a few seconds. NOCO's guide allows up to 30 seconds connected, then a 20 to 30 second rest between tries and no more than five tries in 15 minutes. GOOLOO says no more than four starts in two minutes.
  5. Once the engine runs, remove the clamps and unplug the pack. Do not leave it connected.
  6. Recharge it and get the car battery tested. A battery that needed a jump once usually needs another one.

What do the experts say?

Published sources agree on the shape of the answer: match the pack to the engine, expect cold to hurt, and keep it charged.

  • Consumer Reports (updated October 15, 2026): its sizing table comes from a maker and is more generous than NOCO's. In its 0°F tests none of the packs started weak or dead batteries, and it advises caution with override buttons. The category is archival; CR says it is no longer testing jump starters.
  • The Drive (Hank O'Hop, updated February 11, 2025): names the NOCO GB40 Best Overall, rated for gas to 6L and diesel to 3L; it praises the cables and polarity protection and faults the bag and the few ports.
  • Family Handyman (Bob Lacivita, updated April 26, 2025): 400 to 600A for a four-cylinder, 1,000 to 2,000A or more for a V8, a diesel or very cold climates; boost mode switches off the safety features.
  • Battery University: all batteries deliver less at low temperature, and heat shortens their life.

We could not read Wirecutter, Popular Mechanics or Car and Driver for this guide, so none of them is cited. We also found no university or extension study of cold-weather jump starters.

Is any of them under a recall?

We found no CPSC recall for NOCO or GOOLOO on October 30, 2026, but other jump starters have drawn warnings.

A search of the CPSC recall list for "NOCO" returned no matches on the first page of results, which is not the same as a full database check. CPSC warned consumers in 2026 to stop using Hererod EW40 and PW40 jump starters after 16 fire reports, and warned about HALO Bolt ACDC 58830 chargers made in or before December 2019. Older recalls cover the Whistler Jump&Go and Jackco ZETA (both 2015). The UK's Electrical Safety First listed a June 2024 notice for a GOOLOO/VTOMAN model (JS-378B and X7); that is not a US action and not the GP4000. Check your model against our Recall Check page and cpsc.gov before you buy a pack from an unfamiliar brand.

What is the catch?

Every lithium jump starter is a battery you have to maintain, and the biggest number on the box does not guarantee a start.

  • It will not work if you forget it: charge it every two to three months, and after each use.
  • It can fail without warning: check it for swelling, and replace it if the case bulges.
  • The warranty is one year at NOCO, so a pack that dies in year two is on you.

For a one-engine household, the GB40 is the cheap insurance. If you also keep an eye on your car, see our NOCO GB40 review and the winter car emergency kit.

Quick answers

Which NOCO jump starter do I need?

Match it to your engine: the GB40 for gas engines up to 6.0L and diesel up to 3.0L, the GB50 for 7.0L and 4.5L, the GB70 for 8.0L and 6.0L (NOCO's published ratings, October 2026). Most four- and six-cylinder vehicles are well inside the GB40.

What is the difference between the NOCO GB40 and GB50?

The GB50 adds engine headroom (7.0L gas and 4.5L diesel against 6.0L and 3.0L) and costs about $50 more ($149.95 against $99.95 list). Buy it for a V8, a small diesel or hard winters; skip it for a compact car.

Is the NOCO GB70 worth the money?

Only for a large diesel or a heavy V8. It is the only one of the three rated for a 6.0L diesel, but it costs about twice the GB40 and has the same one-year warranty.

Is the GOOLOO GP4000 as good as NOCO?

On paper it claims more, with 4,000 peak amps and engines up to 10L, but peak amps have no common test and GOOLOO lists 800A of cranking current. We found fewer dated owner reports and less independent testing for it, so buy from its own store.

Will a jump starter work in the cold?

Less well. Consumer Reports found none of the packs it tested started weak or dead batteries at 0°F, though most started a frozen battery when the pack was at room temperature. Keep the pack indoors in a cold snap and consider stepping up a size.

How often should I recharge a jump starter?

GOOLOO says every three months; NOCO gives no interval we could find, and owners report anywhere from monthly to every six months. Check it every two to three months and after each jump.

Can a jump starter start a completely dead battery?

Often, with the manual override: on the GB40 you hold the override button for three seconds with the clamps connected correctly. The override turns off the spark and reverse-polarity protection, so be certain of the clamps.

Is it safe to leave a jump starter in the car?

Not in a hot car. AAA advises against storing a pack in a hot vehicle, and the makers' operating ranges stop at 122°F to 140°F. Keep it in the cabin in summer and indoors in a freeze.

How much does a jump starter cost compared with calling for a jump?

A NOCO pack costs $99.95 to about $199.95 at list. Third-party estimates put a non-member roadside jump at about $50 to $120, so a pack pays for itself after one or two calls.

Have NOCO or GOOLOO jump starters been recalled?

We found no CPSC recall for either brand on October 30, 2026, though we could read only the first page of the CPSC list. CPSC did warn about other brands in 2026, so check any pack on Recall Check and cpsc.gov.
