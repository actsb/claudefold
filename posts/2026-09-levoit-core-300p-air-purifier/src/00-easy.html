What do owners say after months with it?

Across more than 108,000 Amazon reviews at a 4.7 average (Gizmodo, September 2026) the pattern is consistent: people love how quiet and small it is, and the complaints are about the filter light and the filter bill, not about whether it cleans.

What they praise

  • Sleep mode is the nightly setting. Owners describe it as barely audible from the bed. Levoit's spec is 24 dB; independent meters in a real room read 28 dB (TechGearLab) to 37 dB (HouseFresh), which is still the quiet end of a library.
  • It fits anywhere. About 14.5 inches tall and 8.5 inches across (Wirecutter's description), so it lives on a nightstand or on the floor by the door and disappears in white.
  • Smells go first. Owners in pet homes report the house smelling better within days and fewer sneezes within a couple of weeks (paraphrased from reviews surfaced through Slickdeals and consumerability.com); litter-box results are mixed, with one reviewer saying it helped without curing the problem.

What they complain about

  • The filter bill. A genuine Core 300-RF runs about $17–$27 (Amazon's listing and deal threads, 2025–2026) every 6–8 months; HouseFresh puts the all-in running cost at about $109 a year with two filters and the fan on max around the clock. It is the most repeated downside in professional reviews as well.
  • The reset light. The Check Filter light is a timer, not a sensor, so it stays on after a filter change until you hold the button for three seconds; HouseFresh's list of common Levoit problems puts it at the top, and it spikes after a non-original filter is fitted.
  • The big number on the box. "Up to 1,073 sq ft" means one air change an hour; Levoit's own spec and every independent tester put real coverage at 219–222 sq ft. Buyers who expected a whole-floor cleaner say so in the reviews.

The questions people ask before buying

  • Is it really HEPA? Levoit dropped the words "True HEPA" and "H13" for the Core line in August 2023 after a National Advertising Division challenge brought by Dyson, and its parent company settled a class action over the claims for $15 million in 2026 without admitting wrongdoing (claims close November 3, 2026). Independent labs still measure strong particle removal: HouseFresh took a test room's PM1 to zero in 54 minutes, TechGearLab reached 99% smoke removal in 46 minutes.
  • What room size, honestly? 219 sq ft at 4.8 air changes an hour, per Levoit's spec and the AHAM-verified CADR of 141 CFM for smoke; the "1,073 sq ft" is the same unit doing one air change an hour.
  • Can it run 24/7? Yes, and Levoit recommends it. HouseFresh measured 35.5 W at top speed, so a year on max is roughly 311 kWh, about $57 at the September 2026 US average of 18.3 cents per kWh (electricchoice.com), and a fraction of that on Sleep mode.

How do you use the Levoit Core 300-P right?

Happy owners run it on high with the door shut while they are out and on Sleep at night; the returns come from people who ran it with the filter still wrapped, pushed it into a corner, or expected it to clean the whole floor.

  1. Take the plastic off the filter. Twist the base cover off, pull the cylinder out, strip the bag, seat it, twist the cover back. Levoit's own filter page says to remove the packaging before running the unit; a wrapped filter is the usual reason a new purifier rattles and moves no air.
  2. Give it 15 inches on every side. The manual asks for 15 in (38 cm) of clearance and nothing that blocks airflow, such as curtains. It breathes in through the perforated lower shell and out the top, so a corner or a shelf starves it.
  3. One unit, one room, door closed. Levoit's spec is 219 sq ft at about 4.8 air changes an hour, and the EPA's smoke rule of thumb (a CADR of at least two-thirds of the floor area) lands at roughly the same 210 sq ft. Put it where you spend the most hours, usually the bedroom, and keep the door shut, which is also Oregon State Extension's advice for smoke days.
  4. High when you are out, Sleep when you are in bed. Speed 3 cleans fastest; Sleep mode runs the lowest fan speed and dims the panel (the manual), and a press of the display button turns the light off. Levoit's spec is 24 dB in Sleep; testers measure 28–37 dB, a library rather than a fan.
  5. Vacuum the outside every month or two. The mesh pre-filter is the outer layer of the cylinder, not a separate sleeve; Levoit's maintenance guide says a gentle pass with a vacuum brush every 1–3 months keeps airflow up. The HEPA-grade and carbon layers cannot be washed.
  6. At 6–8 months, swap and hold for three seconds. When the Check Filter light comes on, fit a genuine Core 300-RF and hold the button until the light goes out. The light is a timer, so it comes on whether or not the filter looks dirty; with pets, smoke or a 24/7 schedule, Levoit says to change sooner.

What do the experts say?

Every independent tester lands on the same verdict, excellent value for a small room with the filter cost and the coverage claim as the caveats, and their coverage numbers agree with each other: about 220 sq ft, not 1,073.

  • Wirecutter (The New York Times) (guide by Tim Heffernan, continuously updated): the Core 300 is its pick for small spaces of about 200 sq ft or less; in its test the unit cut particulates by more than 97% on high in 30 minutes in a 135 sq ft office, and it notes that running it around the clock on medium costs about $300 in electricity over five years plus about $180 for seven filters.
  • HouseFresh (Danny Ashton, updated 2025): in a 728 cu ft test room the Core 300 took PM1 to zero in 54 minutes; measured 37.1 dB in Sleep and 50.2 dB on max, 35.5 W at top speed; "still one of the best value small air purifiers," though HouseFresh would pay a little more for the 300S's sensor and washable pre-filter.
  • TechGearLab (Core 300-P test): 63% of PM2.5 removed within 10 minutes in a 200 sq ft space and 99% by 46 minutes; Sleep mode measured at 28 dB; it states plainly that the unit does not have a True HEPA filter and still ranks its cleaning top-tier.
  • Modern Castle (three objective tests): PM2.5 down to 0.2 after an hour, no ozone detected, 43–66 dB measured at 3 ft, about 4 dB louder than its testing average on max.
  • Consumer Reports lists the Core 300-P for a medium room up to 222 sq ft with Energy Star certification; its scores sit behind the membership paywall, so we do not quote them.
  • US EPA, indoor air filtration factsheet (revised June 2026): a purifier's smoke CADR should be at least two-thirds of the room's floor area, which puts the Core 300-P's 141 CFM at about 210 sq ft, and higher fan speeds and longer run times filter more air.
  • American College of Allergy, Asthma & Immunology: for a single room, choose a HEPA cleaner and check that its CADR matches the room size; a room unit supplements central filtration rather than replacing it.
  • Where the experts and the box disagree: nobody independent reproduces Levoit's 24 dB, and every tester uses the roughly 220 sq ft figure, not 1,073. On the HEPA label, the BBB National Programs note records that Levoit's parent discontinued the claims in 2023 without a ruling on the merits; the settlement site has the claim details for purchases made between August 2019 and August 2023.

What's the catch?

It is a subscription in disguise: over three years the filters cost more than the purifier, and the light that tells you to buy one is a timer, not a sensor.

Nothing about the Core 300-P is bad; a few things are honest trade-offs. Budget for them and it is the best $85 in the house; ignore them and it is the purifier that ends up in the closet with a red light on.

  • Filter cost. $17–$27 every 6–8 months for a genuine Core 300-RF; HouseFresh's all-in estimate is about $109 a year with two filters and the fan on max around the clock. It matters to anyone who will not set a reminder. The fix: the 2-pack, Subscribe & Save, and the deal weeks (Slickdeals threads through 2025–2026 show $17–$19).

Levoit says 50 dB on top speed; HouseFresh measured 50.2 dB and Modern Castle up to 66 dB at three feet, so run high while you are out and Sleep while you are in. And if the H13 label is what you were paying for, Levoit stopped using it in 2023; the independent test results above are the number that matters, not the label.

Quick answers

Is the Levoit Core 300-P a true HEPA air purifier?

Levoit no longer uses the words "True HEPA" or "H13" for the Core line. It dropped them in August 2023 after a National Advertising Division challenge brought by Dyson, and its parent company VeSync settled a class action over the claims for $15 million in 2026 without admitting wrongdoing. Independent testers still measure strong particle removal, with HouseFresh clearing a test room's PM1 to zero in 54 minutes and TechGearLab reaching 99% smoke removal in 46 minutes, so it cleans like a HEPA machine; it simply is not labelled as one.

What size room does the Levoit Core 300-P actually cover?

About 219 sq ft with the air turned over 4.8 times an hour, per Levoit's own specification and its AHAM-verified smoke CADR of 141 CFM. The "up to 1,073 sq ft" on the listing is the same unit doing one air change an hour, which is not enough for allergies or smoke. One unit per room is the rule.

How loud is the Levoit Core 300-P at night?

Levoit rates Sleep mode at 24 dB. Independent meters in real rooms read 28 dB (TechGearLab) to 37 dB (HouseFresh), which owners describe as barely audible from the bed. On the top speed it is a fan you notice: about 50 dB by Levoit's spec and HouseFresh's meter, and up to 66 dB at three feet in Modern Castle's test.

How often do you replace the filter, and what does it cost?

Every 6–8 months per Levoit, sooner with pets, smoke or round-the-clock use. A genuine Core 300-RF was listed at about $27 on Amazon in September 2026, with deal prices of $17–$19 through 2025–2026 in Slickdeals threads. Budget $35–$55 a year, less on Subscribe & Save or the 2-pack.

Why does the filter light stay on after I change the filter?

Because it is a timer, not a sensor. With the unit on, press and hold the Check Filter button for about three seconds until the light goes out; if it will not clear, make sure the fan has fully stopped and hold for up to seven seconds. It is the most common support question about this purifier.

Can I use a cheaper third-party filter?

You can, and they roughly halve the filter bill. Levoit says non-genuine filters "may lead to delayed replacement, lower air quality, and damaged purifier motor," and no independent head-to-head test on this exact model surfaced in our research. Our position: genuine during the two-year warranty, and if you go generic afterwards, only one that states its filter class on the listing.

Can it run all day, and what does it cost in electricity?

Yes; Levoit recommends continuous use. HouseFresh measured 35.5 W at top speed and 24 W on medium; a year on max is about 311 kWh, roughly $57 at the September 2026 US average of 18.3 cents per kWh (electricchoice.com), and a small fraction of that on Sleep mode. Standby draw is under 0.8 W by Levoit's spec.

Core 300-P or Core 300S-P: which should I buy?

Same body, same filter, same cleaning. The 300S-P adds a PM2.5 sensor, auto mode, the VeSync app, scheduling and an auto-dimming display for about $25–$45 more (typically $109–$129 in 2026, per levoitreview.com). Buy the 300-P if you will set the speed yourself; buy the S if you want it to react to cooking or a smoky day on its own.

Does it help with wildfire smoke and pet smells?

Smoke particles, yes: 141 CFM of smoke CADR is adequate for one closed room of about 210 sq ft by the EPA's rule of thumb, run on high with the door shut. Smells are the weaker suit; the Original filter carries about 45 g of activated carbon (TechGearLab), and for pet or smoke odors Levoit sells the Pet Allergy and Smoke Remover filters, which review sites put at roughly double the carbon.

Is the Levoit Core 300-P worth it over a Dyson?

For cleaning a bedroom, yes, at roughly a fifth of the price. The Dyson Purifier Cool TP07 lists at $499 and has sold for $349; it is a fan and a purifier in one, tested on Dyson's own protocol rather than the AHAM CADR standard, and it takes Dyson's own filters only. The Core 300-P at $80–$100 with a $20 filter does the one job for one room and has 108,000 reviews behind it.
