What they praise

What they complain about

  • Charge loss: NOCO and Family Handyman say it holds charge for up to a year, but owner and blog reports say three to six months. One summary mentions capacity fading at 12 to 18 months.

How do you use the NOCO GB40 right?

The habit that separates happy owners from returns is checking the charge lights every few months.

Steps follow NOCO's GB40 instructions as seen in search snippets; the step people skip is the recharge.
  1. Turn off the loads. Lights, radio and A/C draw current the starter would rather send to the engine.
  2. Red to positive, black to negative or bare metal. A solid red error light means the clamps are reversed, and the unit will not run.
  3. Press Power and wait for the white Boost light. That means it has detected the battery and is ready.
  4. Start the car. Most start at once; some need up to 30 seconds. If not, wait 20 to 30 seconds and retry, and do not exceed five jumps in 15 minutes.
  5. Remove the clamps and recharge. Use the USB cable, and keep the pack above half charge.

A blinking error light means the pack is too hot; let it cool. The manual override (hold the button three seconds) is for batteries below about 2 volts, and it disables spark-proof and reverse-polarity protection, so use it only when you are sure the clamps are right.

A jump starter that is flat is a paperweight, and lithium packs lose capacity.

Quick answers

Is the NOCO GB40 worth it?

For a typical car or SUV, most reviewers and owners say yes. The value case rests on its safety features, size and sale prices around $80 to $90. It is a poor fit for large diesels.

What engines can the GB40 start?

NOCO's spec is gas engines up to 6.0L and diesels up to 3.0L. The GB70 is listed for 8.0L gas and 6.0L diesel.

How many starts do I get per charge?

NOCO claims up to 20 starts per full charge. Real-world numbers will be lower on bigger engines and cold days.

How often should I recharge it?

Sources conflict: NOCO and Family Handyman say it holds charge up to a year, while owner reports say three to six months. Check the lights every few months and top up.

Does it come with a wall charger?

Reports say no. It charges by USB, about three hours at 2A per the instructions as summarised, so keep a USB-A charger on hand.

Can it jump a completely dead battery?

Below about 2 volts the unit may not detect the battery. The manual override (hold the button three seconds) can force it, but it disables the spark and reverse-polarity protection.

What do the red and blinking lights mean?

A solid red error light means the clamps are reversed. A blinking one means the pack is too hot and needs to cool.

Does it work in cold weather?

Owner reports are mixed: one summary mentions a failure at 10°F and another a start in bitter cold. Keep it charged and indoors when you can.

Is a 2000A no-name box better than the GB40?

Not necessarily. Reviewers describe the peak-amp figures on cheap boxes as marketing, and say real output can be far lower. NOCO's edge is build quality and a known warranty.

What warranty does the GB40 have?

One year limited from purchase, for the original buyer, per a warranty-guide snippet we could not open in full. Confirm on NOCO's site.
