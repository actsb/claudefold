What do owners say after months with it?

Home Depot shows 4.4 out of 5 from about 1,200 reviews and Target shows 4.61 from 3,286 ratings (both Oct 13, 2026), yet the unhappy reviews are specific: cracks and lids you cannot replace.

We read 300 Home Depot reviews of the 10-piece set (dated July 2019 to May 2026). 232 were tagged as collected through a promotion, so we set them aside and read the other 68. Cracking comes up in 29 of those 68. Unhappy owners write more often than happy ones, so treat that as a warning, not a failure rate. Target's 2026 reviews we could open (eight, July to September) were mostly positive and mentioned no cracking.

What they praise

  • The seal. Owners at Target and Home Depot keep returning to lids that stay shut in lunch bags and tote bags.
  • Clear sides. Reviewers like seeing leftovers without opening the box.
  • Dry goods and pantry use. A Home Depot reviewer (Feb 2026) bought two sets to keep bugs out of the pantry in Florida, and another (Feb 2026) reported no staining after a couple of years.

What they complain about

  • Cracks. Home Depot reviewers report cracked lids or bodies after about six months (Feb 2025), within a year with top-rack washing (Apr 2025), and after four years (Nov 2025). One long-term owner (Oct 2024) said the first crack took five years.
  • No replacement lids. Ten of the 68 mention replacements, and one reviewer (Apr 2025) says the lids crack and cannot be replaced. We could not open Rubbermaid's own store to check.
  • Stains and cloudiness. Ten reviews mention stains, mostly to say there were none; two mention cloudy or scratched plastic. A 3-star Target reviewer (Aug 2025) saw a spaghetti-sauce stain and pitting on a 12-piece container after one microwave use.

The questions people ask before buying

  • Is a 10-piece set ten containers? No. Target's listing and ATK both describe five containers and five lids: one 9.6-cup, two 3.2-cup and two 1.3-cup.
  • Can I microwave it? Target's listing says it is microwave-safe with the lid latches opened. America's Test Kitchen would rather you did not microwave plastic at all, so use it for reheating only and never for hot cooking.
  • Is it dishwasher-safe? Target's listing allows the top rack. ATK advises hand-washing, and several cracking reports in Home Depot reviews mention dishwasher use, so hand-washing is the safer habit.

How do you use the Rubbermaid Brilliance right?

Cool the food, close both latches, and wash by hand.

  1. Count what is in the box. A 10-piece set is five containers and five lids, so plan your fridge for five meals, not ten.
  2. Wash before first use. The gasket is attached and soft enough to clean with a sponge without removing it (ATK).
  3. Cool food first. ATK says to let food reach room temperature before sealing it in plastic.
  4. Close both latches. Two latches clip over the gasket; ATK's shaken, water-filled test container let no drops out.
  5. Open the latches to microwave. Target's listing says to open the vented latches. ATK recommends not microwaving plastic, so reheat only.
  6. Hand-wash if you want it to last. Target allows the top rack, but ATK says three to five years is typical for well-cared-for plastic (Michael Tunick, Drexel University).

What do the experts say?

What's the catch?

The plastic can crack, and replacement lids are hard to find.

The seal is the strength and the plastic is the weakness. Cracks in the reviews we read appear after months to years, and ATK itself puts well-cared-for plastic at three to five years. Retailer pages also disagree about the warranty (Target shows one year on one listing), and Rubbermaid's own warranty page was blocked to us, so we do not state its terms.

  • Who it hurts: people who microwave and dishwasher daily.
  • The fix: hand-wash, keep food cool before sealing, and keep your receipt.
  • The alternative: the Brilliance Glass set, which ATK also ranked first among glass sets, at a higher price.

Quick answers

Is the Rubbermaid Brilliance really leak-proof?

In America's Test Kitchen's December 2025 test a large container filled with dyed water and shaken hard let no drops escape. Owners report leaks once a lid or body cracks.

What is the difference between 10-piece and 12-piece sets?

The 10-piece is five containers and five lids. The 12-piece is six of each (Target, Oct 2026). Piece counts include lids.

How long does it last?

ATK cites Drexel food scientist Michael Tunick for three to five years of well-cared-for plastic. Home Depot reviewers report cracks from about six months to five years.

Can you buy replacement lids?

Reviewers say they cannot find them. We could not open Rubbermaid's store to confirm, so check before relying on it.

Does it stain?

Ten of the 68 non-promotional Home Depot reviews mention stains, mostly to say there were none. A 3-star Target reviewer reported a spaghetti-sauce stain on a 12-piece container.

Is it freezer-safe?

Target's listing says so. ATK froze a water-filled container overnight and dropped it from about 2.5 feet in its test.

What plastic is it made of?

ATK describes the set as Tritan plastic. Its page also notes a 2023 study of microplastics moving from containers into food, which is why it advises against heat.

Is glass better?

Glass handles heat and dishwashers better and does not hold odors, but it is heavy and can shatter. ATK picked the Brilliance Glass 9-piece as its glass winner.

What does it cost?

Dated sale posts put the 10-piece near $17 at Walmart (Dec 2025). Larger sets cost less per piece. The button shows today's price.

What should I check on the listing?

That it says 10 piece, Tritan and model 2183421, and that it is not the glass set or a Pantry canister.
