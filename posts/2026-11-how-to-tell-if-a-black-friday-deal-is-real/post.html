How do you tell if a Black Friday deal is real?

Compare today's price with what several stores actually charged for the same model in recent months, not with the price the store crossed out.

The check below takes about five minutes on a phone. It uses only things anyone can see: the model number, three store pages, a price-history chart and the store's own policy page. We use the same steps for every entry on our Price Check page.

The five-minute check, built from the FTC's pricing guides, the stores' own reference-price pages (November 2026) and Consumers' Checkbook's 2025 study. The step people skip is copying the full model number.
  1. Ignore the crossed-out price. Each store sets its own rule for what "was", "reg" or "compare at" means (the table below). It is a claim by the seller, not a fact about the market.
  2. Copy the full model number. Colours, bundles, refurbished units, older generations and store-only versions all have their own numbers, and their own prices. If the number on the deal doesn't match the one you researched, you are comparing different products.
  3. Check three stores. The maker's own store, one big-box store and one more. Kevin Brasler of Consumers' Checkbook told WTOP that "just doing a quick internet search will show you what other retailers are selling that item for" (November 21, 2025). The price most of them charge is the real regular price.
  4. Look at the history. Google's price insights label a price as low, typical or high against sellers across the web, and Chrome on a desktop shows up to 90 days of history on many store pages. If today is only "typical", it isn't a Black Friday deal.
  5. Read the fine print. "Log in to save 15%", a store card, Kohl's Cash, a bundle or a gift card is a condition, not a lower price. Work out what you pay at the till.
  6. Buy where a later drop is paid back. Target matches its own lower price until December 24, Best Buy matches its own drops on most items bought from October 30 to December 31, and Costco adjusts for 30 days. Walmart.com doesn't adjust after you buy, so buy there only at the low.

Printable checklist: screenshot this before you shop

  • ☐ My number for this item is $____ (written before the sale).
  • ☐ The model number on the deal matches mine, letter for letter.
  • ☐ Price at the maker's store: $____ · at store 2: $____ · at store 3: $____
  • ☐ Price history says "low", or today is under the lowest price since October.
  • ☐ What I pay at checkout, without sign-in tricks, cards or store cash: $____
  • ☐ This store pays back a later drop (Target, Best Buy, Costco), or this is the low (Walmart).
  • ☐ Sold by the store or the maker, not an unknown marketplace seller.
  • ☐ Receipt saved, return date noted.

What do "was", "reg" and "compare at" really mean at each store?

At the stores that publish a rule, "was" usually means a median of their own recent prices; the vaguer labels can mean almost anything.

A 90-day median sounds fair, and it is better than nothing. But it measures the store against itself. If a store sold an item at a high price for three months, its "was" is honest by its own rule and still higher than what three other stores charged. That is why step 3 matters more than step 1.

How often are sale prices fake?

Often: every study we found says somewhere between a third and most "deals" were matched or beaten at another time.

  • Consumers' Checkbook, "Sale Fail" (November 13, 2025): a nonprofit consumer group checked more than 25 items at each of 25 national chains every week for 24 weeks from February 2025. 21 of the 25 showed sale prices more than half the time, up from six chains in its 2018 study. Only Apple, Costco and Dell consistently ran real discounts; Walmart was borderline, with items on sale 48% of the time. The worst offenders named were Bass Pro Shops, Bed Bath & Beyond, Dick's, Foot Locker, Gap, JCPenney, Michaels, Nordstrom, Old Navy and Wayfair.
  • Visualping (updated November 28, 2025): a company that sells price-tracking tools followed 2,620 products daily from October 1 to December 1, 2025, and found 33% of Black Friday prices were higher than the lowest price since October 1, by 37% on average. Luggage was worst (61%), microwaves next (50%). Its stores include Canadian ones, so read it as North American.
  • WalletHub (November 2025, as syndicated by FOX 5 DC): about 36% of roughly 3,100 Black Friday ad prices offered no saving against the same items' prices in the weeks before. We read this one from a news summary only.
  • Which? (UK, November 25, 2025): 83% of 175 Black Friday deals were the same price or cheaper at least once in the year around them. A British study, but the tactics travel.

Adobe's 2026 forecast is a useful sanity check: it expects peak discounts of 18% for appliances, 23% for TVs and computers and 30% for electronics this season (Adobe, September 28, 2026). Our inference, not Adobe's: when a tag says 60% or 70% off a mixer or a vacuum, the reference price is more likely to be the story than the discount.

Which model-number traps catch people?

The deal on the page is often for a different product: a bundle, an older generation, a store-only version or a refurbished unit.

  • Black Friday TV models. Consumer Reports calls them "derivative" models: sets made for the sale that look like the reviewed model but can have fewer HDMI ports, a lower refresh rate or a different panel (as explained by Money Crashers and BGR, 2025). If a model number turns up only in Black Friday ads and nowhere in reviews, assume it is one.
  • Club and store versions. Consumer Reports' 2025 TV roundup noted a Samsung sold at Costco under a different model number from the Best Buy version. Costco's "Artisan" KitchenAid is the Artisan Plus KSM50P3PXSO, not the KSM150.
  • Bundle codes. Kohl's sells the KSM150FB and KSM150FE bundles and KitchenAid sells the KSM150TBCU: the same mixer with extras, under codes you can't price against a plain KSM150.
  • Older generations. AirPods Pro 2 for AirPods Pro 3; an F7N or a front-only F7NS for the F7NP; a Duo Plus, Rio or "V5 refurbished" unit for the Duo.
  • Bigger is not always dearer. On Black Friday 2025 Target had KitchenAid's 5.5-quart bowl-lift KSM55 at $254.99, below the 5-quart Artisan at $297.49 (Slickdeals, November 30, 2025). Compare models, not percentages.

Are store cash, sign-in and bundle deals real discounts?

Only if you would spend the store cash anyway, can meet the condition, and wanted the extras; otherwise count only what you pay at checkout.

Kohl's 2025 Black Friday deal on the KitchenAid Artisan is the textbook case: $350 shipped, plus $105 in Kohl's Cash you could spend only from November 30 to December 10, plus about $25 in rewards only with a Kohl's card (Slickdeals, November 23, 2025). The money that left your account was $350. The "$245 net" existed only if you went back and spent $105 more at Kohl's inside ten days. This year Kohl's gives $15 in Kohl's Cash for every $50 spent during Black Friday Week, November 22–28 (Kohl's, November 2026).

Other conditions to price in: Target's "log in to save 15% in cart" (the source of last year's $297.49 mixer), paid early access for Walmart+ and Target Circle 360 members, and doorbusters in tiny quantities, like Walmart's 2025 Kraft "Mac Friday" box that shoppers said was gone in seconds (Fox Business, November 2025). Gift-card deals are the maker's favourite: Apple's 2025 Black Friday offer was a gift card with the AirPods at full price. None of these are scams. They just aren't the price on the tag.

Which price-history tools can you trust?

Use them to see the range, not as the last word: each one sees only some stores, for a limited time.

  • Google's price insights label a price as low, typical or high, using "info from sellers across the web", and can flag a 30-day low. Google says the feature doesn't predict future prices and is US-only (Google Help, read November 20, 2026). Google publishes no formula, and we found no independent accuracy test.
  • Chrome's Shopping insights (desktop) show a typical price range and up to 90 days of history on many store pages, with a price-drop alert (9to5Google, November 7, 2023). Merchants can opt out of having their prices shown, so a missing chart means nothing.
  • Amazon's own price history now sits on product pages. EcomCrew reported it was extended to 365 days on June 30, 2026. It shows one store's prices; step 3 still applies.
  • Coupon extensions are not price checkers. After a YouTube investigation in December 2024, creators sued PayPal's Honey over affiliate commissions; the main suit was dismissed with leave to amend in November 2025 and refiled in January 2026 (Bloomberg Law). Capital One agreed to pay about $4 million to settle a similar suit over its shopping extension (American Banker, December 2025). Neither case is about price accuracy, but both are a reason to check prices yourself.

Target, Best Buy, Costco and Kohl's adjust to their own lower price for a while; Walmart.com doesn't, and almost nobody matches other stores during Black Friday week.

Is a fake "was" price illegal?

Often, on paper: federal guides and some state laws require a former price to be real, but enforcement is rare, so the check is on you.

  • Federal. The FTC's Guides Against Deceptive Pricing say a former price is legitimate only if it is "the actual, bona fide price at which the article was offered to the public on a regular basis for a reasonably substantial period of time" (16 CFR 233.1). The same rule notes a former price "is not necessarily fictitious merely because no sales at the advertised price were made", which is why "offered" prices are so easy to stage.
  • California. Business and Professions Code section 17501 bars advertising a former price unless it was the prevailing market price in the three months before the ad, or the ad states when it was (statute text).
  • Massachusetts counts a former price as genuine if it was offered for the 14 days before the ad or on at least 28 of the previous 90 days (940 CMR 6.05, as summarized by Cornell LII).
  • Enforcement. Monica Vaca, a former deputy director of the FTC's consumer protection bureau, told InvestigateTV that inflating a price before a sale violates the FTC Act, but that since 2021 the agency "can't get money back" for shoppers (Hawaii News Now / InvestigateTV, July 27, 2026). Private class actions fill some of the gap: JCPenney agreed to a $50 million settlement over price comparisons in 2015 (JCK), and suits over Best Buy's "Comp. Value" were filed in 2025 and 2026 (complaints published by Truth in Advertising; allegations only).

What do the experts say?

Consumer advocates agree: the crossed-out price is an anchor, and the only defence is to know the real price before you see the banner.

  • Kevin Brasler, Consumers' Checkbook, on WTOP (November 21, 2025): retailers are "rarely, if ever, charging those list or regular prices", so focus on the actual cost of the item rather than the supposed savings.
  • Samantha Gordon, Consumer Reports deals editor, on CBS Philadelphia (updated November 3, 2025): stores raise the "original" price before a sale, so you can end up paying what you would have paid two weeks earlier. She recommends price-history trackers and reading price-match and refund policies.
  • Brasler again, on CBS Philadelphia (November 19, 2025): the same deals keep coming back, so "there's no pressure, there's no rush", and Costco "very infrequently discounts stuff that it's selling".
  • NBC Select's experts (updated December 1, 2025): economist Erik Guzik of the University of Montana said 20–30% off may be "quite good" this year, especially for tariff-hit goods; RetailMeNot's Stephanie Carls said if it's a good price and the exact model you want, take it.
  • Reena Sewraz, Which? (November 25, 2025): "for the majority of shoppers, Black Friday is a false economy." A stronger line than the US data supports, but the direction is the same.

Quick answers

How can I tell if a Black Friday deal is real?

Ignore the crossed-out price, copy the full model number, and check what the maker's store and two other stores charge for that exact model. Then look at a price-history chart. If today's price is under what they charged in recent months, it's a real deal.

What does "was price" mean at Target and Walmart?

Both say it is a 90-day median of their own prices, leaving out sales and clearance (Target Help and Walmart's comparison-price rules, read November 20, 2026). It shows what that store charged, not what the market charged.

What is Best Buy's "Comp. Value"?

Best Buy says it can be based on prices at which the product or a comparable item was, or will be, offered by Best Buy, its marketplace sellers, makers or other retailers. It is not proof the product ever sold at that price.

Which stores have honest sale prices?

In Consumers' Checkbook's 2025 study of 25 chains, only Apple, Costco and Dell consistently ran legitimate discounts, and Walmart was borderline. The worst included Bass Pro Shops, Dick's, Gap, JCPenney, Michaels, Nordstrom, Old Navy and Wayfair.

Are fake sale prices illegal?

The FTC's pricing guides say a former price must be a bona fide price offered for a reasonably substantial period, and California requires it to be the prevailing price in the previous three months. Enforcement is rare, so check prices yourself.

Are AirPods Pro 3 a good deal at $179.99?

Yes. Target's "reg $249.99" is padded (Apple charges $249), but $179.99 on November 20, 2026 is $40 under the 2025 holiday low of $219.99 and $10 above the lowest price we found at a store other than Amazon, $169.99 at Walmart in June 2026.

Is the Walmart Little Green the same as the 1400B?

Not the $75 one. The Little Green deals at Walmart in October 2026 were the 3369, which Bissell lists as a similar model, not the same one. The 1400B was $94.99 at Bissell on November 20, 2026.

Is Kohl's Cash a real discount?

Only if you would spend it anyway. It is money you must spend at Kohl's later, inside a short window: the 2025 KitchenAid deal paid $105 in Kohl's Cash usable only from November 30 to December 10. Count what you pay at checkout.

Should I buy now or wait for Black Friday?

Buy now if today's price is under your number and the store pays back later drops (Target until December 24, Best Buy on most items bought October 30 to December 31, Costco for 30 days). At Walmart.com, which doesn't adjust, buy only at the low.

Are price-history tools accurate?

They are useful but partial. Google's price insights cover sellers across the web but publish no formula, Chrome shows up to 90 days and merchants can opt out, and a store's own chart shows only that store. Use them with a three-store check.

The bottom line: the crossed-out price is the seller's story, so check the price itself

Most "was" prices are a store measuring itself against itself, and in Checkbook's 2025 study most chains were "on sale" most of the time.
