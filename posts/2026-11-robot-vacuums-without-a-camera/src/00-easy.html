How does a robot vacuum see without a camera?

With a spinning laser that measures distance (LiDAR), sometimes helped by a projected infrared pattern (structured light or a line laser); neither takes a picture you could look at, which is the point and also the limit.

Prof. H teaches robotics, so the engineering first. A LiDAR turret, which makers also call LDS, spins a laser rangefinder and builds the floor plan from distances to walls and furniture. It works in the dark, but it scans at the height of the turret, so it sees a sofa and misses a sock. RTINGS sorts obstacle sensors into four kinds: bump sensors, which it says are "arguably not avoidance" because the robot has already hit something, light-based sensors, 3D structured light and cameras (RTINGS obstacle avoidance test). Structured light projects an infrared pattern ahead and reads how it bends, so the robot knows something is there without knowing what it is. A camera robot adds an RGB camera and an AI model that recognises the object: a cord, a shoe, pet waste.

One honest caveat. A structured-light or line-laser module still has a small image sensor that reads where the infrared pattern lands; an Ecovacs T30S manual describes its sensor collecting images with a monocular camera to read the laser line. So "no RGB camera" is the accurate claim for the Q10 S5+, the Deebot and the eufy, not "no image sensor at all". We found no maker statement on whether those infrared frames are kept, so we do not claim they are never stored. The two pure-LiDAR robots here, the Roborock Q7 M5+ and the Tapo RV30 Max Plus, have only a laser and a bumper in front; iRobot's support page lists every Roomba with a front camera and says all models not listed, the 205 among them, do not have one (iRobot support). TechRadar's review text mentions a camera on the 205; we follow iRobot's own list.

LiDAR is not risk-free either. In 2020, researchers from the National University of Singapore and the University of Maryland turned a hacked robot vacuum's LiDAR into a crude microphone ("LidarPhone", ACM SenSys 2020), but the attacker had to control the robot first. Mozilla's Privacy Not Included still calls LiDAR the safer bet than cameras.

What do you give up without a camera?

The ability to tell objects apart: camera-free robots run into cords, socks and rug tassels, and none here promises to avoid pet waste, so you tidy before each run and keep the robot away from a pet accident.

The clearest number comes from CNET's lab (best robot vacuums of 2026, Ajay Kumar, November 13, 2026): the camera-free Roomba 205 Combo "only avoided one of six objects", while the Roomba Combo 10 Max, which has cameras, was the only robot to dodge all six. Vacuum Wars scored the Roborock Q10 S5's structured-light avoidance 1.25 against a 3.39 average. RTINGS says the Q7 M5 has no real-time obstacle avoidance and gets stuck on rugs, cords and clutter. The Verge (Jennifer Pattison Tuohy, January 13, 2026) says of its budget pick, the Tapo, that there is no AI obstacle detection, "so cables and socks will trip it up".

Pet waste is the fear behind most of these questions, and it is fair. iRobot backs its camera Roomba Max 705 with a pet-owner promise: a replacement if it fails to avoid solid pet waste in the first year, with its camera feature on (iRobot pet promise). No camera-free robot comes with a promise like that. Even cameras are not perfect: RTINGS notes some camera models still struggle with power cords, and TechRadar saw the Dreame X50 Ultra miss short wires.

Before you buy: settings and privacy

Pick up the floor, draw no-go zones, leave the data-sharing programs off, use a strong app password and factory-reset the robot before you sell it.

  1. Pick up cords, socks and toys before each run. A LiDAR turret maps walls and furniture; Vacuum Wars and The Verge both say to tidy first on these robots.
  2. Draw no-go zones around desk cables, pet bowls and tasselled rugs. Tapo's FAQ puts them under Edit Map, Virtual Wall/No-Go Zones; iRobot's under Smart Map, Keep Out Zone. eufy warns a zone can drop out when the room changes and suggests magnetic strips near a fireplace.
  3. Leave the data programs off. eufy says obstacle photos are not uploaded unless you join its User Experience Program; ABC reported in October 2024 that Ecovacs' product improvement program collects photos and voice from camera models. On a camera-free robot there are fewer photos to share, but the map still goes to the cloud.
  4. Use a long, unique password for the app and keep the firmware updated. Korea's 2025 test found weak app logins at two brands. Under the FCC's July 2026 rule on foreign-made robots, models already approved keep getting updates through at least January 1, 2029 (Vacuum Wars, September 9, 2026).
  5. Do not schedule a run while a puppy or a sick pet is home alone. None of these robots can see pet waste.
  6. Remove the robot from your account and factory-reset it before selling. The DEF CON 2024 researchers found Ecovacs data and login tokens that survived account deletion.

If you already own a camera robot and want it to stop taking pictures: Roborock says you can turn off AI obstacle recognition in its app, and iRobot lets you opt out of Obstacle Image Review under Account, Privacy (but not turn off detection on the Plus 500 and Max 700). For a local-only robot with no maker cloud, the open-source Valetudo project supports some rooted Roborock and Dreame models; none of our six picks is on its list we checked, and rooting can void the warranty.

Quick answers

Do all robot vacuums have cameras?

No. LiDAR robots such as the Roborock Q7 M5+, Q10 S5+, Tapo RV30 Max Plus and Roomba 205 map with a laser and have no RGB camera (maker pages, November 23, 2026). Most premium robots now add one for obstacle recognition.

Which robot vacuum has no camera?

Our first pick is the Roborock Q10 S5+, which uses LiDAR and infrared structured light, at $229.99 at Roborock on November 23, 2026. The Q7 M5+, Tapo RV30 Max Plus, Roomba 205, Deebot N30 Pro Omni and eufy Omni C28 also have no RGB camera.

Can a LiDAR robot vacuum see you?

Not as a picture. LiDAR measures distances to build a floor plan. Researchers showed in 2020 that a hacked robot's LiDAR could pick up sound, but the attacker had to control the robot first (LidarPhone, ACM SenSys 2020).

Does structured light count as a camera?

Not an RGB camera, but the module has a small infrared sensor that reads the projected pattern; an Ecovacs manual calls it a monocular camera. It sees shapes, not a photo of your room.

Do robot vacuums without a camera avoid dog poop?

Not reliably. RTINGS says the Q7 M5 can run over pet waste, and no camera-free robot here promises to avoid it. iRobot offers a pet-waste promise only on its camera Roomba Max 705.

Is LiDAR or a camera better for a robot vacuum?

LiDAR maps well in the dark and takes no photos; a camera recognises cords, socks and pet waste. In CNET's November 2026 lab, a LiDAR Roomba avoided one of six objects and a camera Roomba six of six.

Can I turn off the camera on my robot vacuum?

On some. Roborock says turning off AI obstacle recognition leaves the camera inactive; iRobot lets you opt out of Obstacle Image Review but says detection cannot be turned off on the Plus 500 and Max 700 series.

Do robot vacuum companies store photos of my home?

Roborock says its cloud does not store obstacle images; iRobot says images not shared with it are deleted from its servers after 30 days; eufy says photos are not uploaded unless you join its User Experience Program (maker pages, 2026).

Is iRobot still in business in 2026?

Yes. Picea completed its purchase of iRobot out of Chapter 11 on January 23, 2026; US customer data moved to a US subsidiary, iRobot Safe, and iRobot said it expected no disruption to app or support.

About $462 to $597 for the Roborock Q7 M5+ with parts, about $510 to $530 for the Tapo RV30 Max Plus and about $705 to $710 for the mop-dock Deebot and eufy (store and parts prices, November 23, 2026).
