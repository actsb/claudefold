What do owners say after months with it?

The reviews we read lean strongly positive, about 4.6 stars from roughly 150,000 Amazon ratings in an undated search summary, with the handle as the recurring complaint.

What they praise

  • The foot-pedal wringer: no bending, and floors left only lightly damp.
  • The triangular head, which reaches corners and edges.
  • Streak-free hardwood when the head is spun well, according to reviewers.

What they complain about

  • The handle is the most-cited weakness: reports of it separating at the connector, not staying tight, or breaking within weeks.
  • The pedal and spin mechanism: some owners report a snapped plastic pedal arm or a basket that stops spinning. Replacement parts are sold by third parties.
  • Smell: a musty head, which owners tie to leaving it damp in the bucket. We did not find widespread complaints about streaks or about genuine heads wearing out; fraying reports mostly named generic heads.

One review dated March 2026 said the newest version felt thinner. That is a single review, so treat it as a flag, not a trend. We could not read Reddit or forum threads for this guide.

How do you use the O-Cedar spin mop right?

Pump the pedal enough: O-Cedar says 5 to 7 pumps, and a too-wet head is the usual cause of streaks on hardwood.

  1. Assemble and lock the handle. Twist the sections together and press the head onto the frame until it clicks. A loose connector is where owners report trouble.
  2. Fill the bucket. Water up to the line, with floor cleaner if you like. Sealed floors only.
  3. Dunk, then pump. Put the head in the basket and use the pedal 5 to 7 times (O-Cedar). Bob Vila reported needing more than 40 wrings on a hardwood test to get the head dry, so go by feel.
  4. Step on the head to remove it. You do not have to touch the dirty head.
  5. Wash it. Machine or hand wash, no bleach or fabric softener. Sources disagree on the temperature (warm, up to 86°F, or cold), so check your manual.
  6. Dry it flat or hanging. A head left damp in the bucket is the usual cause of the smell owners describe. O-Cedar suggests replacing heads about every 3 months.

What's the catch?

The plastic parts: the handle most often, and the pedal mechanism too.

Owners praise the design but report that the handle can loosen, separate or break, and some report a snapped pedal arm. Most owners do not report this, and we cannot say how many do. The mop also does not scrub: it is a wet mop, and dried-on mess needs a second pass.

  • A broken handle or pedal matters most if you mop often. Keep your receipt and ask O-Cedar about parts; its warranty terms are unconfirmed.
  • Pedal wringing leaves floors lightly damp, which matters on hardwood. Wring more, or go over it with a dry cloth.
  • If you want suction, scrubbing and no bucket, a floor washer is a different and pricier tool.
