What does each smart speaker record?

All of them listen for a wake word, and all of them except the Sonos Era 100 SL send some voice data to a cloud when you speak; the difference is what is kept and for how long.

Amazon Echo: kept until you change it

Amazon's privacy FAQ (undated, read November 6, 2026) says you can review and delete recordings under More, then Alexa Privacy in the app, or at amazon.com/alexaprivacysettings. You can auto-delete after 3 or 18 months, or choose not to save recordings. If you choose not to save, voice recordings are deleted after processing, but text transcripts are kept for 30 days after your last interaction in a chat, and Voice ID stops working. The microphone button disconnects power to the microphones. A common worry is stale: in March 2025 Amazon removed "Do Not Send Voice Recordings", but TechRadar (March 18, 2025) reported it affected exactly three models (Echo Dot 4th gen, Echo Show 10 and Echo Show 15), not every Echo. Prof H Lab covers the exit and the ads: how to turn off Alexa+ and Alexa+ ads on the Echo Show.

Apple HomePod mini: audio not stored unless you opt in

Apple's Siri privacy page (dated October 26, 2026) says "Your audio data is not stored by Apple unless you opt in to Improve Siri and Dictation"; if you opt in, Apple may keep it for up to two years. Apple's HomePod guide says request history is tied to a random identifier for 6 months, and you delete it in the Home app under Siri History, Delete Siri History. We found no physical mute switch on the HomePod mini; you turn off listening by voice or in the Home app (UNVERIFIED that none exists on the 2nd generation).

Google Home Speaker: 18 months if Home History is on

Google's Gemini for Home page says that by default activity saves for 18 months when Voice Match and Home History are on; you can choose 3, 18 or 36 months auto-delete and delete at myactivity.google.com/product/home. Using your recordings to improve services, including training generative AI, is a separate setting that is off by default. The speaker has a physical mute toggle on the bottom (Google Store). For the step-by-step on older Nest devices, see Prof H Lab's guide to turning off Gemini on Google Home.

Sonos: nothing saved on the SL, a choice on the Era 100

Sonos's privacy statement (September 2026) says classic Sonos Voice is processed on the device with no audio saved or sent to the cloud; the optional Sonos 27voice upgrade sends your command to Sonos cloud infrastructure for AI processing, and we did not find a retention period for it. If you use Alexa on the Era 100, the recording goes to Amazon and Sonos keeps no copy. The Era 100 has a switch on the back that disconnects power to its microphones, and the Era 100 SL, Sonos says, has no microphone. A 2020 Mozilla *Privacy Not Included entry praised the older mic-less Sonos One SL on the same point (snippet only; older product).

Does any of this change what the experts say?

Not much has been independently tested on the 2025 and 2026 models. Consumer Reports' smart speaker privacy guide (Allen St. John, updated October 2022) predates Alexa+, Gemini for Home and the HomePod mini 2, and Northeastern and Imperial College researchers (2020) found accidental activations of 1.5 to 19 times a day when TV audio played near older speakers, with recordings usually under 10 seconds and no evidence of constant recording (secondary coverage). So what we can compare is the makers' own stated defaults, not measurements.

How do you do the five-minute privacy pass?

Set the history before the first command, keep training off, and learn where the mute is.

  1. Choose the history. Alexa: More, Alexa Privacy, set auto-delete to 3 months or choose not to save (transcripts stay 30 days). Google: leave 18 months or shorten it.
  2. Keep training off. Google's "improve services with your voice and audio recordings" setting is off by default; leave it. Apple: decline Improve Siri.
  3. Find the mute. Echo: the mic button. Google Home Speaker and Era 100: a switch. HomePod mini: by voice or in the Home app.
  4. Check the plan. Alexa+ is free only with Prime; Gemini Live needs Premium.
  5. Delete old audio now and then: Echo through Alexa Privacy, Google through My Activity, Apple through Siri History.
  6. Or skip the microphone with the Era 100 SL.

What is the catch?

This guide compares documentation, not hands-on tests, and the best privacy answer is a speaker that cannot hear.

Quick answers

Is Alexa+ free with Prime?

Yes. Amazon says Alexa+ is included at no additional cost to Prime members and costs $19.99 a month otherwise (Amazon, updated September 1, 2026).

How much does Alexa+ cost without Prime over three years?

$719.64 at $19.99 a month for 36 months. Non-Prime users also get a limited free chat tier on Alexa.com and the app.

Can I go back to the old Alexa?

PCWorld reported in January 2026 that you can say "Alexa, exit Alexa+". We could not confirm it still works after the September 2026 launch, so test it on your device.

Does Alexa record everything you say?

Amazon says Echo devices record after the wake word and keep recordings until you set auto-delete (3 or 18 months) or choose not to save. A 2020 university study found accidental activations of 1.5 to 19 times a day on older speakers and no evidence of constant recording.

Can I stop Echo from sending recordings to the cloud?

Not on current models. In March 2025 Amazon removed that switch, which only three models had. You can choose not to save recordings, and the mic button cuts power to the microphones.

Does HomePod record conversations?

Apple says audio is not stored unless you opt in to Improve Siri. Requests are tied to a random identifier for 6 months; delete them in the Home app under Siri History.

HomePod mini 2 vs HomePod mini 1: is it worth it?

Cult of Mac found it better in almost every way for $149, but Siri AI is not out yet, so only classic Siri works today. The first generation is no longer on Apple's buy page.

Google Home Speaker vs Echo Dot Max: which is better?

For Google homes, the Home Speaker, at $79.99 to December 2. For Alexa homes with Prime, the Echo Dot Max. Both owners we read complain about lag or routines. Both have paid tiers: Premium and Alexa+.

Do I need a subscription for Gemini on the Google Home Speaker?

Not for basics such as music, timers and home control. Gemini Live and some features need Google Home Premium at $10 or $20 a month, according to the Google Store on November 6, 2026.

Is there a smart speaker without a microphone?

Yes: the Sonos Era 100 SL. Sonos says it has no internal microphones and no voice control, and it works with the Sonos app, AirPlay and Bluetooth.
