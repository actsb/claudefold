The 10 best portable power stations of 2026

Ranked by the job they do, because a CPAP user, a family sizing for a three-day outage and a homeowner with a well pump are shopping for three different machines. Every pick lists the list price and the sale range we've seen in 2026; almost all sell far below list most of the year, and we say which are worth waiting for October or November to buy.

Best overall Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2

IMAGE SLOT — Amazon SiteStripe → Image → Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2, then delete this box.
Price: $799 list; $499–$529 typical, lower in sale events
Capacity / output: 1,024 Wh LFP; 2,000 W continuous, 3,000 W surge
Recharge: 0–100% in 49 min (HyperFlash, 1,600 W); solar up to 600 W (~1.8 h); 12 V car
Weight: 24.9 lb
Ports: 5 AC, 2× 140 W USB-C, 15 W USB-C, USB-A, 120 W 12 V car socket
UPS / idle / warranty: 10 ms; 9 W standby; 5 years, 80% after 4,000 cycles

This is the unit most households should buy, and the reasons stack up quickly. It holds a full kilowatt-hour and pushes 2,000 W — enough to run a refrigerator and a microwave at the same time, or to start a 1/2 HP sump pump — in a box one adult carries in one hand. It recharges from empty to full in 49 minutes, a figure Guinness certified, which in a real outage means one hour of restored grid at 3 a.m. refills it completely. It idles at 9 W, the lowest in its class, so it doesn't eat itself while it waits. The 10 ms UPS keeps a router, a desktop or a CPAP running through the switchover. And it comes from the company whose customer service the owner threads praise without qualification. OutdoorGearLab names it the best-value power station; PhoneArena says it "sets a new standard for portable power reliability"; How-To Geek's headline — "isn't perfect, but it's perfectly priced" — is the consensus.

The imperfections are real and small. Solar input tops out at 600 W, which is fine for a 200–400 W panel but a ceiling if you ever want to charge it fast from the sun. Unlike the original C1000, the Gen 2 takes no expansion battery — Anker traded that for the lighter chassis — so if you'll want 2 kWh next year, buy 2 kWh now. HyperFlash is loud; use the app's slower mode overnight. And Anker's ecosystem doesn't reach into whole-home backup the way EcoFlow's does. None of that changes the recommendation. At the sale price it's the best combination of capacity, power, speed, weight and support in the market, and the reason it's reported as the best-selling 1 kWh unit on Amazon.

  • Five AC outlets, two 140 W USB-C
  • Best-rated support of the four brands

Cons

  • No expansion battery

  • Loud on fast charge

Best UPS & expandable 1 kWh EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus

IMAGE SLOT — Amazon SiteStripe → Image → EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus.
Price: $999 list; $599–$699 typical
Capacity / output: 1,024 Wh LFP; 1,800 W continuous, 3,600 W surge; X-Boost to 2,200 W
Recharge: 0–100% in 56 min at 1,500 W AC; solar 2× 500 W = 1,000 W (~70 min)
Weight: 27.6 lb
Expansion: DELTA 3 Extra Battery, to just over 5 kWh
UPS / warranty: under 10 ms; 5 years

If the C1000 Gen 2 is the best box, the DELTA 3 Plus is the best platform. It matches Anker on the things that matter in an outage — a sub-10 ms UPS, a 56-minute recharge, a kilowatt-hour of LFP — and beats it on the things that matter in year three: a 1,000 W solar input (two 500 W MPPT ports) that fills the battery from the sun in an hour and change, an expansion battery that takes it past 5 kWh, and the deepest app and smart-home ecosystem in the category. TechRadar and the solar-specialist sites make it their default "best for most people," and The Solar Lab's review title — "amazing, but one big quirk" — is fair: the quirk is that 1,800 W continuous is a little less than Anker's 2,000 W, and X-Boost gets you to 2,200 W only for resistive loads.

The reasons some owners choose Anker instead are worth knowing before you buy. The app requires an account and is the only route to firmware updates. The DELTA 3 chassis draws "fan and temperature control" complaints under sustained heavy load and a few reports of thermal shutdowns in hot garages. And the list price is a fiction that makes the unit look $400 more expensive than the C1000 Gen 2 when the real gap on sale is about $100. Pay the $100 if you want expansion, big solar or the EcoFlow ecosystem; save it if you want the simplest, lightest kilowatt-hour.

Best for multi-day outages & camping Jackery Explorer 2000 v2

IMAGE SLOT — Amazon SiteStripe → Image → Jackery Explorer 2000 v2.
Price: $1,499 list; $599 (Black Friday low) to $899
Capacity / output: 2,042 Wh LFP; 2,200 W continuous, 4,400 W surge
Recharge: 80% in 66 min, full in 102 min; solar up to 400 W (5–6 h with two 200 W panels)
Weight: 39–40 lb
Expansion: none
Efficiency / warranty: ~90% claimed; 5 years (3 + 2 on registration)

OutdoorGearLab's top pick for extended off-grid use is the one we'd buy for a family that has to get through more than a night. Two kilowatt-hours runs a full-size fridge, the router, phones and lights for 17–20 hours without rationing, or the better part of three days with the discipline in the outage playbook and a 400 W panel; 2,200 W with a 4,400 W surge starts anything in a normal house short of central air. What makes it the pick over the equally good Bluetti and Anker 2 kWh units is the weight — 39 pounds, ten to fourteen fewer than the Elite 200 V2 — and the simplicity: no account, a basic app, an "Emergency Charge" button on the box, and a five-year warranty from the brand that has been doing this longest.

What you give up is the ecosystem. The 2000 v2 doesn't take an expansion battery, its solar input is a modest 400 W, and its UPS is a plain pass-through rather than the sub-10 ms class. If you know you'll want 4 kWh in two years, buy the DELTA 3 Max Plus and its expansion path instead; if you want 2 kWh that just works, this is it. On price, it is the most seasonal pick in the guide: $899 most of the year, $699–$749 during Prime events and $599 last Black Friday, so if the calendar allows, wait.

Best home backup EcoFlow DELTA Pro 3

IMAGE SLOT — Amazon SiteStripe → Image → EcoFlow DELTA Pro 3.
Price: $3,699 list; $1,999–$2,499 typical; $3,599 bundled with the Smart Home Panel 2
Capacity / output: 4,096 Wh LFP; 4,000 W continuous, 8,000 W surge; 120 V and 240 V from one unit
Recharge: 0–80% in about 89 min on AC; solar up to 2,600 W (1,600 W high-PV + 1,000 W low-PV); generator input
Weight: about 114 lb, wheels and telescoping handle
Expansion: 12 kWh on one unit; 48 kWh with three units and the Smart Home Panel 2
Noise / warranty: ~30 dB quiet mode; 5 years

"The most capable single-unit portable power station for home backup" is how the specialist reviewers put it, and the specification supports it: four kilowatt-hours of LFP, a 4,000 W inverter that supplies 120 V and 240 V from one box (so a well pump, a dryer or an air handler is possible without buying a second unit), 2,600 W of solar input that refills it in a couple of sunny hours, and an expansion path that runs from one extra battery in the closet to a 48 kWh Smart Home Panel 2 installation that switches the whole house over automatically. OutdoorGearLab names it the best for home backup, RV.com calls it "the best gets better," and on sale at $1,999 it costs less than a mid-size standby generator before installation. It is also, in its quiet mode, close to silent — the DELTA Pro 3's fans are the one thing in this class nobody complains about.

The realities: 114 pounds is a two-person lift up a step, the reason it has wheels; 4 kWh is a day of a household's essentials, not a week; the idle drain of a big inverter means you turn the AC side off when it isn't needed; the app and account rules are EcoFlow's; and installing a transfer switch or the Smart Home Panel 2 is an electrician's job that adds $500–$1,500. If your list includes 240 V, the DELTA Pro 3 and the F3800 below are the only two choices in this guide; if it doesn't, a 2 kWh unit plus a panel is often the smarter money.

  • Expands to 48 kWh; whole-home panel option
  • Quiet; wheels; 5-year warranty

Cons

  • 114 lb
  • Panel or transfer switch needs an electrician
  • EcoFlow app and account
  • Idle drain if left on

Most common owner complaint: weight and the cost of the whole-home accessories. Who should skip it: anyone without a 240 V need and a place to keep 114 pounds; renters (the F3800 or a 2 kWh unit is easier to move out).

Best for 240 V & EV charging Anker SOLIX F3800 / F3800 Plus

IMAGE SLOT — Amazon SiteStripe → Image → Anker SOLIX F3800 Plus.
Price: $3,999 list; $1,709–$2,399 seen in 2026 ($1,999 with a 400 W panel in May); the Plus about $300 more
Capacity / output: 3,840 Wh LFP; 6,000 W at 120/240 V
Outlets: NEMA 14-50 and L14-30 (RV, EV, transfer switch), plus standard AC and USB
Solar: up to 2,400 W (F3800) / 3,200 W and a wider voltage window (F3800 Plus)
Expansion: to 26.9 kWh with six expansion batteries; two units pair for 12,000 W
Weight / warranty: about 132 lb on wheels; 5 years

The F3800 is the unit for the house with a 240 V problem: a well pump, an electric dryer, a heat pump's air handler, or an electric car in the driveway. Its 6,000 W inverter is half again the DELTA Pro 3's, it has the 14-50 and L14-30 outlets that plug straight into a transfer switch or an RV, and its party trick is charging an EV — Anker says a full unit puts roughly twelve miles into a car at 240 V, which is not a road trip but is the "emergency mile" to a charger that works when the gas stations don't. TechRadar names it the best power station for home backup; OutdoorGearLab calls it "our favourite large-capacity unit"; the whole-home kit with the 10-circuit manual transfer switch is the cleanest install of the four brands. The F3800 Plus (March 2025) adds 3,200 W of solar and a voltage window that accepts rooftop-style panels; if you're planning a real array, it's worth the $300.

Two cautions from the owner threads. The F3800 Plus drew specific complaints for idle drain — it powers its own electronics from the pack when switched on, and left idle for weeks it loses meaningful charge — so switch it off or leave it on the wall. And its slightly smaller capacity (3,840 Wh) and slower AC recharge than the DELTA Pro 3 mean that, for a house with no 240 V or EV need, EcoFlow's unit is the better buy at the same money. For the house that does, nothing else at this price does the job.

Most common owner complaint: the idle drain, and the weight of the expansion batteries. Who should skip it: anyone without a 240 V or EV need — the DELTA Pro 3 or a 2 kWh unit and a panel is the better buy.

Best value 2 kWh Bluetti Elite 200 V2

IMAGE SLOT — Amazon SiteStripe → Image → BLUETTI Elite 200 V2.
Price: about $1,000; $949 with Amazon's coupon
Capacity / output: 2,073.6 Wh LFP; 2,600 W continuous, up to 3,900 W with Power Lifting
Recharge: 80% in about 50–60 min (TurboBoost); solar up to 1,000 W (~2.4 h)
Weight: 53.4 lb
Cells / cycles: CNAS-certified automotive-grade LFP; 6,000+ cycles claimed
Warranty: 5 years

On paper the Elite 200 V2 is the best 2 kWh station you can buy: 2,600 W continuous (the Jackery is 2,200, the DELTA 3 Max 2,400), 1,000 W of solar input (2.5 times the Jackery's), 80% in under an hour, cells certified by a national lab and rated for 6,000 cycles — twice the industry's usual claim — and a chassis 40% smaller than the model it replaced. StorageReview's headline was "lab-grade reliability"; The Van Conversion calls it the best station for van life; and at $949 it is "the clearest value in the 2,000 Wh class," which is why it's here.

Off paper there are two things. It weighs 53 pounds — a real two-hand lift, and the reason the Jackery ranks above it for anyone who will move it — and it's a Bluetti, which means the warranty is only as good as a support department that owners rate the worst of the four. Buy it on Amazon for the return window, register it, update it, and if it runs for the first month it will most likely run for a decade. It does not expand.

Lightest 1 kWh Jackery Explorer 1000 v2

IMAGE SLOT — Amazon SiteStripe → Image → Jackery Explorer 1000 v2.
Price: $799 list; $550–$650 typical with coupons
Capacity / output: 1,070 Wh LFP; 1,500 W continuous, 3,000 W surge
Recharge: 0–100% in about 1.7 h on AC (faster to 80% in Emergency mode); solar up to 400 W
Weight: 23.8 lb (reviewers measure ~25)
Ports: 100 W USB-C, USB-A, 12 V, AC outlets
Warranty: 5 years (3 + 2 on registration)

The 1000 v2 is the kilowatt-hour for people who will carry it: a pound and a half lighter than the C1000 Gen 2, four lighter than the DELTA 3 Plus, with a handle that folds flat and a shape that fits a car-trunk organiser. It has a little more capacity than either (1,070 Wh), it cleared a specialist's start-up test on a 1/3 HP submersible sump pump with 2–3 hours of intermittent cycling, and Jackery's "easiest recommendation" reputation applies — no account, a basic app, and the longest track record in the business. Coupons on Amazon and Jackery's site regularly pull it to $550, which makes it the cheapest name-brand kilowatt-hour some weeks.

The trade is output and speed: 1,500 W continuous is enough for a fridge, a microwave or a coffee maker, not all three at once, the solar input is 400 W, the recharge is 1.7 hours rather than 50 minutes, and it doesn't expand. Campers and CPAP users won't notice; a household sizing for a serious outage should spend the extra $50 on the C1000 Gen 2.

Best small station & CPAP Bluetti Elite 30 V2

IMAGE SLOT — Amazon SiteStripe → Image → BLUETTI Elite 30 V2.
Price: $299 list; $219–$249 typical
Capacity / output: 288 Wh LFP; 600 W continuous, 1,500 W Power Lifting; two AC outlets
Recharge: 80% in 45 min; solar up to 200 W (XT60, 12–28 V)
Weight: 9.48 lb
Ports: nine, including 100 W USB-C and a 12 V socket
UPS / cycles: 10 ms; 3,000+ cycles

The Elite 30 V2 is the small station that behaves like a big one: two real AC outlets, a 600 W inverter that runs a fridge in a pinch, a 10 ms UPS that a $219 product has no business having, and an 80% recharge in 45 minutes. OutdoorGearLab scored it highest of every small unit it tested (72/100), T3 called it "top-performing, small-form portable power at a knock-down price," and it is the unit we'd hand a CPAP user: run the machine on its 12 V DC cord with the humidifier off and 288 Wh is a full night with margin; through AC with the humidifier on, plan on the 1 kWh class. For a weekend of phones, a laptop, camp lights and a fan it is more than enough, and it's a genuinely nice object.

The caveats are the category's and the brand's: 288 Wh runs a refrigerator for two hours, not a night; the fan has a Bluetti-style chatter in UPS mode that some owners hate; and support is Bluetti's. At $219 the calculation still favours it over the Jackery 300 Plus ($199–$299, 300 W, no UPS) and the RIVER 3 ($199, 245 Wh) for anyone who wants the AC outlets to mean something.
