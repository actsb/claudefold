Ninety-five million American households have a pet, and most of them buy the same three things on Amazon: something for the fur, something for the grooming, and, if there's a cat, the robot that ends litter duty. We went through the reviews, the expert tests and the price history of Amazon's best sellers to pick one winner at $25, one at $85 and one at $699 — and then read what owners say about actually receiving them.

Here's the trick with pet products: the categories are enormous, the listings are a mess of look-alikes, and the reviews are the most emotional on the internet. Nobody writes 900 words about a phone charger. People absolutely write 900 words about the roller that saved their beige sofa. So we treated those 190,000-plus ratings the way we treat lab data in our other guides — looked for the themes that repeat, checked them against the testers at Wirecutter, CNN Underscored, Reader's Digest, Dogster and Catster, and threw out anything we couldn't verify. What's left is three products, three prices, and the honest version of what they do.

Good: the ChomChom Roller — $25 and 190,000 people who agree

A palm-sized plastic roller with two strips of nylon micro-bristles and a rubber squeegee inside. You rock it back and forth over the couch; the bristles grab hair on the forward stroke, the squeegee scrapes it into a covered chamber on the way back, and when the chamber is full you press a button and drop out a felt-like wad of fur that looks like a small second pet. No batteries, no sticky refills, no cord. It was invented in Bellevue, Washington in 2012 by two founders who patented the mechanism, and it has been Amazon's best-selling pet hair remover for most of the decade — Yahoo Shopping calls it the No. 1 bestselling pet hair remover on Amazon, and Today reports it holds the top spot in both the cat and the dog hair-removal categories.

Why it sells

Speed. Reader's Digest cleared a polyester cushion in about thirty seconds; ABC15's on-camera test took forty-five seconds to strip a couch and scored it "no bull." The second reason is that it costs nothing to run: the sticky-roller people spend $30 a year on refills and still lose. The third is that it doesn't care what kind of hair you have — fine cat fluff, coarse Jindo guard hairs, or the long human hair that Apartment Therapy found tangled in with the dog's.

The one-week learning curve

  1. Short, quick strokes at about 45 degrees. Rolling in one direction does nothing; the back-and-forth is the whole mechanism. The Reader's Digest tester says it took about a week to get natural.
  2. Pull the fabric taut. Couch cushions and car seats are perfect. Loose throws, blankets and clothing bunch up and defeat it — use a sticky roller for clothes.
  3. Keep your thumb off the button. It sits exactly where a thumb lands, and the door pops open mid-roll and returns the fur to the couch. Owners learn to grip the handle further back.
  4. Empty it before it's full. In a heavy-shedding home the chamber packs solid halfway through a sofa.
  5. Dry only. Damp fur after a rainy walk just smears. Wait an hour.

Not for carpet

CNN Underscored put the ChomChom against the Uproot Cleaner Pro and drew the line clearly: the roller owns upholstery and bedding, and it will not dig embedded hair out of carpet pile. For rugs and stairs you want the Uproot (about $20–$25) or a real vacuum. Cat-tree platforms, with their tight flat carpet, are the exception — owners say those are one of its best uses.

Not it? Two alternatives

  • Cheaper: the Black+Decker pet hair roller — Wirecutter's other favourite, the same mechanism with an easy-empty bin, and it has been spotted under $10. Choose it if you want the idea at half the price and can live with a less refined build.
  • Pricier: the Bissell Pet Hair Eraser cordless hand vacuum ($50–$75) for carpeted stairs, car floors and deep pile. Reader's Digest's reviewer owns both and still reaches for the ChomChom more, because the Bissell needs charging and is loud.

Better: the Neakasa P1 Pro — the brush that's also a vacuum

Here's the idea that made this thing a best seller: what if the fur never reached the couch at all? The P1 Pro is a small corded canister vacuum with a hose that snaps onto grooming tools. Brush the dog and the loose fur goes down the hose into a one-liter cup instead of onto the floor, the bed, your black trousers and, somehow, the inside of the dishwasher. The kit includes a grooming brush, a de-shedding brush, a low-noise clipper with guard combs, a nozzle for cleaning up around the animal, and a cleaning brush. There's no app, no Wi-Fi, no subscription and no battery to die, which in 2026 is practically a feature.

Which tool for which pet

  • The nervous dog: start on eco mode (Neakasa quotes 52 dB, quieter than a household vacuum) with just the brush, no clipper, and treats. One owner who blogged the process said the noise was "a hurdle" for a session or two — and then the dog stopped bolting the way it does from the floor vacuum.
  • The double coat (Jindos, huskies, shepherds, goldens): the de-shedding brush, in sections, on a towel. This is where the 1-liter cup shows its limit — Dogster's tester paused mid-groom to empty it on a heavy shedder. If that's your dog every week, the P2 Pro's 2-liter cup is the upgrade that halves the interruptions.
  • The cat: the soft grooming brush on the lowest suction. The listing markets it for cats and "other animals"; owners say cats tolerate the brush far better than they tolerate the clipper.
  • Between groomer visits: the clipper with a guard comb for sanitary trims and paw feathering on small and medium dogs. It is not a shave-down tool; a full shave of a thick coat is slow and dulls the blade, which Neakasa's own FAQ admits.
  • After the dog: the nozzle head on the sofa cushions before guests arrive.

What owners and testers say

The praise is consistent: the fur goes into the bin, the dog gets used to it, and the kit pays for itself in a groomer visit or two. The complaints are consistent too: the cup fills fast, the clipper is a trimmer, and suction fades if you let the filter clog or crack the hose — maintenance isn't optional. Two things stand out for 2026. Neakasa extended the warranty to two years for units bought on or after January 1, 2026, covering the vacuum base (not the accessories) and only from authorized sellers, which on Amazon means the Neakasa store, not a random reseller. And the Amazon listing advertises a 45-day return window, more generous than the brand site's 30.

Not it? Two alternatives

  • Cheaper: the Oneisall grooming vacuum kit. A March 2026 head-to-head says: pick it if you groom once a month or less; pick the P1 Pro if you groom weekly or fight real shedding.
  • Pricier: the Neakasa P2 Pro — 10,500 Pa, a 2-liter cup and a longer detachable hose, for large breeds, double coats and multi-pet homes.

Best: the Litter-Robot 4 — for the cat people

Noeul sits this one out. The Litter-Robot 4 is the most-wanted premium pet product on the internet, the thing cat owners send each other at 11 p.m. with the message "should I?" It's a rotating globe on a base: after the cat leaves, the globe turns, clumps fall through a screen into a sealed, carbon-filtered drawer, and the clean litter stays. Laser sensors and a scale read the litter level, the drawer, and which cat visited and how much it weighed. The Michigan company behind it, Whisker, says it has 1.8 million users and 7,000-plus verified reviews of this model at 4.4 out of 5. In October 2025 it launched the pricier Litter-Robot 5 and 5 Pro and the smaller EVO, and the 4 settled into the middle of the range at $699 — where, for now, it's still the one to buy, because the new 5 Pro drew a "too many bugs" verdict from BGR and a reliability step-back from Cats.com.

What it costs to run

Owners spend roughly $50 to $120 a year on drawer liners and carbon filters, and multi-cat homes add $30–$50. The optional OdorTrap packs are the budget-breaker: about $15 a pair with a two-week life, which is up to $360 a year if you never turn them off. Most people don't. There's no required subscription; Whisker+ ($7.99 a month, 30-day trial) only adds long-term history and trends.

What owners say after a year

The praise clusters around four things: the odor really does stay in the drawer, you go from daily scooping to emptying a bag once a week, it's quiet (about 37 dB while cycling, roughly a whisper, and about 10 dB quieter than the old Litter-Robot 3), and the app's weight tracking has a habit of catching problems early — one long-term reviewer credits it with flagging a urinary infection. The complaints are the price, the occasional false "drawer full" reading, and the consumables. The false reading has a known cause: dust or fur on the bezel sensors. Wipe them, vacuum the bezel, and it stops; persistent faults are a warranty matter.

A safety note you should read before buying any self-cleaning box

In 2024 and 2025 there were reports of cats killed by cheap Ferris-wheel-style automatic litter boxes sold under no-name brands on Amazon; one of those models was removed from the site. No Litter-Robot has been recalled, and the 4 has anti-pinch sensors that stop and retract the globe if something is caught. The documented injuries in Whisker units are rare and tied to cats under the 3-pound minimum or to owners disabling safety features. The lesson is not "avoid robot litter boxes." It's "avoid the $150 one with no brand, no weight sensor and no company behind it."

Amazon, Costco or direct?

This is the one product on this page where the answer isn't automatically Amazon. Buying direct gets you a 90-day in-home trial and automatic warranty activation; Amazon and Costco purchases return under the retailer's own policy (30 days at Amazon) and you must activate the WhiskerCare warranty manually in the app. Against that, the retail bundles have been the best value of 2026: Costco's $699.99 bundle in July came with the 3-year warranty, ramp, fence, mat, a six-pack of OdorTrap and 25 liners, which direct would cost around $900. On Amazon the Litter-Robot is listed "by Whisker" in Core, Complete and Supply bundles; compare what's in the box, and check the buy box says Whisker before you pay.

Not it? Two alternatives

  • Comparable: the PETKIT Purobot Max Pro 2 ($559.99, dipping toward $440) — an AI camera that recognises each cat's face and reads their health, with higher running costs and a younger support network. Cats that refuse enclosed boxes: the open-top Neakasa M1 at about $499.

What buying on Amazon actually gets you

Pet supplies are the category where Amazon's logistics matter most, because a lot of it is heavy, recurring and needed on a schedule. Here is what you get, from Amazon's own policy pages and its 2025–2026 announcements, and where the competition still wins.

Same-day is free for Prime members on orders over $25 in most cities. A $4 billion rural build-out announced in April 2025 is meant to triple the small-town delivery network by the end of this year.   • Subscribe & Save. Five percent off every subscription delivery, up to 15 percent when five or more subscriptions ship on the same day. Food, litter, and the Litter-Robot's liners and filters qualify; the roller and the grooming kit are one-time buys. Chewy still wins the first order (35 percent off, capped at $20) but settles to 5 percent on select brands after that, and Profitero's 2025 price study found Amazon about 1 percent cheaper than Chewy and 3 percent cheaper than Walmart and PetSmart across pet supplies.

For these three, the right sellers read ChomChomRoller, Neakasa and Whisker. Counterfeits are a real pet-category problem — fake Seresto flea collars, fake Cosequin supplements and fake FURminators have all been documented — and ChomChom fought a wave of knock-offs in 2020 by putting an Amazon Transparency code on every unit. A roller that arrives with no branding is not the real one.

Quick answers

Does the ChomChom work on cat hair?

Yes — it tops Amazon's cat hair-removal category as well as the dog one. Fine fluff comes up as well as coarse hair; the only thing it can't handle is loose fabric or carpet pile.

Original, Mini, To-Go or Elite?

Original for sofas and beds; Mini (about half the size, $19–$24) for cars, stairs and cat trees; To-Go for a purse or glovebox; Elite is a size-and-colour variant that sends part of the price to the ASPCA.

Will the Neakasa handle a husky or a golden?

It de-sheds them fine; the 1-liter cup is the limit, so expect to stop and empty it. If that's every week, buy the P2 Pro with the 2-liter cup instead.

Can I shave my dog with the P1 Pro clipper?

Trims and touch-ups on small and medium dogs, yes. A full shave-down of a thick coat is slow and dulls the blade; that's a groomer job or a dedicated clipper.

What wears out on the grooming vacuum?

Filters and clipper blades. Neakasa's FAQ says a clogged filter or a cracked hose is the usual reason for weak suction; replacements are cheap and on Amazon.

Does the Litter-Robot need a subscription?

No. The app is free. Whisker+ at $7.99 a month only adds long-term history and trends.

Which litter goes in it?

Standard clumping clay works best; granular tofu litters are fine. Pellets, non-clumping, paper and pine won't sift, and white or translucent crystal litter confuses the sensors.

Is a Litter-Robot safe for a kitten?

Use semi-automatic mode until the kitten reaches 3 pounds; automatic cycling needs the weight sensor to register the cat. There's no upper limit — Whisker says 25-pound cats use it, though a minority of owners find the globe snug.

Is Amazon cheaper than Chewy for pet supplies?

Usually, by a little: about 1 percent on Profitero's 2025 basket. Chewy wins the first autoship order with 35 percent off (max $20); Amazon wins on delivery speed and on standing Subscribe & Save discounts up to 15 percent.

What should I never buy in this category?

Any automatic litter box with no brand, no weight sensor and no company to call; any roller that arrives unbranded; and any flea collar or supplement from a seller that isn't the brand or Amazon itself. The three products here have a real company behind them — that's half of why they're best sellers.
