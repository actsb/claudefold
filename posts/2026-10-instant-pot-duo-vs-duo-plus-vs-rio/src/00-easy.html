The three 6-quart Instant Pots people argue about are closer than their names suggest. The Duo, Duo Plus and Rio all run at 1,000 watts with a stainless pot, and Instant Pot's own store lists them at $109.99, $139.99 and $109.99 (October 2026). Our pick for most kitchens is the plain Duo. The Duo Plus earns its $30 only if you will use sous vide or want a quieter steam release, and the Rio is the one to check twice: it has no low-pressure setting that we could confirm, and Target shoppers rate it 3.9 out of 5. This guide sorts out what each model adds, what owners and test kitchens report, the model numbers that trip buyers up, and what to pay before Black Friday on November 27.

Key takeaways

  • Buy the Duo 6-quart. Same power and stainless pot as its siblings, the most owners and recipes behind it, and $109.99 at Instant Pot's store (October 2026).
  • Pay up for the Duo Plus only for specific extras. Sous vide, cake, a quieter steam release and two spare sealing rings. The core cooking is the same, so we would not pay more than about $20 over the Duo.
  • The Rio is a trade, not an upgrade. Its pressure setting is high only per Pressure Cooking Today, the pot has no handles per America's Test Kitchen, and 20% of its 247 Target reviews are one star (October 2026).
  • The catch is the model numbers. The Walmart Rio Chef Series has a coated aluminum pot, and Target's 6-quart bundle is a different unit from the Duo Plus. Check the model number before you pay.

What actually differs between the Duo, Duo Plus and Rio?

Less than the box art suggests: all three have 1,000 W and a stainless pot, so the differences are the extra programs, the steam release and the display.

Instant Pot's own product pages (seen October 21, 2026) put the Duo and the Rio on the same 7 functions and 13 one-touch programs. The Duo Plus V4 adds sous vide and cake for 9 functions, lists 25 recipe presets, and ships with a steam rack and two extra sealing rings. The Pro is the only one of the four at 1,200 W. The three differences that matter in the kitchen

  • Steam release. The Duo and Rio use the Easy-Release switch. The Duo Plus V4 has the Whisper Quiet release, which TechGearLab described in its review (October 2022) as a new valve with a remote release button. If your kitchen is open-plan, this is the real reason to pay for the Plus.
  • Pressure settings. Pressure Cooking Today reports that the Rio's pressure cook setting is high only; in its Rio review (2023, updated 2024) the author confirmed to a reader in 2025 that no low setting is normal. Low pressure matters mostly for eggs and delicate foods; we could not confirm the Duo's current low setting from the manual, so check yours.
  • The pot. All three use 18/8 stainless steel with a tri-ply base. TechGearLab found the Duo Plus V4 scorched while sautéing and stained, and America's Test Kitchen found the Rio's sauté underpowered, so none of them browns like a Dutch oven. The Pro's flat pot is the one ATK praised for searing.

The model-number traps

Four listings share these names and are not the same machine. Most of the unhappy reviews we found are from buyers who got a version they did not expect.

  • Rio Chef Series (Walmart). Model 112-2000-01 has a non-stick, ceramic-coated aluminum pot, not stainless, per Instant Pot's product page and a Slickdeals commenter in October 2026. A Slickdeals post had it at $99 on June 20, 2026, and a later one at $89 in October. It is a different cooker from the standard Rio.
  • Rio Gourmet (Costco). Model 112-2213-01 has a different button layout (Soup, Meat/Stew, Bean/Chili and so on), per Instant Pot's page.
  • Target's 6QT 9-in-1 bundle. Model 112-2356-01 lists 9 functions, 15 one-touch programs and two rings, but it is not the Duo Plus V4 (112-0169-01). Slickdeals owners disagree about whether its cord is removable, so treat that as unverified.
  • Older Duo Plus V3. Model 112-0156-01 had Cake, Egg and Sterilize buttons and 15 programs, and the button list changed in later versions. Match the model number, not the name.

What owners praise

  • Speed and the stainless pot. Target reviewers of the Rio praise the stainless pot as easier to clean than a non-stick one and say cooking is much faster (January and September 2026).
  • It lasts, for most people. Best Buy owners of the older Duo Plus report one to two years of use "like the first day", though many of those reviews were incentivized.
  • The recipes exist. Food Network said many of its own test-kitchen recipes were developed for the Duo.

What owners complain about

  • Dead displays and timers. Target bundle reviews from September and October 2026 describe a dead display and a unit that "died after only a handful of uses". Complaint-site posts from 2023 and 2024 describe timers that never start and a "LID" error; that site only collects complaints, so it skews negative.
  • The burn notice. America's Test Kitchen (April 2022) lists the causes: debris under the pot, a misplaced ring, a dirty float valve, browned bits on the bottom after sautéing, or too little thin liquid. The fix is to deglaze, add about half a cup of water, reseal and add a minute or two.
  • Hot metal and the missing handles. One Target reviewer of the Rio (September 2026) warned of hot metal plates near the lid handle. We found no second source for that, so take it as one owner's report.

The questions people ask before buying

  • Is the Duo Plus pot non-stick? No. Instant Pot support said on the Best Buy Q&A page that it is stainless and cleans easily; owners suggest a liner if rice sticks.
  • Is the sealing-ring smell normal? Yes, with silicone. Guides recommend steaming with vinegar, and a spare ring for sweet foods; the Duo Plus ships with two.
  • Which size? Third-party guides put 6 quarts at roughly four to six people and note that you can only fill it to about 4 quarts.

What do the experts say about these models?

Experts do not agree: Food Network picks the Duo, Tom's Guide picks the Rio, America's Test Kitchen picks the Pro, and nobody recommends any of them for slow cooking.

  • America's Test Kitchen (May 14, 2025): the winner was the Instant Pot Pro 8-quart. The Duo 6-quart "struggled with slow cooking" (a 6-quart stew was still pink after 8 hours), and the Rio did not slow-cook well, browned poorly on an underpowered sauté, and has a pot without handles. The Duo Plus was not tested.
  • Food Network (updated February 21, 2025): best overall was the Duo, for easy controls, the yogurt function, its recipe support and the lowest price in the lineup; it noted the lid can be hard to lock. The Pro was "most presets".
  • Tom's Guide (2026 roundup, from the search summary; we could not load the full page): the Rio is best overall, the Duo Plus is the most versatile, and its Duo Plus review found it performed well except on the rice program.
  • Consumer Reports (January 1, 2026 multi-cooker ratings) names other Instant Pot models, not these; it gives no verdict on the Duo, Duo Plus or Rio. We could not verify Wirecutter's current pick because its site was unreachable, so we do not cite it.

The pattern we draw from this is ours: the testers rank the plain Duo and Rio roughly level on pressure cooking and both behind the Pro, so the extra $30 for the Plus has to come from sous vide, cake or noise, not from better results.

How do you use an Instant Pot right?

Do the water test before your first meal: it takes about 15 minutes and tells you the ring, lid and valve work.

  1. Seat the ring and check the rim. A ring that is twisted or stretched is the usual reason a lid will not seal.
  2. Run the water test. Three cups of water, valve on Sealing, Pressure Cook for 5 minutes, then Quick Release. Expect 5 to 15 minutes before the countdown starts.
  3. Add enough thin liquid. Instant Pot's FAQ says 1½ cups for a 6-quart (a dev-site article says 1 cup, so use at least that). Tomato, cream and thick sauces do not count; this is also the main cause of the burn notice.
  4. Respect the fill lines. Never past two-thirds, and only to half for rice, beans and foods that expand.
  5. Pick the release. Natural release for beans, oatmeal and soups; quick release for vegetables, per the FAQ.
  6. Do not use it for canning. The National Center for Home Food Preservation (University of Georgia, 2019) says it does not support USDA canning processes in electric multi-cookers, and Penn State Extension (updated August 2025) says not to use their canning features. See the NCHFP notice.

What's the catch with all three?

None of them is a good slow cooker, and the most common failures are small parts: the ring, the lid and the display.

America's Test Kitchen found the Duo and Rio both slow-cook poorly, so keep a real slow cooker or buy a Duo Crisp if you want an air fryer lid. Owners also report that the display or timer can fail early, mostly on Target's own units, and support has mixed reviews: a complaint site collected posts in 2023 and 2024 about unanswered phones, which we treat as anecdotal.

  • Hands full of rice? The stainless pot can stick; owners use a liner or more oil.
  • Small kitchen? The Rio is the slimmest, but measure: listed widths run from about 12 to 14 inches.
  • Elderly or one-handed cooks may prefer the Pro's pot handles.

  • Pricier: the Instant Pot Pro 6-quart ($169.99 list, October 2026) — more power and pot handles for people who cook on it most days.

Quick answers

Which Instant Pot 6-quart should I buy?

The Duo, model 112-0170-01, for most kitchens. It matches the Duo Plus and Rio on power (1,000 W) and pot (18/8 stainless) and lists at $109.99 at Instant Pot's store (October 2026).

Is the Duo Plus worth $30 more than the Duo?

Only if you will use sous vide or cake, want the Whisper Quiet release, or value the two spare sealing rings. For rice, beans and stews, the core cooking is the same, and Tom's Guide found the Plus's rice program the weak spot.

What is the difference between the Rio and the Duo?

The Rio has the same 7 functions and 13 programs, a slimmer body and a time-only display. Pressure Cooking Today reports its pressure setting is high only, and ATK noted its pot has no handles.

Does the Rio have a low pressure setting?

Not that we could confirm. Pressure Cooking Today says no, and Instant Pot's Rio page does not list one. Check the manual for your exact model.

Is the Walmart Rio Chef Series the same as the Rio?

No. The Chef Series (112-2000-01) has a non-stick ceramic-coated aluminum pot, not stainless, per Instant Pot's product page.

Is Target's 6-quart bundle a Duo Plus?

No. It is a Target-exclusive 6QT 9-in-1 (112-2356-01) with 15 programs and two rings, and its Target reviews are weaker than the standard Duo Plus's at Best Buy.

Can I use an Instant Pot for canning?

No. The National Center for Home Food Preservation says it does not support USDA canning processes in electric multi-cookers, and Penn State Extension (August 2025) advises against it. Use a pressure canner with a tested recipe.

How long does the sealing ring last?

Instant Pot says to replace it every 12 to 18 months, or sooner if it is stretched or damaged. A genuine ring is $9.99 at Instant Pot's store (October 2026).

Why does my Instant Pot say Burn?

Usually debris under the pot, a misplaced ring, a dirty float valve, browned bits after sautéing, or too little thin liquid, per America's Test Kitchen (April 2022). Deglaze, add water and reseal.

When is the best time to buy?

Early November through Black Friday on November 27, 2026. Past sales ran about 32% to 57% off list depending on the model, per Bob Vila and a pressure-cooker blog (November 2025).
