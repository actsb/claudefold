The reviews we could read lean positive: a search summary of a Walmart listing showed 4.6 out of 5 for the 6-quart Duo (October 5, 2026; the review count was not visible), with complaints about the gasket and a few early failures.

What they praise

  • Speed and versatility: one pot for rice, beans, stews, soup and yogurt, per the Walmart summary.
  • The stainless inner pot, which is easy to clean (it is uncoated 304 stainless steel; a ceramic pot is sold separately).
  • The front-mounted release switch on current models.

What they complain about

  • The sealing ring is fiddly to remove and reseat, and the lid is awkward to clean, according to the same summary.

How do you use the Instant Pot Duo right?

Add enough liquid and seat the sealing ring properly: the manual says 1½ cups minimum for the 6-quart, and a puckered ring is the usual cause of a lid that will not seal.

  1. Run the test first. Per the v5 manual, run the Steam program with water and no food for 5 minutes, then discard the water. Older videos show a 3-cup test from older manuals; follow the manual for your model.
  2. Seat the sealing ring. Press it down firmly over the ring rack with no puckering. The manual says it should not fall out when you turn the lid over, and should be replaced every 12 to 18 months.
  3. Add liquid and respect the lines. At least 1½ cups of water-based liquid for the 6-quart. Never fill above PC MAX, and only to the ½ line for rice, beans, oats and noodles, which expand or foam.
  4. Lock the lid and set Seal. Line up the arrow with the unlock symbol and turn clockwise. Allow up to 30 minutes to reach pressure; frozen food takes longer.
  5. Choose the release. Natural release (leave the switch on Seal until the float valve drops, up to 40 minutes or more) suits stews, chili, beans and grains. Quick release (switch to Vent) suits vegetables and seafood that overcook.
  6. Open with care. Keep hands and face away from the steam, and wait until the float valve has dropped before turning the lid. Wash the lid and ring after each use.

What's the catch?

The catch is the model naming: a listing that says Duo may be the 9-in-1 Duo Plus, a Target bundle, or an older Duo Nova.

Target's $69.99 exclusive bundle, for example, is a 9-in-1 Duo Plus-class cooker with extra accessories, not the plain Duo (instantpot.com listing, October 2026 snippet). That is not bad, but it is a different product. The other drawbacks are real too.

Quick answers

What is the difference between the Instant Pot Duo and the Duo Plus?

The Duo Plus is a 9-in-1 with extra settings such as cake and eggs and a brighter display, per Good Housekeeping as syndicated. It usually costs more. Check the model number on the listing.

How much liquid does a 6-quart Duo need?

The v5 manual (August 2025) says 1½ cups, or 375 mL, of water-based liquid unless the recipe says otherwise. The 3-quart needs 1 cup and the 8-quart 2 cups.

How long does it take to come to pressure?

The manual says allow up to 30 minutes to reach pressure, and longer for frozen food. That time is not counted in the recipe's cook time.

Should I use natural or quick release?

Natural release suits foods that can keep simmering, such as stews, chili, beans and grains. Quick release suits vegetables and seafood that overcook, per the manual.

Can I pressure can in an Instant Pot?

No. The National Center for Home Food Preservation says its canning processes are not recommended for electric multi-cookers, even with a canning button.

How often should I replace the sealing ring?

The manual says every 12 to 18 months, or sooner if it is stretched or damaged. Instant Pot's store listed a single ring at $9.99 and a two-pack at $14.99 in a snippet.

Is the Duo a good slow cooker?

Not really. America's Test Kitchen found it struggled at slow cooking, so if that is your main use, look at the Cosori 6-quart or a dedicated slow cooker.

What size should I buy?

As a rule of thumb from cooking blogs, 6 quarts suits about 4 to 6 people, and 8 quarts suits larger households. We found no lab guidance on size.

Does the Duo have a warranty?

Yes: one year, limited, repair or replace, for purchases from authorized retailers, per Instant Pot's warranty page. Registration within 30 days is optional and does not change your rights, per the same page.

Is the Duo still worth buying in 2026?

Yes at a sale price, if you want a straightforward pressure cooker. Wirecutter now prefers the Rio, so a clearly cheaper Duo is the case for it; at full list it has less going for it.
