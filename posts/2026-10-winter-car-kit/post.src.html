What do the official checklists agree on?

They agree on the core: a way to stay warm, see, clear the glass, dig out and call for help. They disagree on the extras.

  • Ready.gov (page updated March 12, 2026): jumper cables, flares or a reflective triangle, an ice scraper, a car phone charger, a warm covering, a paper map, and cat litter or sand for tire grip. It does not list a shovel, food or a flashlight.
  • Consumer Reports (updated February 2, 2026): an ice scraper and snow brush, a portable shovel, gloves and a blanket, tire chains or traction boards, booster cables or a portable jump starter, a first-aid kit, a flashlight and a phone charger or power supply. It adds a fire extinguisher and tire sealant, and warns the sealant “may not work well in extreme cold.”
  • CDC (December 2, 2025): the longest list we found, 17 entries, including tow chains, tire chains, waterproof matches and a can to melt snow.
  • National Weather Service checklist (a 2015 PDF): flashlight and batteries, blankets, first aid, shovel, food, water, sand or kitty litter, scraper and brush, booster cables, flares.
  • AAA South Jersey (undated): 15 items, and the only list here that names traction mats, alongside sand and cat litter, hand and foot warmers and a charger programmed with AAA’s number.
  • New York DMV (December 12, 2023): its state transportation department lists a shovel, flares, jumper cables, rope and a scraper, and relays federal advice to add a broom and sand or cat litter.

Two things the lists leave open. First, they say “jumper cables or a jump starter” without ranking them, which is the question our top pick answers. Second, they say “sand or cat litter” without saying one is better: in Reviewed’s ice-rink test (updated February 2024) a puck slid 52 inches on bare ice, 26 on kitty litter and 10 on sand, and clay litter turns to paste when it is damp (CBC). That is a lab-style test, not a car test, but it is the only data we found.

Do you need a jump starter or jumper cables?

Carry a jump starter if you drive alone or park where no one else will pass. Cables are cheaper but need a second running car.

The Drive (updated February 11, 2025) picked the NOCO Boost Plus GB40 as best overall and said its writer owns one; Outdoor Life (May 8, 2025) tested jump starters on a 2015 Ford Escape and a 6.0L 2006 GMC Yukon XL and named the GB40 “best all weather.” NOCO’s own manual rates it from -4°F to 122°F, says not to jump-start a frozen battery, allows no more than five consecutive jump starts in 15 minutes, and weighs 2.4 pounds. The Drive adds the caveat that portable starters “can lose charge in the same cold that drains car batteries,” so check the charge from time to time.

Owner reports are mixed in an instructive way. In a Tahoe Yukon Forum thread (January to February 2020), owners favored a GB40 in the console and the heavier Clore JNC660 for big engines. In a Garage Journal thread (September 2023), one owner reported a GB40 that swelled after a jump and another that lost capacity, while defenders said all small lithium packs eventually fail. The patterns: units that arrived partly charged, were never topped up, sat in a hot parked car or were too small for the engine. We read cables-versus-pack as a trade: the pack is smaller and works alone; cables never go flat.

What is overkill in a winter car kit?

Chains, sealant, tow gear and a fire extinguisher are on the longest lists, but most drivers on plowed roads can skip them. Our reading, not a quote.

  • Tire chains and tow chains. The CDC lists both; Ready.gov says only to carry chains “your local area requires.” If your roads do not require them, they are weight you will not use.
  • Canned tire sealant. Consumer Reports lists it and warns it may not work in extreme cold. A pump and a spare matter more.
  • A fire extinguisher. Consumer Reports lists an automotive-rated one; it is reasonable, but it is not a winter item.
  • A big jump starter plus heavy 4-gauge cables. Pick one. Carry cables as a backup only if you drive a diesel or big V8.
  • Recovery boards for a road car. MaxTrax-class boards are for deep powder and off-road recovery; a Car and Driver test of traction boards (July 2024) was done in sand and mud, not snow.
  • Kitty litter as a plan. Sand beat it in Reviewed’s test, and a bag of either is heavy.
  • A candle in a coffee can. Minnesota’s Department of Public Safety lists one, per CBS Minnesota, so it is not a myth. We found no test of it, and an open flame in a closed car adds fire and carbon-monoxide risk. In our judgment, a wool blanket and hand warmers are the safer way to stay warm.

Do traction mats beat sand?

For a sloped driveway, maybe. For most plowed-road drivers, a small bag of coarse sand does most of the job for about $8.

The Minnesota State Patrol’s “Ask a Trooper” column (Sgt. Jesse Grabow, February 9, 2026) lists sand, chains, snow socks and traction mats, and says to dig out first and put the aid in the path of the drive wheels; floor mats work in a pinch. The evidence for mats in snow is thin: The Drive (May 2023) and AutoGuide (2022) did not test them in snow. Owner reports of the MAXSA Escaper Buddy include a mat that shot out from under the tire (Home Depot reviewer, undated snippet). The same 2-pack showed from $129.99 at Home Depot to $249.99 at Tractor Supply in snippets, so check the model number (20333) and the seller.

What do owners say after a winter with this gear?

Owners say the failures are small and plastic: a shovel lock, a brush handle, a pack that arrived uncharged. Few say the idea was wrong.

Reddit never surfaced in our searches and Home Depot, Lowe’s, Walmart and Costco review pages blocked our tools, so this section rests on forum threads and retailer snippets, each dated where we could. We paraphrase and use no Amazon reviews.

What they praise

  • A jump pack that works first time. Tahoe Yukon Forum owners (2020) report a GB40 in a console that worked; a Walmart reviewer (snippet, undated) says it started a hybrid SUV three times below 10°F.
  • A cheap brush that lasts. A S2000 forum owner says a Hopkins SubZero brush lasted fifteen winters before the top snapped.
  • Lithium AAAs in a headlamp. ToolGuyd explains that alkaline cells fall off in the cold, while Energizer Ultimate Lithium is rated to -40°F.

What they complain about

  • Plastic joints on shovels. A Home Depot reviewer says a telescoping shovel’s head flew off because one screw holds it; Walmart reviewers of folding shovels most often cite the lock.
  • Packs that were not ready. One Walmart reviewer says a GB40 arrived only partly charged and failed on a truck; Garage Journal posters (2023) describe swelling and lost capacity.
  • Mats that launch. Mats that shoot out in powder or crack under heavy trucks, per reports at other sellers.

The questions people ask before buying

  • Which jump starter for a V8 or diesel? Match the engine: the GB40 tops out at 6.0L gas and 3.0L diesel. Owners of bigger engines name the Clore JNC660.
  • Does a lithium pack work in the cold, and where should it live? In the cabin, topped up. Diesel no-starts are often gelled fuel, which no pack fixes.
  • Trunk or cabin? A search summary of Minnesota’s public-safety checklist says to keep the kit in the passenger area because a trunk can freeze shut or be blocked after a crash. We could not open the page, so treat it as a lead.

How do you use the kit when it counts?

Dig out first, put traction under the drive wheels, and if that fails stay with the car with the exhaust clear.

  1. Do not spin the wheels. Spinning digs you in; AAA’s guidance (seen in a search summary only) adds that rocking can harm some transmissions, so check the manual.
  2. Clear the tailpipe. The CDC says a blocked tailpipe lets carbon monoxide “build up quickly inside your vehicle.”
  3. Dig out the drive wheels with the shovel, front and back.
  4. Put sand, a mat or even a floor mat in the path of the drive wheels, as the Minnesota State Patrol column advises.
  5. To jump a dead battery, follow the NOCO manual: clamps on, red to positive, black to bare metal or the negative post, loads off, press power, and wait for the boost light. Retry after 20 to 30 seconds if it does not start.
  6. If you are stranded, stay in the car. The NWS says to run the motor about 10 minutes each hour, crack a window and keep the exhaust clear.

Is Amazon the place to buy it, and when is it not?

Amazon is easy for returns and delivery; basics are cheaper at Harbor Freight, and the AAA kit was cheaper at Lowe’s and Walmart.

  • Harbor Freight owns the cheap basics. Cables $9.99 to $16.99, a headlamp $12.99, a first-aid kit $17.99 and a shovel $14.99 on October 28, with a 90-day return window.
  • Brand stores are not always cheaper. Lowe’s and Walmart sold the AAA kit for less than AAA’s shop (NBC Select, September 24, 2026). AAA’s member price was $54.99 in a snippet but $74.99 on the live page, so do not count on a discount.
  • Be careful with unknown lithium packs.   • Returns. Walmart and Home Depot generally allow 90 days and 30 for electronics; Harbor Freight 90 days (read from its page). We could not confirm Lowe’s.

What does a winter kit really save?

It saves a wait and an awkward call, not much cash. A tow is the cost it avoids.

Our DIY basket is $126 and a jump starter takes it to about $200. A single tow can cost about that: one Kentucky tow company’s rate sheet (effective August 1, 2026), quoted in our tire-inflator guide, lists a $100 hookup plus mileage with a $125 minimum. The kit also costs nothing to carry for years if you never need it, which is the point. If you can tell which gear you will never use, skip it and spend on the jump starter.

Quick answers

What should be in a winter car emergency kit?

The core every list names: an ice scraper and brush, a shovel, a blanket, a flashlight or headlamp, a first-aid kit, a phone charger, and a way to restart the car (cables or a jump starter).

Is a jump starter better than jumper cables?

For solo drivers, yes: it works with no second car. Cables never go flat, so some owners of big engines carry both.

What is the best jump starter for winter?

The NOCO Boost Plus GB40 was The Drive’s best overall (February 2025) and Outdoor Life’s best all weather (May 2025), for gas engines to 6.0L and diesels to 3.0L.

Does a jump starter work in the cold?

Yes, but it loses charge in the same cold as the car battery. NOCO rates the GB40 from -4°F to 122°F; keep it in the cabin and charge it every month or two.

Do traction mats work in snow?

On a dug-out, compacted surface they help; in powder they can shoot out. We found no independent snow test of the MAXSA mats.

Is sand better than cat litter?

In Reviewed’s ice test (February 2024), sand stopped a puck at 10 inches against 26 on litter. Clay litter turns to paste when damp.

Should the kit go in the trunk or the cabin?

A search summary of Minnesota’s public-safety guidance says the passenger area, because a trunk can freeze shut or be crushed. Keep electronics in the cabin.

What do I do if I am stuck in snow?

Clear the tailpipe, dig out the drive wheels and add traction. If that fails, stay in the car, run the engine about 10 minutes an hour with a window cracked (National Weather Service).

Are tire chains and tow chains necessary?

The CDC lists both; Ready.gov says chains only where your area requires them. Skip them on plowed roads.

Is a pre-built winter kit worth it?

It is about the same cost as a DIY basket once you add cables, a headlamp and a power bank to the AAA/Lifeline kit; it is a good gift.
