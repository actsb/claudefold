Two heated blanket recalls in 2026 cover about 373,000 blankets, and the model number is easy to miss on the label. The US Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) recalled about 178,000 OCTROT throws on July 30 and about 195,000 JKMAX blankets and pads on September 17; between them they drew over 1,100 reports of smoking, melting or sparking. Is my heated blanket recalled? How do you check?

Find the model number on the label (the back of the controller, the wash label or the packaging), then compare it with the lists below and with CPSC’s own recall page. If it matches, stop using it and unplug it.

Heated blankets do not carry the model number where you would look first. The CPSC notices point to three places: the back of the detachable controller, a white label on the blanket or its wash tag, and the original packaging. If the label has washed out and you bought online, your order history is the last resort (Bedsure’s 2023 notice names its order-history page). Read the number, then find your brand below.

What if it matches?

Unplug it and stop using it today; do not give it away or sell it, because a recalled blanket is still a fire risk in someone else’s bed. Follow the remedy in the notice, which differs: JKMAX asks for a refund claim, OCTROT and MaxKare want a photo of the cut cord or the disposed blanket first. If the firm does not answer, report it at SaferProducts.gov, the CPSC’s public incident database. One more point for Amazon shoppers: both big 2026 recalls were small brands sold through Amazon marketplace listings, and the refund comes from the brand, not the store.

What if it does not match, or you cannot read the label?

A label that does not match a recall does not make a blanket safe. Check it against the checklist below, and treat a blanket with no readable label, a missing controller or an unknown history the way State Farm’s guide does: replace it (State Farm, September 18, 2026).

  • A detachable controller that makes the blanket washable: Sunbeam’s customer service says to remove it before washing.
  • A price low enough that replacing a blanket every few years is not a hardship.

What they complain about

  • The controller. Walmart reviewers of Sunbeam throws describe a controller that blinks or shows an F2 fault and a blanket that stops heating; one reviewer reports failure in under four months, and others describe warranty trouble (review summaries in search results, undated).
  • Hot spots and uneven or inconsistent auto-off on a Biddeford model, with a few reports of burns (same kind of summary, undated).
  • Auto-off times that do not match the box. The 8-hour claim on our earlier Bedsure post holds only for some variants: a Walmart listing for the U by Bedsure 50×60 says 3 hours.

The questions people ask before buying

  • Can I wash it? Yes, if the manual says so: unplug it, disconnect the controller, soak and wash cold on a gentle cycle, and plug it in only when it is completely dry (Sunbeam booklet, Bedsure guidebook). Fairfax County Fire says “don’t wash an electric blanket”; the makers’ manuals say otherwise, so follow the manual for your model.
  • How long does it last? Healthline and Medical News Today say replace at about 10 years; ESFI says almost all blanket fires involve blankets over 10 years old (snippet only).
  • Is the “ETL” on the box the same as UL? No. UL says its mark means a sample was tested; many retail listings say “ETL,” a different testing lab. Both are recognised labs, so look for either on the label, not just in the title.

How do you use a heated blanket safely?

Keep it flat and visible, plug it into the wall, and test the auto-off while you are awake before you ever sleep with it.

  1. Match the model number to the recalls above. A recalled blanket is the main danger; do it before the first night.
  2. Look for a listing mark on the label, UL or ETL. UL says its mark means a sample was tested.
  3. Lay it flat on top of the sheet and never tuck it in. Heat builds where it is folded or bunched (Sunbeam booklet; State Farm).
  4. Plug it straight into the wall, not an extension cord or power strip, and keep the controller outside the covers where you can see it.
  5. Test the auto-off while awake. Run it on a setting for the full time on a weekend, and switch it off if the controller gets unusually hot (State Farm; Bedsure guidebook: unplug and let it cool 20–30 minutes).
  6. Preheat, then switch it off. UL Solutions suggests warming the bed for about 30 minutes, then turning it off before you get in.

What do safety experts say?

Fire-safety sources agree on the same four rules: nothing on top, no tucking, no extension cords, and replace damaged or old blankets.

  • State Farm (page dated September 18, 2026): keep the blanket flat, do not tuck it tightly under the mattress, plug it into a wall outlet, test the auto shut-off while awake, and stop using it if the controller gets unusually hot or it is recalled or damaged.
  • UL Solutions (undated page): use blankets approved by a nationally recognised testing laboratory; the UL mark means a representative sample was tested; preheat for about 30 minutes and switch off before getting in; avoid use by infants, paralyzed people and people with incontinence.
  • Healthline (February 2020, medically reviewed) and Medical News Today (March 2021): replace at about 10 years; people with diabetes or neuropathy can burn without feeling it, so warm the bed and switch the blanket off first.
  • Fairfax County Fire and Rescue (undated): do not pile toys, pillows or stuffed animals on top, and turn it off when not in use.

We found no useful electric-blanket page at Consumer Reports or NFPA, and no CPSC tips page, so we cite none of them for safety advice.

What's the catch?

A heated blanket is a small fire risk by design, and “no recent recall” is only as good as the search we could run.

We checked CPSC’s recall listing, which ran to October 22, 2026, and found no 2025–2026 heated-blanket recall for Sunbeam, Bedsure, Beautyrest, Biddeford or Serta. That is a finding, not a guarantee: Bedsure had a 350,000-unit recall in 2023, a Sunbeam-brand queen blanket had one the same month, Biddeford Textile recalled 394,000 blankets in 2001, and “Classic Beautyrest” throws were recalled in 2007. Quick answers

Which heated blankets were recalled in 2026?

The CPSC recalled OCTROT heated sherpa throws and blankets on July 30, 2026 (about 178,000, No. 26-652) and JKMAX heated blankets and heating pads on September 17, 2026 (about 195,000, No. 26-778). It also issued a warning about Melinora blankets on July 16, 2026.

Where is the model number on a heated blanket?

On the back of the controller, on a white label or the wash label on the blanket, or on the packaging. JKMAX’s is on the back of the remote and on the packaging label; OCTROT’s is on the packaging.

What should I do if my heated blanket is recalled?

Unplug it and stop using it. JKMAX offers a refund; OCTROT asks you to cut the cord in half and email a photo before it refunds you. Do not give it away or sell it.

Is the Sunbeam heated throw safe?

We found no 2025–2026 CPSC recall for Sunbeam heated blankets. A single Sunbeam-brand queen blanket, model 32810027, was recalled in March 2023. Safe use still depends on the rules above.

Has Bedsure been recalled?

Yes, in March 2023 (No. 23-735, about 350,000 blankets and pads across seven model numbers). We found no 2025–2026 Bedsure recall. Check your label against the seven models.

How long do heated blankets last?

Healthline and Medical News Today say to replace one at about 10 years, sooner if it is stiff, lumpy, frayed or has a damaged cord or controller. Owner reports suggest the controller can fail much earlier.

How much does a heated blanket cost to run?

At the US average of 18.16 cents per kWh (EIA, August 2026), a 100-watt throw costs at most about 14.5 cents for eight hours. A 1,500-watt space heater costs about $2.18 over the same time.

Can I sleep with a heated blanket on?

Not unless the manual allows it and you have tested the auto-off while awake. UL Solutions suggests preheating the bed and switching the blanket off before you get in. Auto-off times vary from 2 to 10 hours by model.

Who should not use a heated blanket?

Sunbeam’s booklet says not to use its throws with an infant, a child, an incapacitated or immobile person, a diabetic, or someone with poor circulation. UL Solutions adds paralyzed people and people with incontinence.

Can I wash a heated blanket?

Most modern throws can be washed if you disconnect the controller first. Sunbeam and Bedsure both say to unplug it, remove the controller, wash cold and gently, and plug it in only when dry. Follow your model’s manual.
