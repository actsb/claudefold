Vera's 10-second verdict

How much does each way of staying warm cost for one night?

A pad or blanket costs 10 to 40 cents for an 8-hour night at typical prices; a 1,500 W heater costs about $1.40 to $3.60 if it runs flat out.

The formula is the one the U.S. Department of Energy publishes for any appliance: watts divided by 1,000, times hours, gives kilowatt-hours, and kilowatt-hours times your price gives dollars. Consumer Reports uses the same arithmetic when it says a 1,500 W heater on its highest setting costs about $2.10 per 8-hour day on the national average (its page was updated October 12, 2025, with an older price than today's 18.16 cents). We ran it at three prices: 12 cents (cheap-power states), 18 cents (about the US average) and 30 cents (California, Massachusetts and Maine, roughly).

Three things in that table need saying plainly.

  • Heater rows are ceilings. A thermostat switches a heater off when the room reaches the set temperature. We found no Energy.gov, maker or extension figure for how often that happens, so if you want a scenario, halve the heater rows (about $1.09 at the national average). That halving is our assumption, not a measurement.
  • Pad and blanket watts are the soft spot. We found a maker spec for the Dreo (1,500 W, per its manual) and for Pelonis (600, 900 and 1,500 W settings, per the maker's model page as it appeared in search); for the pad we used the 120 W Walmart lists for Sunbeam's Wi-Fi queen pad and the 130 W per controller Target prints for the Biddeford queen pad. Sunbeam does not publish watts for its queen blanket on the pages we could reach, so that row is a labelled assumption (100 to 200 W; retail articles cite 60 to 200 W). Read the label on your controller and rerun the sum.
  • Real pads draw less than their rating. A forum owner who measured a Sunbeam queen pad (model not named) saw each side start around 50 to 65 W and fall below 30 W once warm; a separate Kill-A-Watt test of an unstated size went from 9 W on setting 1 to 43 W on setting 5 (builditsolar.com). Both are anecdotes, so treat the 120 W rows as the safe ceiling.

Electricity prices differ by more than three to one across the mainland. The gap is the story. In Nevada a month of full-power heater nights is about $46; in Massachusetts it is about $110. A pad costs under $9 a month in every state shown except Maine and Hawaii.

Check your model against the CPSC notice: Dreo recalled one heater on October 29, 2026, and Bedsure, Sunbeam-branded and several marketplace blankets have been recalled since 2023.

How do you use a heated pad safely and cheaply?

Preheat it, turn it down once you are in, and turn the heater off before you sleep.

  1. Preheat for about 30 minutes. Some Sunbeam models have a preheat setting; the manual says the pad shuts off after 10 hours.
  2. Drop the setting once warm. Setting 1 to 5 on a builditsolar test went from 9 W to 43 W; lower is cheaper.
  3. Lay it flat under the fitted sheet. Never tuck it so tightly the cord or controller folds; keep pets off.
  4. Inspect each season. Frays, dark spots, a blinking controller or a smell: stop using it.
  5. Turn room heaters off at bedtime. Consumer Reports says to turn off a heater when you leave the room or go to bed; keep it three feet from bedding and plug it straight into the wall.

What do the experts say?

Experts agree that a heater is supplemental and should be off when you sleep; none we could reach publishes a kWh figure for heated bedding.

  • Consumer Reports, "Will Using a Space Heater Save You Money?" (updated October 12, 2025): a 1,500 W heater on high costs about $2.10 per 8-hour day on the national average, and heaters work as supplemental heat; in a small drafty room one could cost less than raising the central heat.
  • Consumer Reports, "How to Use Your Space Heater Safely" (updated October 5, 2025): turn the heater off when you leave the room or go to bed, and look for automatic overheat shut-off.
  • NC State Extension, Camden County, "Space Heater Safety" (Kristi Willard, January 26, 2026): citing NFPA and CPSC, space heaters cause nearly half of home heating fires; keep 3 feet of clearance and plug into the wall.
  • U.S. Department of Energy (page read November 9, 2026): turning the thermostat back 7 to 10°F for eight hours a day can save up to about 10 percent a year on heating and cooling.

What's the catch?

We could not read most makers' watts, so the pad and blanket rows are estimates, and all three product types have recall history.

Three limits. First, the 120 W pad figure is a retailer listing for a Wi-Fi model, not a maker spec for the Restful pad we recommend. Second, we found no non-Amazon owner counts for the Sunbeam pad and blanket, so we cannot say how long they last. Third, a pad warms you but not the room, so if the bedroom is below about 60°F you may still want to preheat it.

  • If a controller fails, the warranty (3 years in Sunbeam's manual, 5 years in retailer listings) is the reason to buy from a retailer that takes returns.
  • If your bill is already low (Nevada, Louisiana, Tennessee), the savings are small and comfort should decide.
  • If you rent a drafty apartment, a draft stopper may beat all of this.

Quick answers

How much does it cost to run a heated mattress pad all night?

About 17 cents at the national average price of 18.16 cents per kWh if a queen pad pulls 120 W for eight hours (the rating Walmart lists for Sunbeam's Wi-Fi queen pad). Pads draw less once warm, so most nights cost less. A pad rated 130 W per controller, like the Biddeford at Target, tops out near 37 cents at that price.

Is an electric blanket cheaper than a space heater?

Yes, by a wide margin, for the same night: roughly 10 to 30 cents against about $2 for a 1,500 W heater at the national average. They do different jobs, though. A blanket warms you; a heater warms the air.

How much does a 1,500 W space heater cost per hour?

At 18.16 cents per kWh, 1.5 kWh costs about 27 cents an hour at full power. Consumer Reports puts a 1,500 W heater on high at about $2.10 per 8-hour day on the national average, which matches our $2.18 (it used an older price).

Does a space heater really run at full power all night?

A thermostat switches it off when the room reaches the set temperature, so it often draws less than the rating. We could not find a published duty-cycle figure from Energy.gov or a maker, so we show full power as the ceiling and half as a scenario that is our assumption.

Should I sleep with a space heater on?

Consumer Reports says to turn a heater off when you leave the room or go to bed, and NC State Extension, citing NFPA and CPSC, says space heaters cause nearly half of home heating fires. Warm the room before bed and use a pad or blanket for the night.

Is it safe to leave a heated blanket on all night?

Current Sunbeam pads and blankets shut off after 10 hours of continuous use (3 hours for throws), per Sunbeam's manual we read on Nov 9, 2026. Check the cord and controller for frays or dark spots, never tuck the blanket under the mattress, and stop using it if the controller blinks or smells hot.

Does the heated throw cost less than the pad?

About the same: a 100 W throw is 14 cents for eight hours at 18 cents per kWh. But most listings show a 3-hour auto-off, so it cannot run a full night unrestarted, which makes a throw a sofa product.

Which brands of heated blanket have been recalled?

CPSC has recalled Bedsure heated blankets and pads (March 2023, about 350,000 units, 17 burn injuries), Sunbeam-branded queen blankets distributed by Star Elite (March 2023, model 32810027), and in 2026 JKMAX (about 195,000 units, sold on Amazon), OCTROT and Melinora (a warning). Check the model on CPSC.gov before you plug one in.

Which Dreo heater was recalled?

The DR-HSH015 black ceramic heater with a remote, recalled Oct 29, 2026 (CPSC 27-054, about 338,800 US units, 17 ignition reports). Our Dreo Atom One review covers the DR-HSH004, which is not named in the notice. Check the model label on the underside of your heater.

Does turning down the thermostat save more than any of these?

Energy.gov says turning your thermostat back 7 to 10°F for eight hours a day can save up to about 10 percent a year on heating and cooling. It also helps to dress and layer, so a pad or blanket plus a cooler bedroom is the pairing this guide's numbers support.
