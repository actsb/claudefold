The 10 best smart glasses of 2026

Ranked by the job they do, because a runner, a traveller who can't read the menu, and someone who wants a cinema on a red-eye are shopping for three different products. Every pick lists the list price and the sale price we've seen; several are cheaper than list right now, and two are about to be superseded.

Best AI camera glasses Ray-Ban Meta (Gen 2)

IMAGE SLOT — Amazon SiteStripe → Image → Ray-Ban Meta Gen 2 Wayfarer, then delete this box.
Price: from $379; $459 with Transitions; Optics Rx frames from $499
Battery: 8 h mixed use; case +48 h, 50% in 20 min
Camera: 12MP; 3K/30, 1440p/30, 1200p/60 video; clips up to 3 min (5 via update)
AI: Meta AI with vision, live translation, Live AI (~30 min)
Styles: Wayfarer, Headliner, Skyler; low-bridge fit available
Weight / rating: ~52 g; IPX4

The Gen 2 is the pair most people should buy in this category and the reason is simple: it is the only camera glasses with a finished ecosystem. The eight-hour battery finally lasts a working day, the 3K camera is the best of any glasses (still "phone-adjacent," but the best), the open-ear speakers are the class of the field, and the software does things nobody else's does — video-call from your point of view on WhatsApp, livestream to Instagram, tap for Spotify, ask "Hey Meta, what am I looking at" and get an answer that is usually right. They're sold and fitted at LensCrafters, Sunglass Hut and Target Optical, prescription support is the widest, and Android Police's "so much fun you'll ignore the red flags" is the consensus.

The red flags: the data policy (voice recordings stored by default, captures used for training unless you disable the wake word), a conservative AI that declines more than it should, features that arrive by region-gated rollout months after they're announced, and the growing list of places you'll be asked to remove them. And the timing: Meta Connect is September 23–24 and Gen 3 — leaked with a faster chip and Live AI that runs for hours instead of 30 minutes — is expected to be announced, with Gen 2 discounts to follow. Unless you need them this week, wait ten days.

Pros

  • 8-hour battery, 48-hour case
  • Best camera and speakers in the category
  • POV video calls, livestreaming, translation
  • Widest prescription and retail support
  • Frames people already want to wear

Cons

  • Privacy defaults favour Meta
  • AI says no too often; Live AI ~30 min
  • Phased feature rollouts
  • Gen 3 expected September 23–24

Most common owner complaint: "the update didn't give me the new features" (server-side rollouts). Who should skip it: anyone whose week includes a gym, court, clinic or cinema; privacy-first buyers (Even G2); Android owners who can wait for Android XR.

Best display glasses Meta Ray-Ban Display

IMAGE SLOT — Amazon SiteStripe → Image → Meta Ray-Ban Display.
Price: $799 including the Neural Band; US only (Canada, France, Italy, UK following)
Display: 600×600 full colour, 20° FOV, right lens, up to 5,000 nits
Battery: 6 h mixed; case to 30 h; Neural Band 18 h, IPX7
Weight: ~69 g
Camera / storage: 12MP; 32 GB, 2 GB RAM, Wi-Fi 6, Bluetooth 5.3
Prescription: −4.00 to +4.00 only

This is the product that made every reviewer sit up: a real colour display floating in the right lens — readable in direct sunlight — showing turn-by-turn directions, live captions and translations, messages, a camera viewfinder and Meta AI's answers, controlled by a wristband that reads the electrical signals in your forearm. Pinch to select, twitch a finger to scroll, write a reply on your leg with your fingertip. Tom's Guide: "the display is easy to read, and the neural band works well." Bloomberg's question — "should I buy Meta's new $799 glasses with a screen?" — got a qualified yes for early adopters and a no for everyone else, and AR Compare's 7.4 consensus reflects that split.

The reasons to hesitate are concrete. Six hours of mixed use; 69 grams that reviewers felt after two hours; a display in one eye only, which some find fatiguing; a visible ripple in the right lens; a −4 to +4 prescription range that excludes a lot of people; $799; and, for now, US-only sales through a demo-first retail process. A binocular "Hypernova 2" is reported for 2027. If you want the screen today and can live with those, nothing else is this polished; if you want the screen without the Meta account, the Rokid Glasses cost $200 less.

Pros

  • Full-colour display, sunlight-readable
  • Neural Band is the best input device in wearables
  • Maps, captions, translation, viewfinder
  • Meta's mature app and AI

Cons

  • $799; US only
  • 6-hour battery; 69 g
  • Monocular; lens ripple
  • −4/+4 prescription limit

Most common owner complaint: battery and weight. Who should skip it: prescriptions outside ±4, anyone outside the US, and anyone who wants to watch video (that's XR glasses).

Best value display glasses Rokid Glasses

IMAGE SLOT — Amazon SiteStripe → Image → Rokid Glasses (display model).
Price: $599 (Prime Day discounts seen)
Display: green micro-LED waveguide in both lenses, 480×398, 23° FOV
Weight: 49 g
Camera: 12MP Sony sensor
AI: ChatGPT or Gemini (native Gemini since March 2026)
Prescription: available from Rokid

These are the glasses from the exam video, and the reason that video works is the display: text floats in both lenses, sharp enough to read a translation, a direction or — yes — a solved equation without anyone across the table noticing. Tom's Guide called them "a real Meta Ray-Ban Display rival" and "more feature-packed than competitors," with a bright screen, good speakers and a customisable multi-modal AI; WearableBeat's summary — "a display, a camera, and AI in one normal-looking pair" at 49 grams — is what Meta charges $200 more and 20 more grams for. Picking ChatGPT for logic and Gemini for "what can I cook with this" is a small feature owners love.

What you give up: colour (the display is green only, which is divisive), a camera that struggles in low light, styling that Trusted Reviews called "none of the style," and the comfort of a household name — Rokid is a decade-old AR company with a huge Kickstarter base, not Luxottica. Tom's Guide's month-long tester also wrote that the "bad vibes" around wearables faded but the data worry didn't; Rokid routes your queries through OpenAI or Google. Prescription lenses are available directly.

Best camera-free Even Realities G2

IMAGE SLOT — Amazon SiteStripe → Image → Even Realities G2.
Price: $599; R1 ring controller optional
Display: monochrome green waveguide HUD, both lenses
Camera / speakers: none / none
Battery: about 48 h
Software: translation, navigation, teleprompter, meeting notes, Even AI
Prescription: yes; frames pass as normal glasses

Even Realities made the opposite bet to Meta: a heads-up display with no camera and no speakers, in frames that look like nothing at all. The result is the pair you can wear into a courtroom, a gym, a clinic or a first date. The text floats crisply in both lenses; the software is built for real tasks — live translation of what's being said, walking directions, a teleprompter for a talk, notes from a meeting, a quick AI answer — and the battery is measured in days, not hours. Tom's Guide: "the future of smart glasses I want (with a smart ring)"; Android Police: "made my smartwatch feel obsolete"; AR Compare consensus 8.0. It is also the only display pair here with no privacy asterisk.

It can't take a photo, play music or make a call, and $599 is a lot for a productivity accessory from a small company. But if the reason you're curious about smart glasses is glanceable information rather than capture, this is the most grown-up product in the category.

Most common owner complaint: wanting audio. Who should skip it: anyone who wants a camera or music.

Best for movies & gaming Xreal One Pro

IMAGE SLOT — Amazon SiteStripe → Image → XREAL One Pro.
Price: $599 (permanent cut from $649, April 2026)
Display: dual 0.55" Sony micro-OLED, 1080p, 120 Hz, 57° FOV; ultrawide 3,840×1,080 on PC
Chip: X1 — native 3DoF anchoring, no puck needed
Weight: 87 g; Bose-tuned audio
Works with: iPhone 15+, USB-C Android, Steam Deck, ROG Ally, Mac, Windows; Switch 2 via Xreal Hub
Prescription: snap-in inserts

Plug the One Pro into a phone, laptop or handheld and a 1080p screen the apparent size of a cinema wall floats in front of you; the X1 chip pins it in place so you can turn your head, and on a PC it becomes a 3,840×1,080 ultrawide. Tom's Guide flew 10,912 miles with them and called them the best AR glasses, "at a steep price"; Engadget said they "intrigue me in ways Meta's smart glasses don't"; the flat-prism optics are 40% smaller than the previous generation. For a red-eye, a hotel room, a Steam Deck in bed, or a laptop in a coffee shop with no room for a monitor, nothing here comes close.

Limits: 87 grams on your nose (the top comfort complaint); a tether; a compatibility maze — Lightning iPhones can't drive them, the Switch 2 needs the Hub, and older TVs need an adapter; and no AI or camera at all. The cheaper Xreal One and the new 1S give up FOV and some tricks for less money; the Beam Pro puck makes any of them standalone.

Most common owner complaint: weight and nose pressure after an hour. Who should skip it: anyone with an iPhone 14 or older, and anyone shopping for AI glasses.

Best XR under $500 Viture Luma Pro

IMAGE SLOT — Amazon SiteStripe → Image → VITURE Luma Pro.
Price: $499
Display: Sony micro-OLED, 1200p, 52° FOV, electrochromic dimming
Fit: built-in myopia dials; among the lightest XR frames
Anchoring / ultrawide: via Viture app or dock

Where Xreal wins on features, Viture wins on the picture: the Luma Pro's 1200p panel is "razor sharp," the colour is "mesmerizing," the electrochromic lenses go dark at a tap for a private screen in a bright room, and the fit is the most comfortable in the category. Android Authority's title says it: "the best XR glasses for under $500." What you don't get is the X1 chip — screen anchoring and the ultrawide mode need Viture's dock or app — and Tom's Guide found the Pro a "middle child" between the base Luma and the Luma Ultra. If you'll mostly watch and play rather than work, this is the better $500.

Who should skip it: multi-monitor productivity users (Xreal One Pro).

Best for sports Oakley Meta Vanguard (and the everyday Oakley Meta HSTN)

IMAGE SLOT — Amazon SiteStripe → Image → Oakley Meta Vanguard.
Vanguard: $499; 9 h; IP67; centred 12MP camera; Prizm wraparound lenses; Garmin and Strava
HSTN: $399; 8 h; 3K camera; Transitions available; everyday Oakley styling

The Vanguard is "Ray-Ban Meta on steroids" (Android Central): the longest battery Meta makes, the loudest speakers, an IP67 rating that shrugs off sweat and rain, a camera centred over the nose for true POV footage, and integration with Garmin watches and Strava so a ride's stats overlay your clips. The Run Testers and Engadget liked it as a sports tool and agreed on the limit — "sporty to a fault" — because a wraparound with Prizm lenses isn't a pair you wear to dinner. The HSTN is the compromise: the same electronics as the Ray-Ban Gen 2 in an Oakley frame you can wear all day.

Who should skip it: anyone who wants one pair for everything (Ray-Ban Gen 2 or HSTN).

Gizmodo called them "surprisingly tough competition for Meta's Ray-Ban," and at $80–$110 less they deliver most of the same job — a good 12MP camera, open-ear audio, hands-free ChatGPT or Gemini, translation — with a prescription-first design and, per Notebookcheck, "few shortcomings." Cheapest we'd buy Amazon Echo Frames (3rd Gen)

IMAGE SLOT — Amazon SiteStripe → Image → Amazon Echo Frames (newest model).
Price: $269.99 list; $120–$170 in sales
What they do: Alexa+, calls, music, notifications via open-ear audio; no camera, no display
Styles: multiple frames; blue-light and prescription-ready lenses
Battery: about 6 h of talk, 14 h mixed

They are what they say: normal glasses with Alexa in the temples. Tom's Guide's "Alexa on my face, everywhere I go" is affectionate and accurate; the third generation brought better audio, thinner temples and more styles, and they are prescription-ready. No camera means no bans, no data pipeline and nothing to explain. The Carrera versions ($389.99) put the same electronics in Italian frames. At their frequent $120–$170 sale price they are the least risky smart glasses you can buy.

Who should skip it: anyone who wants the AI to see anything.

Best clearance buy Ray-Ban Meta (Gen 1)

Price: was $299; now $224–$239 at Amazon, Target Optical and Best Buy
Trade-offs vs Gen 2: ~4 h battery, 1080p video, older camera; same AI and app

Being cleared out to make room for Gen 2 and Gen 3, the original Ray-Ban Meta does everything the Gen 2 does with the same Meta AI, in the same frames, for $150 less — the catch being a four-hour battery that owners complained about from day one, and 1080p video. For a first pair, or for someone who'll use them for a few photos and calls rather than all-day AI, the price is right. Expect it to fall further after September 24.
