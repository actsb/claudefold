Brand by brand: what each one is actually good at

Meta (Ray-Ban, Oakley) — the category leader, with a privacy bill attached

Lineup: Ray-Ban Meta Gen 1 (2023, $299, now $224–$239 on clearance); Ray-Ban Meta Gen 2 ($379–$459) in Wayfarer, Headliner and Skyler styles plus the prescription-first Blayzer and Scriber Optics frames ($499); Oakley Meta HSTN ($399); Oakley Meta Vanguard ($499); Meta Ray-Ban Display ($799) with the Neural Band. Strengths: the most complete product — camera, five-mic array, open-ear speakers that are the best in the category, Meta AI with vision, live translation, WhatsApp and Messenger video calling from your point of view, Instagram live-streaming — in frames people already want to wear, sold and serviced through LensCrafters, Sunglass Hut and Target Optical with proper prescription support. The Display's Neural Band is the most impressive input device any reviewer has tried this year. Weaknesses: privacy defaults that favour Meta (see the checklist), a conservative AI, 30-minute Live AI, phased feature rollouts by region, and the Display's weight, monocular ripple, tight −4/+4 prescription range and US-only availability. Owner complaints: features "not showing up after the update," battery on Gen 1, being asked to remove them, and the AI declining requests. Buy Meta if you want the best-supported camera glasses and you've made peace with the data policy — after September 24.

A deliberately critical look at the Ray-Ban Meta Gen 2 — the counterweight to the launch-week enthusiasm.

Rokid — the display rival that beat Google to Gemini

Lineup: Rokid Glasses ($599; binocular green display, camera, AI); Rokid AI Glasses Style ($299, no display); Rokid AR Lite / AR Spatial (tethered XR, ~$500–$600). Strengths: Tom's Guide called the Rokid Glasses "a real Meta Ray-Ban Display rival" — a legible AR display in both lenses, a usable 12MP Sony camera and switchable ChatGPT/Gemini in a 49-gram frame you forget you're wearing; a March 2026 update made them the first glasses to run Gemini natively. The Style is "surprisingly tough competition for Meta's Ray-Ban" (Gizmodo) at $100 less, with a prescription-first approach. Rokid's Kickstarter passed $25 million, and the glasses are on Amazon US with Prime Day discounts. Weaknesses: the green-only display divides opinion; the camera is weak in low light; Trusted Reviews' verdict on the styling was "all of the smarts with none of the style"; the Style has proprietary charging, no battery case, a slightly flimsy build, no video calling and a bridge that fatigues some noses; and Tom's Guide's month-long tester "couldn't stop worrying about my personal data." Buy Rokid if you want a screen in both eyes, or Gemini/ChatGPT rather than Meta AI, at hundreds less than Meta.

Even Realities — the anti-Meta

Lineup: Even G2 ($599) with the optional R1 ring controller; the original G1 discounted. Strengths: no camera, no speakers — just a sharp monochrome waveguide HUD in frames that pass for ordinary prescription glasses, about 48 hours of battery, and software focused on translation, navigation, a teleprompter, meeting notes and quick AI answers. Tom's Guide called it "the future of smart glasses I want (with a smart ring)"; Android Police said it "made my smartwatch feel obsolete"; Gizmodo: "anti-Meta smart glasses with real potential." Weaknesses: no capture at all, a small company, and a $599 price for a productivity niche. Buy Even if you want the heads-up display without the surveillance — and you'll never be asked to take them off.

Xreal — the private-cinema leader

Lineup: Xreal One Pro ($599, cut from $649 in April), Xreal One (less), the newer 1S, Air 2 Pro (older, cheaper), the Beam Pro Android puck and the Hub/Neo Hub adapters. Strengths: the One Pro's 1080p micro-OLED across 57°, 120 Hz, Bose-tuned audio, and the X1 chip that anchors the screen in space (3DoF) with no extra hardware and simulates a 3,840×1,080 ultrawide on a PC. Tom's Guide flew 10,912 miles with them and called them the best. AR Compare consensus: 8.0/10. Weaknesses: 87 g on your nose, a price that "limits appeal" (Tom's Hardware), and the compatibility maze — iPhone 15+, DP-Alt-Mode devices only, Switch 2 via Hub. Buy Xreal if you fly, game on a handheld, or work in a space too small for a monitor.

Viture — the picture-first alternative

Lineup: Luma ($399), Luma Pro ($499), Luma Ultra (more). Strengths: a 1200p Sony micro-OLED panel across 52° that reviewers call "razor sharp," electrochromic lenses that dim instantly for a private screen in a bright room, built-in myopia adjustment dials, and the most comfortable fit in XR. Android Authority: "the best XR glasses for under $500." Weaknesses: fewer software tricks than Xreal's X1-chip models — anchoring and ultrawide need Viture's dock or app — and Tom's Guide's "middle child" verdict on the Pro's place in the range. Buy Viture if picture quality and comfort matter more than features.

Google & Samsung (Android XR) — this fall

Shown at I/O in May with Warby Parker ("refined and timeless") and Gentle Monster ("disruptive") frames: an audio-only pair and a display pair, both companions to an Android phone with Gemini for translation, navigation and notification summaries. Samsung says fall 2026; pricing is unannounced. If you're on a Pixel or Galaxy and can wait, wait — these will be the Meta alternative with the best assistant, and Rokid's Gemini glasses are the stopgap.

Amazon (Echo Frames) and Carrera — audio glasses, done properly

Amazon's third-generation Echo Frames ($269.99 list; $120–$170 in sales) put Alexa+ and open-ear audio in prescription-ready frames with no camera; Tom's Guide's "Alexa on my face, everywhere I go." Carrera's Cruiser and Sprinter ($389.99) are the same electronics in Italian designer frames. Neither has a camera or an AI that can see, which is exactly why they sit on Amazon's best-seller list next to the Ray-Bans: they're the glasses you can wear anywhere. Buy them if you want calls and music in glasses and nothing that makes anyone nervous.

Halliday, Solos, Brilliant Labs — the interesting fringe

Halliday ($489) projects a tiny "DigiWindow" display from a module above the right eye; Android Police called it a "buzzword-laden curiosity I stopped wanting to wear." Solos AirGo V ($299) adds a camera and GPT-4o to audio glasses with swappable fronts. Brilliant Labs Halo is the open-source, on-device pair for tinkerers. None is a first pair; all are why the category will look different in two years.

The $17–$40 "AI smart glasses" — skip

They dominate Amazon's search results and The Street's deal posts. Reviewers who tested them found 2K-at-best cameras, translation that works when the app works, no firmware updates and no clarity about where the footage goes. The Rokid Style at $269–$299 or Echo Frames at $120–$170 on sale are the floor.
