The ten things that actually matter when you buy smart glasses

Roughly in the order they appear as the reason for a return.

1. The kind — and the job you'll actually give them

Ask what you'll do with them on a Tuesday, not at a demo. Hands-free photos of your kids, a quick "what is this building," music on a walk, calls while cooking: kind 1, camera glasses. Reading a translation or the next turn without pulling out your phone, captions in a loud bar, a teleprompter for a presentation: kind 2, display glasses. Watching a film on a flight or gaming on a Steam Deck in bed: kind 3, XR glasses. Nobody has built one pair that does all three well, and the products that try (display glasses that stream video) do the third job badly.

2. The display: monocular vs binocular, colour vs green, and field of view

Display glasses use a waveguide etched into the lens. The Meta Ray-Ban Display is monocular (right eye only), full colour, 600×600 pixels across a 20° field of view, and bright enough for direct sunlight at up to 5,000 nits; the trade-off is a visible ripple in the right lens and a 69 g weight. Rokid Glasses are binocular (both eyes, which many people find less fatiguing), green-only micro-LED at 480×398 across 23°, and 49 g; the green-only readout is divisive but legible. Even Realities G2 is binocular, green, and the most discreet. XR glasses are a different technology entirely — birdbath or flat-prism optics with 1080p–1200p micro-OLED panels across 52–57°, which is why they look like a cinema and the others look like a smartwatch. None of the display AI glasses is for video; all of the XR glasses are.

3. Camera and recording: "phone-adjacent," with limits

Every camera here is 12MP and every reviewer describes the results the same way: fine in daylight, grainy at dinner, phone-adjacent rather than phone-quality. What differs is video — the Gen 2 and Oakley models shoot 3K at 30 fps or 1080p at 60, in clips capped at three minutes (five after a phased update) — and the placement: the Vanguard's camera sits centred over the nose for a true POV, where the Ray-Ban's is in the left corner and catches your hair. All Meta glasses light a front LED while recording; it is small, and bystanders in bright light don't see it. Rokid's Style records with no video-calling support; the Even G2 has no camera at all, which in 2026 is a feature.

4. The AI: whose, how good, and for how long

Meta glasses run Meta AI, which is conservative — reviewers and owners report a lot of "I can't help with that" — but deeply integrated: it sees what you see, reads signs, translates speech in a handful of languages, and controls the camera and music by voice. Rokid lets you switch between ChatGPT and Gemini, and owners praise picking the model for the task. Even's assistant is ChatGPT-based and text-first. Android XR glasses will run Gemini. Two limits apply to all of them: Live AI — the continuous "watch and answer" mode — runs about 30 minutes on Meta hardware before the battery collapses (the Gen 3 rumours are all about extending it to hours), and every assistant gets worse in noise and with accents. The exam video worked because the questions were printed clearly on a desk in a quiet room.

5. Battery: eight hours is the new floor, six is the display tax

Gen 2 Ray-Bans and Oakley HSTN run eight hours of mixed use, the Vanguard nine, the Display six, the Rokid Style "impressive" in reviews (Rokid doesn't publish a clean figure), and the camera-free Even G2 around 48. Every figure assumes short AI queries; video and Live AI cut them to an hour or two. The charging case is not optional — the Ray-Ban case adds 48 hours and charges the glasses to 50% in 20 minutes — and the Rokid Style's lack of a battery case is a genuine drawback owners mention. XR glasses have no battery; they run off the device, and will drain a phone or Steam Deck noticeably faster.

6. Weight, fit and the bridge of your nose

Regular glasses weigh 20–30 g. Rokid Glasses (49 g) and the Ray-Ban Gen 2 (~52 g) are wearable all day for most people; the Display (69 g) and the XR glasses (80–90 g, Xreal One Pro 87 g) draw comfort complaints after two hours, and reviewers say so. Nose fit matters as much as weight: Ray-Ban offers low-bridge-fit Wayfarers, which matter for many Asian and flat-bridge faces; Rokid Style owners report bridge fatigue; XR glasses ship with swappable nose pads. If you can, try them on — LensCrafters, Sunglass Hut, Target Optical and Best Buy stock Meta glasses.

7. Prescription: the range and the real price

Most buyers need lenses, and this is where the price on the box stops being the price. Meta's Display supports only −4.00 to +4.00 total power; Ray-Ban Meta supports a broad range and in March 2026 Meta launched two "built-for-prescription" frames (Blayzer Optics and Scriber Optics, from $499). Getting lenses in: Ray-Ban/LensCrafters and an optometrist typically run $600–$800 for the finished pair with anti-reflective coating; retrofit services (UseMyFrame, VR Optician, RX-able, Lensology, LensDirect, VR Wave) fit lenses to a standard Wayfarer for $45–$210 depending on coatings, Transitions and polarisation. Rokid and Even sell prescription-ready frames; XR glasses take snap-in inserts (about $50–$100) or, on the Viture, have built-in focus dials for mild myopia. Progressives are hit-or-miss on all of them.

8. Privacy and data — what you're agreeing to

Read this before the checkout page. On Meta glasses the wake word keeps the microphones — and the camera-for-AI feature — always ready unless you turn it off; voice recordings are stored by default with no opt-out (deletable manually); and photos and video used with Meta AI can be used to train Meta's models. Rokid routes queries through OpenAI or Google under their policies; Even's G2 has no camera and keeps its footprint small. Then there is everyone around you: the recording LED is easy to miss, and the list of places that will ask you to remove camera glasses now includes courts, cinemas, gyms, clinics, casinos, cruise ships, exam halls and a growing number of workplaces. We give the day-one settings checklist in the tips section.

9. Phone compatibility

Meta, Rokid and Even glasses work with iPhone and Android through their apps; Android XR glasses will be Android-first. XR glasses are the trap: they need USB-C DisplayPort output, which means iPhone 15 or later (Lightning iPhones can't), most flagship Android phones, Steam Deck, ROG Ally, MacBooks and Windows laptops with DP-Alt-Mode ports — and the Nintendo Switch 2, which dropped direct glasses support, needs Xreal's Hub or Neo Hub. Xreal's Beam Pro ($199+) is an Android puck that makes any pair standalone.

10. Who will still support them in 2028

Meta ships features by phased, server-side rollout — Gen 2 owners waited months for promised recording modes — but it ships them, and it will be here. Rokid is a decade-old Chinese AR company with a big Kickstarter following and a growing US presence; Xreal and Viture are established; Even Realities is small but well reviewed; Halliday, Solos and Brilliant Labs are crowdfunded or niche. Amazon's Echo Frames have been through three generations. The $17 ChatGPT glasses will not have an app next year. Buy the ecosystem, not just the frames.
