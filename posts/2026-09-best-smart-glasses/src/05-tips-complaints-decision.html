How to set up, use, and not get thrown out of places

Smart glasses have a steeper "week two" than any gadget we cover: the novelty fades, the battery habit isn't formed, the AI has said no three times, and someone at the gym has said something. Here is how owners who kept wearing theirs got past it.

Day one: the privacy settings (Meta glasses)

  1. Decide about "Hey Meta." The wake word keeps the microphones listening and enables Meta AI's camera use by default. If you'd rather capture only by pressing the button, turn the wake word off in the Meta AI app (Devices → your glasses → Voice). You lose hands-free, you gain certainty.
  2. Voice recordings. Stored by default for up to a year with no opt-out; you can delete them in the app's voice-history settings, and you can make that a monthly habit.
  3. Data for AI training. Review every data-sharing toggle under Settings → Privacy in the Meta AI app, particularly anything about Meta AI with camera and improving Meta's products. The defaults favour Meta; change what you're not comfortable with.
  4. Assume a human may see what you send. The 2026 lawsuit alleges contractors reviewed footage. Don't ask the AI to look at bank statements, medical documents, or people who haven't consented.
  5. Set the capture LED expectation. It's small. Tell the people you're with when you're recording; ask before recording indoors; never in bathrooms, locker rooms, clinics or anywhere with an expectation of privacy. This is also the law in many states for audio.

Getting good results from the AI

  • Frame the question like a camera shot. "Hey Meta, look and tell me what this sign says" works when the sign fills the view and is lit; it fails on a menu in a dim restaurant. Move closer, hold still for a second.
  • Use Live AI in bursts. It runs continuously and drains the battery in about 30 minutes on current Meta hardware. Start it for the museum room, stop it after.
  • Translation: download the language packs in advance (Meta supports a handful — Spanish, French, Italian, Portuguese among them — offline); Rokid and Even rely on the phone connection. Speak in short sentences; it's interpretation, not subtitles.
  • Rokid owners: keep ChatGPT as the default for factual and logic questions and switch to Gemini for anything involving your Google account, places or recipes — the combination is what reviewers praised.
  • Accents and noise hurt every assistant. Lower your voice, speak toward the temple mic, and repeat once; three failures means the room, not you.

Battery habits

  • Put the glasses in the case whenever they're off your face. The Ray-Ban case adds 48 hours and hits 50% in 20 minutes; the Display's case brings it to 30; the Rokid Style has no case, so carry the cable.
  • Video and Live AI are the drains; photos and music barely register. A day of photos and calls is realistic; a day of video is not.
  • Heat kills the small cell: no dashboards, no direct sun on the case.

Prescription lenses without overpaying

  • Get your current prescription and pupillary distance from your optometrist in writing.
  • Meta glasses: if your Rx is inside the range, order lenses at checkout (Ray-Ban/Meta), at LensCrafters, or use a retrofit service (UseMyFrame, VR Optician, RX-able, Lensology, LensDirect, VR Wave) for $45–$210 into a frame you already own. Transitions and polarised are available either way. The Display's ±4.00 limit is hard.
  • Rokid and Even: order prescription frames directly from the maker.
  • XR glasses: buy the insert frame and have any optician fill it, or use the Viture's dials if you're mildly short-sighted.
  • Progressives: ask the service specifically; results vary and returns are limited once lenses are cut.

Getting the most from the camera

  • Photos import to the phone over Wi-Fi through the app; 3K clips take minutes, so do it while charging.
  • For video calls from your point of view, WhatsApp and Messenger on Meta glasses; the other person sees what you see — great for a walk-through of a house or a bike repair.
  • Clip limits: three minutes on Gen 2 (five after the rolling update). Say "Hey Meta, start recording" and it stops itself.
  • Low light: don't. Every reviewer found the same grain.

XR glasses: making them work with your devices

  • Check for USB-C DisplayPort Alt Mode before you buy: iPhone 15 and later yes, Lightning iPhones no; most flagship Android phones yes, most mid-range no; Steam Deck and ROG Ally yes; MacBooks and most modern Windows laptops yes.
  • Nintendo Switch 2 removed direct support — budget for the Xreal Hub or Neo Hub; the Xreal Beam Pro puck makes any pair standalone and adds 3DoF anchoring to older models.
  • Set the IPD/nose pad and the brightness first; use the electrochromic dimming (Viture) or the included light shield (Xreal) on planes.
  • Take a break every hour: 80–90 g on your nose is the top comfort complaint.

Travelling with smart glasses: airports, planes and roaming

  • Security screening. Smart glasses go through the X-ray like any other electronics; TSA and most EU airports do not ask you to remove them from your face at the body scanner, but a few agents will, so have the case in an outside pocket. Nobody in the owner threads reports a pair being confiscated, and the batteries (under 1 Wh in every camera pair, about 2 Wh in the Display) are far below the 100 Wh carry-on limit.
  • In flight. Bluetooth to your own phone is allowed once the cabin crew permit devices; recording other passengers is where complaints come from, and several US carriers now write "no recording of crew or passengers" into their contract of carriage. XR glasses are the one category that is better on a plane than anywhere else: a 120-inch virtual screen in an economy seat is the single most repeated "worth it" comment in the Xreal and Viture reviews.
  • Charging abroad. The charging cases are USB-C and accept any 5 V charger; the Display's folding case takes a normal 20 W phone brick. Pack a short cable — the cases do not charge from a laptop's low-power port reliably.
  • Language and translation. Live translation on the Ray-Ban Meta works offline for the language packs you download at home (English, Spanish, French, Italian, Portuguese and German at the time of writing); the Rokid and Even pairs need a data connection, so buy an eSIM before you land or the headline feature is dead on arrival.
  • Local law. Recording in public is legal in the US and most of Europe; two-party-consent rules for audio apply in twelve US states and in Germany, and Korea now treats covert exam-hall recording as a criminal offence. When in doubt, the LED that shows you are recording is the etiquette that keeps you out of trouble.

Where you'll be asked to take them off

Courts (New York statewide since July 20, 2026, and many federal courthouses), cinemas, gyms, spas and pools, casinos, cruise ships, hospitals and clinics, schools and exam halls, secure workplaces, DEF CON. Some airlines and venues now ask at the door. The graceful move is to carry the case and switch to regular glasses without being asked — or to buy camera-free glasses (Even G2, Echo Frames, XR glasses) if that's most of your week.

What owners complain about: the review deep-dive

We tallied the recurring themes across the recent one-, two- and three-star Amazon reviews, Meta's community forums and the long-term Reddit threads for every pair above.

1. "Dead by mid-afternoon"

The dominant Gen 1 complaint and still the top one on the Display. Gen 2 fixed it for camera glasses; nothing fixes it for heavy Live AI or video use. What helps: the case habit, treating Live AI as a burst tool, and buying Gen 2/HSTN/Vanguard rather than Gen 1 if you'll use the AI a lot. Gen 3's leaked headline is hours of Live AI — the reason to wait.

2. "The AI won't / can't"

Meta AI declines requests that seem harmless, misreads text in poor light, and mishears in noise. Rokid's model switch draws praise; Even's text answers are reliable but narrow. What helps: framing, light, quiet — and lowered expectations. It is a very good assistant for signs, objects, quick facts and translation, not a replacement for your phone.

3. "The feature isn't there"

Meta unlocks features by phased, region-gated, account-by-account rollout. Gen 2 owners spent months waiting for five-minute clips and 1080p60; forum threads document firmware versions that show nothing new. Kickstarter brands (Rokid, Halliday, Brilliant) ship late. What helps: buy for what the glasses do today.

4. "My prescription isn't supported" / "the lenses cost as much as the glasses"

The Display's ±4.00 limit and progressives are the recurring disappointments; $200–$400 for lenses is the recurring surprise. What helps: check the range before buying, and use a retrofit service for standard frames.

5. "Everyone can hear my call"

Open-ear speakers leak by design; the Vanguard's louder speakers leak most. What helps: volume under 60% indoors, and knowing that in a quiet office the person next to you hears the tinny half of your podcast.

6. "They hurt after two hours"

The Display (69 g) and XR glasses (80–90 g) draw most of these; the Rokid Style's bridge and the low-bridge-fit question draw the rest. What helps: trying them on, low-bridge variants, nose-pad swaps, and hourly breaks with XR.

7. "I was asked to remove them"

Rising fastest in 2026 reviews, from gyms and courts to a friend's house. What helps: a case in your pocket, the LED conversation up front, and camera-free glasses if it keeps happening.

8. "Grainy photos, slow transfers"

Universal. What helps: daylight, and importing over Wi-Fi while the glasses charge.
