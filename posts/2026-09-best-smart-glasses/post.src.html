Quick answers

Should I wait for Meta Connect on September 23–24?

If you want Meta camera glasses, yes. The Gen 3 is expected, and the Gen 2 will be discounted either way. The Rokid, Even, Xreal and Viture pairs aren't due a refresh — buy those whenever.

Do they work with an iPhone?

The camera and display glasses all do, through their apps. XR glasses need a phone with video out over USB-C — iPhone 15 and later work; older Lightning iPhones don't.

Is it legal to record people?

In public in the US, generally yes; a recording light shows when you're filming. Indoors, ask. Courts, gyms, cinemas, casinos, clinics and exam halls ban them, and New York banned them from every courtroom this year.

Can I get prescription lenses?

Yes for every pair here, from the maker or a retrofit service. Budget $45–$210 for retrofit lenses, more at an optician. The Meta Display only fits −4 to +4.

Are the $20–$40 "AI glasses" on Amazon any good?

No. Reviewers found weak cameras, apps that stop working and no answer on where your footage goes. The floor for a real pair is the Rokid AI Glasses Style at $299 or the Echo Frames on sale.

How long does the battery really last?

Four to eight hours of normal use on camera glasses; about 30 minutes if the AI is watching continuously. The charging case adds a day or two. Display glasses last about six hours; the Even G2, with no camera, lasts days.

How we evaluated

There is no RTINGS-style lab for smart glasses yet, so we leaned on the reviewers who wear them for weeks rather than hours: Tom's Guide (whose smart-glasses editor logged 50,000 miles of travel testing this year), Wareable, Android Authority, Android Police, Engadget, Gizmodo, TechRadar and Trusted Reviews, plus AR Compare, which aggregates dozens of reviews into consensus scores (Xreal One Pro 8.0, Even Realities G2 8.0, Oakley Meta Vanguard 7.7, Meta Ray-Ban Display 7.4). Where a spec matters — field of view, brightness, weight, battery, prescription range — we cross-checked the manufacturer's figure against what reviewers measured.

Then the part the launch reviews miss: we read the recent Amazon reviews for every pair, sorted by most recent and weighted toward one, two and three stars, the Meta community forums (where firmware and feature-rollout complaints live) and the six-months-later threads on Reddit. Smart glasses fail socially as often as technically — the friend who asks you to take them off, the gym that bans them, the AI that says "I can't help with that" — and those patterns shaped the rankings as much as the hardware did.

What we ignored: megapixel counts (every camera here is "phone-adjacent"), the number of AI features announced but not yet shipped, and manufacturer battery claims for "mixed use," which assume you rarely talk to the AI. Live AI — the mode where the glasses continuously watch and answer — drains any pair in 30–60 minutes, and we say so.

Six things that changed the advice this year

1. Meta's Gen 2 fixed the battery. The original Ray-Ban Meta ran about four hours and died before lunch. The Gen 2 ($379, $459 with Transitions) doubles that to eight, adds 3K video for clips up to three minutes (five after a rolling update), a case that charges to 50% in 20 minutes and adds 48 hours, and better cameras — while keeping the frames most people already like. Android Police's verdict, "so much fun you'll ignore the red flags," captures both the appeal and the caveat.

2. Displays arrived, in two philosophies. The Meta Ray-Ban Display ($799, US only, with Canada, France, Italy and the UK following in 2026) puts a 600×600 full-colour screen in the right lens, readable in sunlight at up to 5,000 nits, controlled by a Neural Band wristband that reads the muscle signals in your wrist so you can scroll with a finger twitch or write on your leg. It weighs about 69 g and lasts six hours mixed. Rokid took the other route: a green micro-LED display in both lenses, 49 g, a 12MP camera, and a choice of ChatGPT or Gemini — the pair that aced the exam — for $599. Even Realities went further, dropping the camera and speakers altogether for a discreet productivity HUD in frames that pass for ordinary glasses.

3. Google and Samsung are coming, and Rokid already runs Gemini. At I/O in May, Google and Samsung showed Android XR glasses built with Warby Parker and Gentle Monster — audio-only and display versions — launching this fall with Gemini for live translation, navigation and notification summaries. Prices weren't announced. Rokid beat them to Gemini in March with a software update, which is why an Android owner in September has a real choice today.

4. Privacy went from footnote to headline. In 2025 Meta changed its policy so that voice recordings are stored by default (up to a year, no opt-out) and camera captures used with Meta AI feed model training unless you disable the "Hey Meta" wake word. A 2026 lawsuit, following a Swedish media investigation, alleges that contractors in Nairobi reviewed footage that included bathroom visits, banking details and sex. Trackers counted 47+ venues and jurisdictions banning camera glasses by mid-year, New York banned them from every courtroom in the state on July 20, DEF CON banned them outright, and the EU's AI Act prohibition on real-time biometric identification in public took effect August 2. A 70-organisation civil-rights coalition forced Meta to shelve a "Name Tag" facial-recognition feature. Buying camera glasses in 2026 means buying into this; buying camera-free glasses means opting out.

5. Sport got its own line. The Oakley Meta Vanguard ($499) is a wraparound with a centred camera, the loudest speakers Meta makes, nine hours of battery, an IP67 rating and Garmin and Strava integration — "Ray-Ban Meta on steroids," per Android Central, and "sporty to a fault," per Engadget, because you won't wear it to dinner. The Oakley Meta HSTN ($399) is the everyday Oakley.

6. XR glasses got cheaper and better. Xreal cut the One Pro to $599 permanently in April; its flat-prism optics are 40% smaller than the previous generation, the field of view is 57°, and the X1 chip anchors a screen in space with no extra hardware. Viture's Luma Pro ($499) answers with a sharper 1200p panel and electrochromic dimming, and Android Authority calls it "the best XR glasses for under $500." The catch for iPhone owners: only the USB-C iPhones (15 and later) can drive them, and the Nintendo Switch 2 needs Xreal's Hub.

The ten things that actually matter when you buy smart glasses

Roughly in the order they appear as the reason for a return.

1. The kind — and the job you'll actually give them

Ask what you'll do with them on a Tuesday, not at a demo. Hands-free photos of your kids, a quick "what is this building," music on a walk, calls while cooking: kind 1, camera glasses. Reading a translation or the next turn without pulling out your phone, captions in a loud bar, a teleprompter for a presentation: kind 2, display glasses. Watching a film on a flight or gaming on a Steam Deck in bed: kind 3, XR glasses. Nobody has built one pair that does all three well, and the products that try (display glasses that stream video) do the third job badly.

2. The display: monocular vs binocular, colour vs green, and field of view

Display glasses use a waveguide etched into the lens. The Meta Ray-Ban Display is monocular (right eye only), full colour, 600×600 pixels across a 20° field of view, and bright enough for direct sunlight at up to 5,000 nits; the trade-off is a visible ripple in the right lens and a 69 g weight. Rokid Glasses are binocular (both eyes, which many people find less fatiguing), green-only micro-LED at 480×398 across 23°, and 49 g; the green-only readout is divisive but legible. Even Realities G2 is binocular, green, and the most discreet. XR glasses are a different technology entirely — birdbath or flat-prism optics with 1080p–1200p micro-OLED panels across 52–57°, which is why they look like a cinema and the others look like a smartwatch. None of the display AI glasses is for video; all of the XR glasses are.

3. Camera and recording: "phone-adjacent," with limits

Every camera here is 12MP and every reviewer describes the results the same way: fine in daylight, grainy at dinner, phone-adjacent rather than phone-quality. What differs is video — the Gen 2 and Oakley models shoot 3K at 30 fps or 1080p at 60, in clips capped at three minutes (five after a phased update) — and the placement: the Vanguard's camera sits centred over the nose for a true POV, where the Ray-Ban's is in the left corner and catches your hair. All Meta glasses light a front LED while recording; it is small, and bystanders in bright light don't see it. Rokid's Style records with no video-calling support; the Even G2 has no camera at all, which in 2026 is a feature.

4. The AI: whose, how good, and for how long

Meta glasses run Meta AI, which is conservative — reviewers and owners report a lot of "I can't help with that" — but deeply integrated: it sees what you see, reads signs, translates speech in a handful of languages, and controls the camera and music by voice. Rokid lets you switch between ChatGPT and Gemini, and owners praise picking the model for the task. Even's assistant is ChatGPT-based and text-first. Android XR glasses will run Gemini. Two limits apply to all of them: Live AI — the continuous "watch and answer" mode — runs about 30 minutes on Meta hardware before the battery collapses (the Gen 3 rumours are all about extending it to hours), and every assistant gets worse in noise and with accents. The exam video worked because the questions were printed clearly on a desk in a quiet room.

5. Battery: eight hours is the new floor, six is the display tax

Gen 2 Ray-Bans and Oakley HSTN run eight hours of mixed use, the Vanguard nine, the Display six, the Rokid Style "impressive" in reviews (Rokid doesn't publish a clean figure), and the camera-free Even G2 around 48. Every figure assumes short AI queries; video and Live AI cut them to an hour or two. The charging case is not optional — the Ray-Ban case adds 48 hours and charges the glasses to 50% in 20 minutes — and the Rokid Style's lack of a battery case is a genuine drawback owners mention. XR glasses have no battery; they run off the device, and will drain a phone or Steam Deck noticeably faster.

6. Weight, fit and the bridge of your nose

Regular glasses weigh 20–30 g. Rokid Glasses (49 g) and the Ray-Ban Gen 2 (~52 g) are wearable all day for most people; the Display (69 g) and the XR glasses (80–90 g, Xreal One Pro 87 g) draw comfort complaints after two hours, and reviewers say so. Nose fit matters as much as weight: Ray-Ban offers low-bridge-fit Wayfarers, which matter for many Asian and flat-bridge faces; Rokid Style owners report bridge fatigue; XR glasses ship with swappable nose pads. If you can, try them on — LensCrafters, Sunglass Hut, Target Optical and Best Buy stock Meta glasses.

7. Prescription: the range and the real price

Most buyers need lenses, and this is where the price on the box stops being the price. Meta's Display supports only −4.00 to +4.00 total power; Ray-Ban Meta supports a broad range and in March 2026 Meta launched two "built-for-prescription" frames (Blayzer Optics and Scriber Optics, from $499). Getting lenses in: Ray-Ban/LensCrafters and an optometrist typically run $600–$800 for the finished pair with anti-reflective coating; retrofit services (UseMyFrame, VR Optician, RX-able, Lensology, LensDirect, VR Wave) fit lenses to a standard Wayfarer for $45–$210 depending on coatings, Transitions and polarisation. Rokid and Even sell prescription-ready frames; XR glasses take snap-in inserts (about $50–$100) or, on the Viture, have built-in focus dials for mild myopia. Progressives are hit-or-miss on all of them.

8. Privacy and data — what you're agreeing to

Read this before the checkout page. On Meta glasses the wake word keeps the microphones — and the camera-for-AI feature — always ready unless you turn it off; voice recordings are stored by default with no opt-out (deletable manually); and photos and video used with Meta AI can be used to train Meta's models. Rokid routes queries through OpenAI or Google under their policies; Even's G2 has no camera and keeps its footprint small. Then there is everyone around you: the recording LED is easy to miss, and the list of places that will ask you to remove camera glasses now includes courts, cinemas, gyms, clinics, casinos, cruise ships, exam halls and a growing number of workplaces. We give the day-one settings checklist in the tips section.

9. Phone compatibility

Meta, Rokid and Even glasses work with iPhone and Android through their apps; Android XR glasses will be Android-first. XR glasses are the trap: they need USB-C DisplayPort output, which means iPhone 15 or later (Lightning iPhones can't), most flagship Android phones, Steam Deck, ROG Ally, MacBooks and Windows laptops with DP-Alt-Mode ports — and the Nintendo Switch 2, which dropped direct glasses support, needs Xreal's Hub or Neo Hub. Xreal's Beam Pro ($199+) is an Android puck that makes any pair standalone.

10. Who will still support them in 2028

Meta ships features by phased, server-side rollout — Gen 2 owners waited months for promised recording modes — but it ships them, and it will be here. Rokid is a decade-old Chinese AR company with a big Kickstarter following and a growing US presence; Xreal and Viture are established; Even Realities is small but well reviewed; Halliday, Solos and Brilliant Labs are crowdfunded or niche. Amazon's Echo Frames have been through three generations. The $17 ChatGPT glasses will not have an app next year. Buy the ecosystem, not just the frames.

How to set up, use, and not get thrown out of places

Smart glasses have a steeper "week two" than any gadget we cover: the novelty fades, the battery habit isn't formed, the AI has said no three times, and someone at the gym has said something. Here is how owners who kept wearing theirs got past it.

Day one: the privacy settings (Meta glasses)

  1. Decide about "Hey Meta." The wake word keeps the microphones listening and enables Meta AI's camera use by default. If you'd rather capture only by pressing the button, turn the wake word off in the Meta AI app (Devices → your glasses → Voice). You lose hands-free, you gain certainty.
  2. Voice recordings. Stored by default for up to a year with no opt-out; you can delete them in the app's voice-history settings, and you can make that a monthly habit.
  3. Data for AI training. Review every data-sharing toggle under Settings → Privacy in the Meta AI app, particularly anything about Meta AI with camera and improving Meta's products. The defaults favour Meta; change what you're not comfortable with.
  4. Assume a human may see what you send. The 2026 lawsuit alleges contractors reviewed footage. Don't ask the AI to look at bank statements, medical documents, or people who haven't consented.
  5. Set the capture LED expectation. It's small. Tell the people you're with when you're recording; ask before recording indoors; never in bathrooms, locker rooms, clinics or anywhere with an expectation of privacy. This is also the law in many states for audio.

Where the "Hey Meta" wake-word switch lives — the single most important privacy setting on Meta glasses.

Getting good results from the AI

  • Frame the question like a camera shot. "Hey Meta, look and tell me what this sign says" works when the sign fills the view and is lit; it fails on a menu in a dim restaurant. Move closer, hold still for a second.
  • Use Live AI in bursts. It runs continuously and drains the battery in about 30 minutes on current Meta hardware. Start it for the museum room, stop it after.
  • Translation: download the language packs in advance (Meta supports a handful — Spanish, French, Italian, Portuguese among them — offline); Rokid and Even rely on the phone connection. Speak in short sentences; it's interpretation, not subtitles.
  • Rokid owners: keep ChatGPT as the default for factual and logic questions and switch to Gemini for anything involving your Google account, places or recipes — the combination is what reviewers praised.
  • Accents and noise hurt every assistant. Lower your voice, speak toward the temple mic, and repeat once; three failures means the room, not you.

Battery habits

  • Put the glasses in the case whenever they're off your face. The Ray-Ban case adds 48 hours and hits 50% in 20 minutes; the Display's case brings it to 30; the Rokid Style has no case, so carry the cable.
  • Video and Live AI are the drains; photos and music barely register. A day of photos and calls is realistic; a day of video is not.
  • Heat kills the small cell: no dashboards, no direct sun on the case.

Prescription lenses without overpaying

  • Get your current prescription and pupillary distance from your optometrist in writing.
  • Meta glasses: if your Rx is inside the range, order lenses at checkout (Ray-Ban/Meta), at LensCrafters, or use a retrofit service (UseMyFrame, VR Optician, RX-able, Lensology, LensDirect, VR Wave) for $45–$210 into a frame you already own. Transitions and polarised are available either way. The Display's ±4.00 limit is hard.
  • Rokid and Even: order prescription frames directly from the maker.
  • XR glasses: buy the insert frame and have any optician fill it, or use the Viture's dials if you're mildly short-sighted.
  • Progressives: ask the service specifically; results vary and returns are limited once lenses are cut.

Getting the most from the camera

  • Photos import to the phone over Wi-Fi through the app; 3K clips take minutes, so do it while charging.
  • For video calls from your point of view, WhatsApp and Messenger on Meta glasses; the other person sees what you see — great for a walk-through of a house or a bike repair.
  • Clip limits: three minutes on Gen 2 (five after the rolling update). Say "Hey Meta, start recording" and it stops itself.
  • Low light: don't. Every reviewer found the same grain.

XR glasses: making them work with your devices

  • Check for USB-C DisplayPort Alt Mode before you buy: iPhone 15 and later yes, Lightning iPhones no; most flagship Android phones yes, most mid-range no; Steam Deck and ROG Ally yes; MacBooks and most modern Windows laptops yes.
  • Nintendo Switch 2 removed direct support — budget for the Xreal Hub or Neo Hub; the Xreal Beam Pro puck makes any pair standalone and adds 3DoF anchoring to older models.
  • Set the IPD/nose pad and the brightness first; use the electrochromic dimming (Viture) or the included light shield (Xreal) on planes.
  • Take a break every hour: 80–90 g on your nose is the top comfort complaint.

Travelling with smart glasses: airports, planes and roaming

  • Security screening. Smart glasses go through the X-ray like any other electronics; TSA and most EU airports do not ask you to remove them from your face at the body scanner, but a few agents will, so have the case in an outside pocket. Nobody in the owner threads reports a pair being confiscated, and the batteries (under 1 Wh in every camera pair, about 2 Wh in the Display) are far below the 100 Wh carry-on limit.
  • In flight. Bluetooth to your own phone is allowed once the cabin crew permit devices; recording other passengers is where complaints come from, and several US carriers now write "no recording of crew or passengers" into their contract of carriage. XR glasses are the one category that is better on a plane than anywhere else: a 120-inch virtual screen in an economy seat is the single most repeated "worth it" comment in the Xreal and Viture reviews.
  • Charging abroad. The charging cases are USB-C and accept any 5 V charger; the Display's folding case takes a normal 20 W phone brick. Pack a short cable — the cases do not charge from a laptop's low-power port reliably.
  • Language and translation. Live translation on the Ray-Ban Meta works offline for the language packs you download at home (English, Spanish, French, Italian, Portuguese and German at the time of writing); the Rokid and Even pairs need a data connection, so buy an eSIM before you land or the headline feature is dead on arrival.
  • Local law. Recording in public is legal in the US and most of Europe; two-party-consent rules for audio apply in twelve US states and in Germany, and Korea now treats covert exam-hall recording as a criminal offence. When in doubt, the LED that shows you are recording is the etiquette that keeps you out of trouble.

Where you'll be asked to take them off

Courts (New York statewide since July 20, 2026, and many federal courthouses), cinemas, gyms, spas and pools, casinos, cruise ships, hospitals and clinics, schools and exam halls, secure workplaces, DEF CON. Some airlines and venues now ask at the door. The graceful move is to carry the case and switch to regular glasses without being asked — or to buy camera-free glasses (Even G2, Echo Frames, XR glasses) if that's most of your week.

1. "Dead by mid-afternoon"

14 questions people ask before buying smart glasses

Is it legal to record people with smart glasses?

In public places in the US, generally yes for video; audio is different — about a dozen states (California, Florida, Illinois, Washington among them) require everyone's consent to record a conversation. Private venues set their own rules, and a growing number ban camera glasses outright. The practical rule: don't record where people expect privacy, and tell people when you are.

Do smart glasses work with an iPhone?

Meta, Rokid, Even and Echo Frames: yes, through their apps, on iPhone and Android. XR glasses (Xreal, Viture): only USB-C iPhones — 15 and later — because they need DisplayPort output.

Can I get prescription lenses?

Yes on everything here except the cheapest no-name pairs, with limits: the Meta Ray-Ban Display supports −4.00 to +4.00 only; Ray-Ban Meta, Rokid and Even support broad ranges; XR glasses use snap-in inserts. Budget $45–$210 for a retrofit service or $200–$400 at an optician on top of the frames.

Can you see the display outdoors?

The Meta Ray-Ban Display is readable in direct sunlight (up to 5,000 nits); Rokid's and Even's green displays are readable outdoors but dimmer; XR glasses need their light shield or Viture's dimming lenses in bright light.

How long does the battery last, really?

Eight hours of light use on Gen 2 Ray-Bans and HSTN, nine on the Vanguard, six on the Display, about two days on the Even G2. Video and Live AI cut any of them to an hour or two. XR glasses have no battery and drain the device instead.

What's the difference between the Meta Ray-Ban Display and Xreal glasses?

The Display is a 20° heads-up readout for text, maps and captions with a camera and AI; Xreal is a 57° 1080p screen for video and apps with no AI or camera. One is a smartwatch on your face, the other is a TV. Nobody sells both in one pair.

Do the glasses work without the phone?

Meta and Rokid glasses can take photos and play music from onboard storage, but AI, translation and calls need the phone's connection. Even G2 needs the phone for most features. XR glasses need a device by definition.

Will I be asked to take them off?

Yes, if you wear camera glasses into a gym, courtroom, cinema, clinic, exam hall or casino, and increasingly in workplaces. Carry the case; or buy camera-free glasses if that's your week.

Is Meta watching what I record?

By default, voice recordings are stored (up to a year) and captures used with Meta AI can be used for training; a 2026 lawsuit alleges contractors reviewed owners' footage. You can disable the wake word, delete voice history and change data-sharing toggles — see the day-one checklist above. Rokid uses OpenAI/Google under their policies; Even's G2 has no camera.

Are the cheap "ChatGPT AI glasses" on Amazon any good?

No. Reviewers found 2K-at-best cameras, apps that stop working, no updates and no clear data policy. The Rokid AI Glasses Style ($269–$299) and Echo Frames ($120–$170 on sale) are the real floor.

Should I wait for Ray-Ban Meta Gen 3 or the Google glasses?

For Meta camera glasses, yes — Connect is September 23–24 and the Gen 2 will be discounted either way. For Android owners who want Gemini, the fall Android XR launch is worth waiting for unless you want the Rokid Glasses today. Display, Rokid, Even, Xreal and Viture aren't due refreshes.

Do they work with hearing aids or over prescription sunglasses?

Open-ear speakers sit above the ear, so they coexist with behind-the-ear hearing aids better than any earbud does, and several hearing-aid wearers in the Meta community threads say the Ray-Ban Meta is the first "headphone" they can actually use. The Nuance Audio glasses from EssilorLuxottica are a different product entirely: FDA-cleared over-the-counter hearing glasses with no camera, for mild to moderate loss. As for wearing smart glasses over your own sunglasses, no — every pair here is a frame, so you replace the frame and order lenses (clear, sun or transition) inside it.

What about warranty and returns?

Meta gives one year in the US and the Ray-Ban stores accept returns for 30 days if the lenses are stock; prescription lenses usually make the pair non-returnable, which is the single most common regret in the returns threads, so try a stock pair first. Amazon's 30-day window applies to the Xreal, Viture, Rokid and Even pairs sold there. Rokid's warranty runs through the seller, not Rokid US, and owners report two- to four-week turnarounds; Even Realities handles its own service from the US and was the best-rated after-sales experience in the threads we read.

Could I really use these to cheat on a test?

The exam video shows it works on printed problems in a quiet room — which is exactly why exam boards, schools and universities banned them this year and why Korea brought its first prosecution. Don't. The same capability, pointed at a menu in Lisbon, is the legitimate reason to buy them.

Final verdict

Decide the kind first. For camera-and-AI glasses, the Ray-Ban Meta Gen 2 is the finished product — buy it after Meta Connect on September 24, or the Rokid AI Glasses Style for $100 less and no Meta account. For a screen in your eye, the Meta Ray-Ban Display is the most polished and the Rokid Glasses are the value, with a display in both eyes and your choice of ChatGPT or Gemini; the Even Realities G2 is the one you can wear anywhere because it has no camera. For a private cinema, the Xreal One Pro for features and the Viture Luma Pro for the picture. For sport, the Oakley Meta Vanguard. Under $200, the Echo Frames on sale, and nothing else.

Whatever you buy: change the privacy defaults on day one, learn where you'll be asked to remove them, keep the case in your pocket, and treat the AI as a very good assistant for signs, objects and languages rather than a replacement for your phone. That — and a realistic battery expectation — is the entire difference between the owners who wrote the five-star reviews and the ones who returned them in week two.
