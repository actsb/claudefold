Why do we put the thermometer first?

Because it is the only item here that decides whether the meal is safe.

The USDA's minimum for turkey is 165°F, measured in the innermost part of the thigh, the innermost part of the wing and the thickest part of the breast (University of Illinois Extension and USDA FSIS guidance). Consumer Reports tested 21 pop-up timers and reported that three popped below 165°F, one as low as 139.5°F. The roasting pan, the knife and the gravy tools are all about speed and comfort. The thermometer is about not getting anyone sick.

America's Test Kitchen's 2025 instant-read test named the ThermoWorks Thermapen ONE its top pick, reading in about a second, and the ThermoWorks ThermoPop 2 its budget pick. For one big dinner a year the ThermoPop 2 is the sensible buy. You pay for speed with the Thermapen ONE, which matters if you probe a roast, a steak and a loaf of bread every week.

One habit makes either one work: check it in ice water the day before. A properly working thermometer should read 32°F there. If it is off by more than a degree or two, call ThermoWorks's support, not Thanksgiving morning.

When should you start, and when does it end?

Start thawing by Saturday, November 21; finish leftovers by Sunday, November 29.

  1. Thaw in the fridge. The USDA gives about 24 hours per 4 to 5 pounds: 4 to 12 pounds takes 1 to 3 days, 12 to 16 pounds 3 to 4 days, 16 to 20 pounds 4 to 5 days. Cold-water thawing takes about 30 minutes per pound. Never thaw on the counter.
  2. Test the thermometer. Ice water reads 32°F.
  3. Roast at 325°F. Stuffing or casserole needs the oven at 325°F or higher and 165°F inside.
  4. Probe three spots. Thigh, wing and breast, at 165°F; the University of Illinois Extension says check the thigh first because dark meat cooks slowest.
  5. Keep hot food hot. 140°F or above.
  6. Put leftovers away within 2 hours. Eat or freeze by day 3 or 4, and reheat to 165°F.

One conflict worth knowing: Butterball's roasting page says 170°F in the thigh. The USDA minimum is 165°F. Both are safe; we would not go below 165°F.

Quick answers

When should I start thawing a turkey for Thanksgiving 2026?

Thanksgiving is Thursday, November 26. The USDA says a refrigerator thaw takes about 24 hours for every 4 to 5 pounds, so a 16 to 20 pound bird needs 4 to 5 days. Start on Saturday, November 21 to be safe; the 12 to 16 pound range can start on Sunday, November 22.

What temperature should the turkey reach?

The USDA's minimum is 165°F, measured in the innermost part of the thigh, the innermost part of the wing and the thickest part of the breast. Butterball's own roasting page gives 170°F; both are safe, and we would not cook below 165°F.

Is the pop-up timer good enough?

No. Consumer Reports tested 21 pop-up timers and reported that three popped below 165°F, one as low as 139.5°F. Use a thermometer and take several readings.

Do I need a $100 Thermapen, or is a $40 ThermoPop enough?

For one dinner a year the ThermoPop 2 is enough: America's Test Kitchen's testers named it their budget pick. The Thermapen ONE reads in about a second and has the better reputation among enthusiasts, so it pays off if you cook all year.

Is an electric carving knife worth buying?

Only if you carve for a crowd. America's Test Kitchen's testers liked the Black+Decker ComfortGrip 9-inch for slicing skin-on turkey without tearing. For four people a sharp slicing knife costs less and stores easier.

What is a fat separator for?

It lets you pour off drippings so the fat stays behind and the juices go into the gravy. America's Test Kitchen's testers preferred bottom-draining models and named the OXO Good Gravy 4-cup the winner.

Should I stuff the turkey?

The USDA says cooking stuffing in a casserole is safer: it is easier to reach 165°F. If you do stuff the bird, the stuffing itself must reach 165°F.

How long do Thanksgiving leftovers last?

Refrigerate within 2 hours (1 hour above 90°F). The USDA's guidance is 3 to 4 days in the fridge, 3 to 4 months in the freezer for best quality, and a reheat to 165°F. For a Thursday dinner that means eating or freezing by Sunday, November 29.

Do I need a warming tray?

Not necessarily. Hot food must stay at 140°F or above, and a slow cooker on the 'keep warm' setting does that without taking an oven rack. We found no expert review of buffet warming trays, so we do not recommend a model.

Do I need to buy extra chairs?

Count your guests first. Butterball reports the average host expects about nine guests (2025). We found no expert test of folding chairs, so we left them out of the picks.
