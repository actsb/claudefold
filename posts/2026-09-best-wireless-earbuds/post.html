Six words on the box, in one line each

  • ANC — active noise cancelling: microphones listen to the noise around you and the earbuds cancel it. Great for planes and trains; less magic for voices.
  • Transparency — the opposite: lets the outside in so you can hear a barista or a bike bell without taking them out.
  • Multipoint — connected to your laptop and your phone at the same time. If you take calls at a desk, you want it.
  • Codec (AAC, LDAC, aptX) — how music is squeezed over Bluetooth. Only matters if you pay for hi-res streaming and have an Android phone.
  • IP rating — sweat and rain resistance. IPX4 is fine for the gym; IP55 and up for rain and dust.
  • Open-fit vs. in-ear — open-fit (AirPods 5) rests in the ear with no tip; in-ear seals the canal for better bass and noise cancelling; open-ear (Shokz) sits outside it entirely.

Do AirPods work with Android?

As ordinary Bluetooth earbuds, yes. You lose the instant pairing, the settings, Find My and most of the tricks. On Android, buy the Sony, the Pixel Buds or the Galaxy Buds instead.

Should I wait for the AirPods 5?

Are the $80 earbuds actually good?

Yes. The Soundcore Liberty 4 NC and the EarFun Air Pro 4+ give you good noise cancelling, 8 hours and an app. What you give up is call quality and the last 20% of sound and ANC.

How do I stop them falling out?

Try every tip size (most people need a different size in each ear), run the fit test in the app, and twist them in slightly. If they still move, you want ear hooks (Powerbeats Pro 2) or an open-ear design (Shokz).

Buy only when the listing says "Ships from Amazon, Sold by Amazon" (or Apple). Check the serial number at Apple's coverage page when they arrive. A price far below $199 for the Pro 3 is the tell.

How we evaluated

Earbuds are one of the few categories where independent labs publish real measurements, so we started there. SoundGuys measures noise attenuation across the frequency range, battery to the minute and microphone quality with standardized recordings; RTINGS tests the same things on its own rig and adds latency and app scores; What Hi-Fi? and TechGearLab cover sound quality and fit; Consumer Reports adds durability. Where they agree, we treat it as fact. Where they disagree — usually on sound signature, which is taste — we say so.

Then we did what the labs can't: we read the recent Amazon reviews for every pair, sorted by most recent and weighted toward the one-, two- and three-star reviews, plus the "six months later" threads on Reddit. Earbuds fail in ways a two-week review never sees — a battery that lasts four hours by month eighteen, a case hinge, a firmware update that breaks multipoint, a left bud that stops charging — and those patterns decide our rankings as much as the lab numbers do.

A pair made the list only when it measured well in at least two labs, its owner reviews showed no recurring failure that the labs wouldn't catch, and it was actually available in the US at a sane price in September 2026. What we ignored: driver size in millimetres, "Hi-Res" logos, the number of microphones, and spec-sheet battery figures measured with ANC off at 50% volume — we quote ANC-on figures throughout.

The earbud market in September 2026: what changed

Five things reshaped the advice this year.

1. Apple put noise cancelling in the cheap AirPods. Announced September 9 and shipping September 18, the AirPods 5 make ANC standard across both models — $129 with a regular case, $149 with a wireless-charging case that also adds swipe volume controls on the stems and an extra hour of battery. Apple claims the open-fit ANC removes up to 50% more noise than the AirPods 4 with ANC, and the buds inherit Adaptive Audio, Conversation Awareness and Live Translation from the Pro line, on the same H2 chip, with an IP57 rating. The catch is the one physics imposes on every open-fit design: with ANC on, the $129 pair runs four hours, the $149 pair five. For people who hate silicone tips in their ear canal, they are the new default; for everyone else, they are the reason the AirPods Pro 3 keep getting cheaper.

2. Sony took the all-round crown back. The WF-1000XM6 ($329.99) fixed the two things that held the XM5 back — fit and mics — and pushed noise cancelling to within a hair of Bose. SoundGuys measured an 88% average noise reduction across the spectrum and a battery that ran 9 hours 41 minutes with ANC on against Sony's 8-hour claim; What Hi-Fi? called the sound "wonderfully natural, musical" and named it good enough "to worry every major rival at the money." They cost more than everything except Bose, and XM5 owners have little reason to upgrade, but if you want one pair that is excellent at everything on any phone, this is it.

3. Bose kept the ANC title and fixed the annoyances. The QuietComfort Ultra Earbuds (2nd Gen) ($299, routinely $249) are, by every lab we read, still the quietest earbuds on sale. The second generation added the wireless charging the first one inexplicably lacked, Bluetooth 5.4 with LE Audio, noticeably better microphones and software tweaks to the ANC. Six hours of battery is the price of that silence.

4. Samsung and Google doubled down on their own phones. The Galaxy Buds4 Pro ($250) sound and feel superb and add Galaxy AI live translation, but their best tricks only work on a Galaxy, and six hours of ANC-on battery (closer to five with every feature on) is short for a 2026 "Pro." Google skipped a new Pixel Buds Pro this year, gave the Pro 2 ($229) new colours, new features and a $40 cut, and launched the $130 Pixel Buds 2a, whose ANC gets surprisingly close to the AirPods Pro 3 in the bass and mids. Both are excellent — on the right phone.

5. The $80 tier caught up to 2023's flagships. Hybrid ANC, LDAC hi-res, multipoint, wireless charging and a proper app are now standard at $70–$100. The Soundcore Liberty 4 NC (~$80), EarFun Air Pro 4+ and Nothing Ear (a) ($79) genuinely compete with what $200 bought two years ago. The gap that remains is in the last 20% of ANC depth, call quality in noise, and how long the company supports the product.

Two smaller shifts worth knowing: open-ear earbuds (Shokz OpenFit 2/2+, Bose Ultra Open, Soundcore AeroFit 2) went from a runner's niche to a mainstream category for anyone who wants to hear the office or the street; and health features arrived — heart-rate sensing in the AirPods Pro 3 and Powerbeats Pro 2, and the FDA-cleared hearing-aid and hearing-test features on the AirPods Pro, which for mild-to-moderate loss are a genuine alternative to a $1,500 device.

The ten things that actually matter when you buy earbuds

Every product page lists forty features. These ten decide whether you keep the pair or return it — roughly in the order they show up as the reason for a one-star review.

1. Fit and seal: the whole game

Nothing else on this list works if the earbud doesn't seal your ear canal. Noise cancelling depends on a seal; bass depends on a seal; a bud that slips loses both and then falls out on the treadmill. Ear canals vary far more than earbuds do, which is why the most common one-star review in the category — across every brand and every price — is "they hurt / they fall out." Three things help. Tip selection: the AirPods Pro 3 ship five sizes of foam-infused silicone tips (the best stock tips in the business); Sony's XM6 improved fit but ships fewer tip options than rivals; third-party foam tips (Comply, and Sony's own foam) fix a poor seal on almost anything with a standard nozzle. Fit tests: Apple, Sony, Samsung and Google all have an in-app test that plays a tone and checks the seal — run it before you judge the sound. Shape: stem designs (AirPods, Nothing, Beats Studio) sit shallow and suit narrow canals; bulb designs (Sony, Bose, Samsung) fill the concha and suit larger ears; hooks (Powerbeats, Shokz) never move. If earbuds have never fit you, an open-fit (AirPods 5) or open-ear (Shokz) design is the answer, not another tip size.

2. Noise cancelling: three tiers, not forty models

Active noise cancelling uses microphones to generate anti-noise; it is superb at steady low-frequency sound (engines, HVAC, road roar) and weak against voices and sudden sounds, which is why a plane goes silent and a coworker doesn't. In 2026 lab testing the field sorts into three tiers. Tier 1 — Bose QC Ultra 2nd Gen (the deepest), Sony WF-1000XM6 (88% average reduction in SoundGuys' test), AirPods Pro 3 (Apple claims 2× the Pro 2). Tier 2 — Galaxy Buds4 Pro ("close to AirPods, a little off Sony"), Pixel Buds Pro 2 and 2a, Technics AZ100. Tier 3 — Nothing Ear (3), Sennheiser MTW4, and the good budget pairs, which cut the drone but leave more of the mid-range. Two things the spec sheet won't tell you: some people feel ANC as pressure or hear a faint hiss — if you're one of them, adaptive modes (Apple's Adaptive Audio, Sony's Adaptive Sound Control) that vary the strength are more comfortable than full ANC; and wind defeats every ANC system, which is why the good ones have a wind-reduction mode.

3. Sound quality and codecs: what you can and can't hear

Above about $150 every pair here sounds good; the differences are tuning. Apple, Bose and Samsung tune for a warm, bass-forward, "everything sounds nice" signature; Sony is fuller and more detailed; Technics and Sennheiser are the audiophile choices — more natural, more spacious, less flattering to bad recordings. Every major app has an EQ, so a signature you dislike is fixable. Codecs matter less than forums suggest: iPhones only transmit AAC, so LDAC and aptX are irrelevant on an iPhone; Android phones can use LDAC (Sony, Soundcore, Nothing, EarFun) or aptX/aptX Lossless for a higher-bitrate stream that most listeners can't distinguish from AAC in a blind test but that costs battery — the Nothing Ear (3), for instance, loses runtime noticeably with hi-res on. The new LC3 codec over LE Audio (Bose 2nd Gen, Galaxy Buds4 Pro, XM6) is the one that matters long-term: lower latency, better battery and Auracast broadcast audio in airports and gyms.

4. The ecosystem trap: which earbuds actually work with your phone

This is the mistake we see most in the reviews: someone buys the "best earbuds" for the wrong phone and loses half of what they paid for. AirPods on Android are decent earbuds with no app, no Adaptive Audio, no fit test, no Find My and no firmware updates. Galaxy Buds on an iPhone have no app at all — no ANC adjustment, no EQ. Pixel Buds on an iPhone: same story. The brands that give you everything on every phone are Sony, Bose, Technics, Sennheiser, Nothing, Soundcore and EarFun, and if there is any chance you'll switch platforms in the life of the earbuds, that should decide it.

The full feature set of Apple, Samsung and Google earbuds is gated to their own phones; Sony, Bose and the rest are platform-neutral.

5. Battery: divide the claim by the features you'll use, then by two at year two

Manufacturers quote runtime at moderate volume, usually with ANC off; we quote ANC-on figures throughout. The XM6, AirPods Pro 3, Pixel Buds Pro 2, Powerbeats Pro 2 and Liberty 4 NC lead at 8 hours (Sony measured 9h41m); Technics and Sennheiser sit at 7.5; the Galaxy Buds4 Pro, Bose and Nothing Ear (3) at 6 (Samsung's drops to 4.5–5 with hi-res, 360 audio and adaptive ANC all on); the AirPods 5 at 4–5. Cases add 20–30 hours. The number that matters more is the one nobody prints: earbud batteries are tiny and non-replaceable, and heat, daily full charges and storage at 100% wear them down — expect 60–70% of day-one runtime by the second year on every brand, which is why a pair that starts at 8 hours ages better than one that starts at 4.

6. Microphones and calls: the most under-weighted spec

If you take calls outdoors or in an open office, mic quality should outrank sound quality. The labs' standardized recordings put Bose (2nd Gen), Sony XM6 (AI beamforming, a big step over the XM5) and the AirPods at the top, Samsung and Google close behind, and Technics, Sennheiser and Nothing (whose "Super Mic" underwhelmed reviewers) at the bottom of the premium tier — the audiophile pairs are the worst phones. Budget pairs are fine indoors and poor in wind. SoundGuys and RTINGS publish the actual recordings; listen before you buy if calls are your job.

7. Transparency and adaptive modes

A good transparency mode makes the earbuds vanish — you hear the cashier naturally without pulling a bud out. Apple's remains the benchmark, Bose and Sony are close, and the budget pairs sound like a walkie-talkie. Adaptive modes that switch between ANC and transparency automatically (Apple's Adaptive Audio, Sony's Adaptive Sound Control, Samsung's Adaptive ANC) and features that lower the music when you speak (Apple's Conversation Awareness, Sony's Speak-to-Chat) are the ones owners end up using daily.

8. Controls

Touch pads register every adjustment of the bud as a tap, which is why "touch controls" is a recurring complaint for Sony, Samsung and most budget pairs. Stem-squeeze controls (AirPods, Nothing, Beats Studio) and physical buttons (Powerbeats) draw far fewer complaints. If you hate accidental pauses, this matters more than it sounds.

9. Durability: IP ratings, cases and sweat

IPX4 is splash-proof; IP57 (AirPods Pro 3, AirPods 5) and IP55 (Shokz OpenFit 2+) shrug off sweat and rain. Sweat is what kills gym earbuds — it corrodes charging contacts, which is the real story behind most "one bud stopped charging" reviews. Case hinges and lids are the other weak point; Sony's XM6 case is bulky but solid, Nothing's metal case is the nicest at its price, and the cheapest cases crack.

How to set up and get the most out of any earbuds

Half of the one-star reviews we read describe problems the owner could have fixed in five minutes. Here is the five minutes, plus the habits that keep a pair sounding new for three years.

The first ten minutes

  1. Update the firmware before you judge anything. Open the brand's app (Apple updates AirPods silently while they're in the case near your iPhone) and install the pending update. A surprising share of "ANC is weak / connection drops" reviews describe launch firmware that was fixed within weeks.
  2. Run the fit test with every tip size. Apple (Settings → your AirPods → Ear Tip Fit Test), Sony (Headphones Connect → Optimal earbud tips), Samsung (Wearable app → Earbud fit test) and Google all have one. Insert, twist slightly backward, run the test. If medium "fails," try large on one side and medium on the other — asymmetric ears are common.
  3. Set the ANC mode you'll actually use. Full ANC for planes and trains; the adaptive mode (Apple Adaptive Audio, Sony Adaptive Sound Control, Samsung Adaptive ANC, Bose Aware with ActiveSense) for everything else. Adaptive modes are why owners stop complaining about "pressure."
  4. Turn on the talk features. Apple's Conversation Awareness and Sony's Speak-to-Chat drop the volume and open transparency when you start talking; Bose and Samsung have equivalents. This is the feature people don't know they want until it's on.
  5. Pick your controls. Most apps let you remap taps and pinches. Assign volume to something — the default is often to leave it off — and turn off any gesture you keep triggering by accident (Sony owners: disable the "touch to lower volume" wide-area tap if you keep pausing music while adjusting the bud).

Fit and comfort

  • Pain after an hour usually means the tip is too big, not too small. Size down, or switch to foam tips (Comply makes them for nearly every model here), which compress and seal without pressure.
  • If a bud works loose when you chew or talk, the tip is too small or the bud is inserted straight instead of with a slight backward twist. Hooks (Powerbeats) or fins (Bose) solve it for good if tips can't.
  • Earbuds that never fit anyone in your family? That is what open-fit (AirPods 5) and open-ear (Shokz) designs are for. Stop buying tip sizes.
  • Glasses wearers: hooks and fins can collide with temple arms; stem designs (AirPods, Nothing) rarely do.

Sound

  • Use the EQ. Every brand here has one. If bass feels heavy (Apple, Bose, Samsung defaults), cut 2–3 dB below 100 Hz; if vocals sound recessed, lift 2 kHz. Soundcore's HearID and Sony's Find Your Equalizer build a curve from a quick hearing test — try them.
  • Codecs on Android: in Developer Options or the brand's app, LDAC has "Sound quality / Balanced / Connection" priority. "Sound quality" (990 kbps) drops out in crowded places; "Balanced" (660 kbps) is the sensible default. On iPhone none of this exists — AAC only, and it sounds fine.
  • Spatial audio with head tracking is great for films and gimmicky for music. Turn it off for music if vocals sound like they're in a bathroom.
  • Protect your hearing: iOS Settings → Sounds & Haptics → Headphone Safety lets you cap volume at 85 dB; Android has a similar limiter under Sound. ANC lets you listen quieter — use that, not the volume, to beat the noise.

Calls

  • In wind, turn on the wind-reduction mode (Sony, Bose, Samsung have one) and keep the buds' mics — the stems on AirPods, the front edge on bulbs — clear of scarves and hoods.
  • Apple's Voice Isolation (Control Center → Mic Mode during a call) is the single biggest call-quality improvement for AirPods in noise.
  • If people say you sound far away, you probably have a poor seal; the mics use the seal for reference.

Multipoint and switching

  • Multipoint (two devices at once) is on Sony, Bose, Samsung, Google, Nothing, Soundcore and EarFun; enable it in the app. Its known failure mode is two devices fighting for the audio — a Windows laptop's notification sound stealing the connection from your phone. Turn off "connect automatically" on the device you use less.
  • AirPods don't do multipoint; they auto-switch between your Apple devices. If that switching annoys you, Settings → AirPods → Connect to This iPhone → When Last Connected.
  • Pairing to a laptop or TV with no Bluetooth? A Bluetooth transmitter (AirFly and similar) turns any 3.5 mm jack — including a plane seat — into a source.

Battery care — the difference between two years and four

  • Heat is the enemy. Don't leave the case in a hot car or on a sunny windowsill; a 40°C afternoon does more damage than a year of use.
  • Don't leave the buds sitting at 100% for days. Use them. Cases keep buds topped up constantly; if you're storing a pair for a month, leave it at around half.
  • Use the quick charge (3–5 minutes for about an hour on most flagships) rather than draining to zero repeatedly.
  • Sony's and Samsung's apps show battery health; check it at 18 months. When a bud hits ~60% of its original runtime, that's normal ageing, not a defect.

Cleaning — the fix for "one bud won't charge"

  • Weekly: wipe the buds and the inside of the case with a dry microfibre cloth. Monthly: pull the tips, wash them in warm water, dry fully, and clean the mesh with a dry soft brush or a piece of putty (Blu-Tack) pressed gently — never a pin, never liquid.
  • The charging contacts on the buds and in the case collect wax and sweat. Wipe them with a dry cotton swab; this fixes most "one side not charging" complaints instantly.
  • Silicone tips lose their grip after 6–12 months; replacement sets are $10–$15 and restore the seal (and the ANC).

Lost a bud? Bought AirPods online? Two more tips

  • Apple, Sony, Samsung and Google all sell single replacement buds and cases; Find My / Find My Device / Sony's app can play a chime from a bud under the sofa. Register the serial number the day you buy.
  • Checking AirPods are genuine: pair them and look at Settings → Bluetooth → (i) — real AirPods show different serial numbers for the left and right bud; fakes clone one serial for both. Fakes also can't join Find My. Enter the serial at checkcoverage.apple.com; "no matching record" means fake, but a match doesn't prove real, because counterfeiters clone valid serials. Buy from Amazon.com as the seller, Apple, Best Buy, Target or Walmart, and return anything that fails these checks.

What owners complain about: the review deep-dive

We tallied the themes in the recent one-, two- and three-star Amazon reviews and the long-term Reddit threads for every pair in this guide. Here is what comes up, in order, with what it means for your choice.

A qualitative tally of themes across the earbuds in this guide — not a statistical survey.

1. "They fall out / they hurt"

The top complaint at every price and the #1 reason for returns. It is rarely the earbud's fault and almost never fixable by buying a more expensive pair with the same shape. What helps: the fit test, foam tips, and switching shape — stem vs bulb vs hook vs open. The AirPods Pro 3's new fit produced the fewest fit complaints of any sealed flagship this year; Sony's XM6 fixed the XM5's slipping; Technics' AZ100 still draws fit complaints.

2. "Battery is half what it was"

The second most common theme, and the one nobody can fix. Earbud cells are tiny, non-replaceable and cooked by heat. Owners of every brand report 60–70% of day-one runtime by year two. What helps: buy the pair with the most headroom (8-hour pairs age into 5-hour pairs; 4-hour pairs age into 2.5-hour pairs), keep them out of heat, and treat two-to-three years as the design life.

3. "Cuts out / stutters / one side drops"

Crowded 2.4 GHz environments (airports, gyms, downtown), a phone in a back pocket, LDAC set to maximum quality, and multipoint fights between a laptop and a phone account for most of it. What helps: firmware updates (Samsung and Google both shipped connection fixes this year), LDAC on "Balanced," and turning off auto-connect on the second device. Bluetooth 5.4 / LE Audio pairs (Bose Gen 2, Buds4 Pro, XM6) are measurably more stable.

4. "One earbud won't charge"

Almost always dirty contacts, not a dead battery. What helps: a dry cotton swab on the pins and the case cradle. If cleaning doesn't fix it within the first year, it's a warranty case — register the product on day one.

5. "ANC pressure / hiss / wind roar"

Some people feel ANC as a pressure change; a faint hiss is the sound of cheap ANC processing; wind defeats every system. What helps: adaptive modes, a lower ANC level where offered, wind-reduction mode, and — for pressure-sensitive people — Bose, whose ANC produces the least "eardrum suck" in owner reports.

6. "Touch controls have a mind of their own"

Sony, Samsung (older models) and most budget touch pads register adjustments as taps. What helps: remapping or disabling gestures in the app; buying stems (Apple, Nothing, Buds4 Pro's pinch) if this drives you mad.

7. "People can't hear me on calls"

Wind and traffic beat most earbud mics; a poor seal makes it worse. What helps: Bose Gen 2, Sony XM6 and AirPods if calls are your job; wind mode; Apple's Voice Isolation. Avoid Technics, Sennheiser and Nothing for calls.

8. "They're fake" / "they were used"

Overwhelmingly an AirPods problem on marketplace listings. What helps: buy sold-by-Amazon.com or from Apple/Best Buy/Target/Walmart, check the separate L/R serials and Find My on arrival, and return within the window if anything is off.

12 questions people ask before buying earbuds

Are the AirPods Pro 3 worth it over the AirPods 5?

If you can wear ear tips, yes, at any sale price under $220: the seal gives you far deeper ANC, real bass and 8 hours versus 4–5, plus heart rate and the hearing-aid feature. The AirPods 5 win only for people who genuinely can't tolerate tips, or who want to spend $129.

Sony WF-1000XM6 or AirPods Pro 3?

On an iPhone that you'll keep, the Pro 3 — cheaper on sale, better integrated, nearly as good at everything. On anything else, or if you might switch, the Sony: better sound, slightly better ANC, longer battery, and every feature works everywhere. The labs are split by a hair on ANC and agree Sony sounds better.

Which earbuds have the best noise cancelling in 2026?

Bose QuietComfort Ultra Earbuds (2nd Gen), then Sony WF-1000XM6 and AirPods Pro 3 within a hair. Among cheaper pairs, the Pixel Buds 2a ($130) punch far above their price.

Do I need LDAC / aptX / "Hi-Res"?

Not on an iPhone (it only sends AAC). On Android, LDAC or aptX gives a higher-bitrate stream that few people can distinguish from AAC in a blind test, at a cost in battery and connection stability. Nice to have; not a reason to choose a pair.

Will AirPods work with my Android phone?

As basic Bluetooth earbuds, yes — music, calls, ANC on/off by squeezing. You lose the app, Adaptive Audio, Conversation Awareness, the fit test, Find My, hearing features and firmware updates. Buy Sony, Bose, Pixel Buds or Soundcore instead.

How long do wireless earbuds last?

Two to four years. The batteries are the limit: expect about 60–70% of the original runtime by the second year, and replacement isn't practical. Heat and storing at 100% shorten it; an 8-hour pair simply has more headroom than a 4-hour pair.

Why does only one earbud charge?

Dirty contacts, nine times out of ten. Wipe the pins on the bud and the cradle in the case with a dry cotton swab. If it persists in year one, it's warranty.

Is noise cancelling bad for your ears?

No — it lowers the sound you're exposed to, and lets you listen at lower volume. Some people feel a pressure sensation from strong ANC; adaptive or lower ANC levels solve it. What harms hearing is volume: cap it at 85 dB in your phone's settings.

Can AirPods really work as hearing aids?

The AirPods Pro 2 and Pro 3 have an FDA-cleared hearing-aid feature for mild-to-moderate hearing loss, set up with a five-minute hearing test on an iPhone (US and selected countries). For that range of loss it is a genuine, far cheaper alternative; it is not a replacement for a fitted aid with severe loss.

Open-ear vs bone conduction — which should I get for running?

Open-ear (Shokz OpenFit 2/2+, Bose Ultra Open, Soundcore AeroFit) for almost everyone: real bass, real sound, and you still hear traffic. Bone conduction (Shokz OpenRun) only if you need your ear canal completely free — swimmers, some hearing-aid wearers — or if your workplace requires it.

How do I know if AirPods bought online are real?

Pair them and check Settings → Bluetooth → (i): genuine AirPods show different serial numbers for the left and right bud; fakes clone one serial for both. Fakes cannot join Find My. Check the serial at checkcoverage.apple.com — "no record" means fake, though a match doesn't prove genuine. Buy from Amazon.com as the seller, Apple, Best Buy, Target or Walmart, never from a marketplace seller with a too-good price.

Should I wait for the next Sony / Bose / Samsung model?

Sony's XM6 (February 2026), Samsung's Buds4 Pro (March 2026) and Bose's 2nd Gen (2025) are all current; nothing major is expected before 2027. Apple's next Pro is not expected until 2027 either. The only "wait" that pays is three weeks for Prime Big Deal Days.

Final verdict

If you want one pair of earbuds that is excellent at everything on any phone, buy the Sony WF-1000XM6. If you have an iPhone and can wear ear tips, buy the AirPods Pro 3 at $199 and don't look back; if you can't wear tips, the AirPods 5 with the wireless case. If silence is the whole point, the Bose QuietComfort Ultra Earbuds (2nd Gen). Galaxy owners: the Galaxy Buds4 Pro.
