How to set up and get the most out of any earbuds

Half of the one-star reviews we read describe problems the owner could have fixed in five minutes. Here is the five minutes, plus the habits that keep a pair sounding new for three years.

The first ten minutes

  1. Update the firmware before you judge anything. Open the brand's app (Apple updates AirPods silently while they're in the case near your iPhone) and install the pending update. A surprising share of "ANC is weak / connection drops" reviews describe launch firmware that was fixed within weeks.
  2. Run the fit test with every tip size. Apple (Settings → your AirPods → Ear Tip Fit Test), Sony (Headphones Connect → Optimal earbud tips), Samsung (Wearable app → Earbud fit test) and Google all have one. Insert, twist slightly backward, run the test. If medium "fails," try large on one side and medium on the other — asymmetric ears are common.
  3. Set the ANC mode you'll actually use. Full ANC for planes and trains; the adaptive mode (Apple Adaptive Audio, Sony Adaptive Sound Control, Samsung Adaptive ANC, Bose Aware with ActiveSense) for everything else. Adaptive modes are why owners stop complaining about "pressure."
  4. Turn on the talk features. Apple's Conversation Awareness and Sony's Speak-to-Chat drop the volume and open transparency when you start talking; Bose and Samsung have equivalents. This is the feature people don't know they want until it's on.
  5. Pick your controls. Most apps let you remap taps and pinches. Assign volume to something — the default is often to leave it off — and turn off any gesture you keep triggering by accident (Sony owners: disable the "touch to lower volume" wide-area tap if you keep pausing music while adjusting the bud).

Fit and comfort

  • Pain after an hour usually means the tip is too big, not too small. Size down, or switch to foam tips (Comply makes them for nearly every model here), which compress and seal without pressure.
  • If a bud works loose when you chew or talk, the tip is too small or the bud is inserted straight instead of with a slight backward twist. Hooks (Powerbeats) or fins (Bose) solve it for good if tips can't.
  • Earbuds that never fit anyone in your family? That is what open-fit (AirPods 5) and open-ear (Shokz) designs are for. Stop buying tip sizes.
  • Glasses wearers: hooks and fins can collide with temple arms; stem designs (AirPods, Nothing) rarely do.

Sound

  • Use the EQ. Every brand here has one. If bass feels heavy (Apple, Bose, Samsung defaults), cut 2–3 dB below 100 Hz; if vocals sound recessed, lift 2 kHz. Soundcore's HearID and Sony's Find Your Equalizer build a curve from a quick hearing test — try them.
  • Codecs on Android: in Developer Options or the brand's app, LDAC has "Sound quality / Balanced / Connection" priority. "Sound quality" (990 kbps) drops out in crowded places; "Balanced" (660 kbps) is the sensible default. On iPhone none of this exists — AAC only, and it sounds fine.
  • Spatial audio with head tracking is great for films and gimmicky for music. Turn it off for music if vocals sound like they're in a bathroom.
  • Protect your hearing: iOS Settings → Sounds & Haptics → Headphone Safety lets you cap volume at 85 dB; Android has a similar limiter under Sound. ANC lets you listen quieter — use that, not the volume, to beat the noise.

Calls

  • In wind, turn on the wind-reduction mode (Sony, Bose, Samsung have one) and keep the buds' mics — the stems on AirPods, the front edge on bulbs — clear of scarves and hoods.
  • Apple's Voice Isolation (Control Center → Mic Mode during a call) is the single biggest call-quality improvement for AirPods in noise.
  • If people say you sound far away, you probably have a poor seal; the mics use the seal for reference.

Multipoint and switching

  • Multipoint (two devices at once) is on Sony, Bose, Samsung, Google, Nothing, Soundcore and EarFun; enable it in the app. Its known failure mode is two devices fighting for the audio — a Windows laptop's notification sound stealing the connection from your phone. Turn off "connect automatically" on the device you use less.
  • AirPods don't do multipoint; they auto-switch between your Apple devices. If that switching annoys you, Settings → AirPods → Connect to This iPhone → When Last Connected.
  • Pairing to a laptop or TV with no Bluetooth? A Bluetooth transmitter (AirFly and similar) turns any 3.5 mm jack — including a plane seat — into a source.

Battery care — the difference between two years and four

  • Heat is the enemy. Don't leave the case in a hot car or on a sunny windowsill; a 40°C afternoon does more damage than a year of use.
  • Don't leave the buds sitting at 100% for days. Use them. Cases keep buds topped up constantly; if you're storing a pair for a month, leave it at around half.
  • Use the quick charge (3–5 minutes for about an hour on most flagships) rather than draining to zero repeatedly.
  • Sony's and Samsung's apps show battery health; check it at 18 months. When a bud hits ~60% of its original runtime, that's normal ageing, not a defect.

Cleaning — the fix for "one bud won't charge"

  • Weekly: wipe the buds and the inside of the case with a dry microfibre cloth. Monthly: pull the tips, wash them in warm water, dry fully, and clean the mesh with a dry soft brush or a piece of putty (Blu-Tack) pressed gently — never a pin, never liquid.
  • The charging contacts on the buds and in the case collect wax and sweat. Wipe them with a dry cotton swab; this fixes most "one side not charging" complaints instantly.
  • Silicone tips lose their grip after 6–12 months; replacement sets are $10–$15 and restore the seal (and the ANC).

Lost a bud? Bought AirPods online? Two more tips

  • Apple, Sony, Samsung and Google all sell single replacement buds and cases; Find My / Find My Device / Sony's app can play a chime from a bud under the sofa. Register the serial number the day you buy.
  • Checking AirPods are genuine: pair them and look at Settings → Bluetooth → (i) — real AirPods show different serial numbers for the left and right bud; fakes clone one serial for both. Fakes also can't join Find My. Enter the serial at checkcoverage.apple.com; "no matching record" means fake, but a match doesn't prove real, because counterfeiters clone valid serials. Buy from Amazon.com as the seller, Apple, Best Buy, Target or Walmart, and return anything that fails these checks.

What owners complain about: the review deep-dive

We tallied the themes in the recent one-, two- and three-star Amazon reviews and the long-term Reddit threads for every pair in this guide. Here is what comes up, in order, with what it means for your choice.

1. "They fall out / they hurt"

The top complaint at every price and the #1 reason for returns. It is rarely the earbud's fault and almost never fixable by buying a more expensive pair with the same shape. What helps: the fit test, foam tips, and switching shape — stem vs bulb vs hook vs open. The AirPods Pro 3's new fit produced the fewest fit complaints of any sealed flagship this year; Sony's XM6 fixed the XM5's slipping; Technics' AZ100 still draws fit complaints.

2. "Battery is half what it was"

The second most common theme, and the one nobody can fix. Earbud cells are tiny, non-replaceable and cooked by heat. Owners of every brand report 60–70% of day-one runtime by year two. What helps: buy the pair with the most headroom (8-hour pairs age into 5-hour pairs; 4-hour pairs age into 2.5-hour pairs), keep them out of heat, and treat two-to-three years as the design life.

3. "Cuts out / stutters / one side drops"

Crowded 2.4 GHz environments (airports, gyms, downtown), a phone in a back pocket, LDAC set to maximum quality, and multipoint fights between a laptop and a phone account for most of it. What helps: firmware updates (Samsung and Google both shipped connection fixes this year), LDAC on "Balanced," and turning off auto-connect on the second device. Bluetooth 5.4 / LE Audio pairs (Bose Gen 2, Buds4 Pro, XM6) are measurably more stable.

4. "One earbud won't charge"

Almost always dirty contacts, not a dead battery. What helps: a dry cotton swab on the pins and the case cradle. If cleaning doesn't fix it within the first year, it's a warranty case — register the product on day one.

5. "ANC pressure / hiss / wind roar"

Some people feel ANC as a pressure change; a faint hiss is the sound of cheap ANC processing; wind defeats every system. What helps: adaptive modes, a lower ANC level where offered, wind-reduction mode, and — for pressure-sensitive people — Bose, whose ANC produces the least "eardrum suck" in owner reports.

6. "Touch controls have a mind of their own"

Sony, Samsung (older models) and most budget touch pads register adjustments as taps. What helps: remapping or disabling gestures in the app; buying stems (Apple, Nothing, Buds4 Pro's pinch) if this drives you mad.

7. "People can't hear me on calls"

Wind and traffic beat most earbud mics; a poor seal makes it worse. What helps: Bose Gen 2, Sony XM6 and AirPods if calls are your job; wind mode; Apple's Voice Isolation. Avoid Technics, Sennheiser and Nothing for calls.

8. "They're fake" / "they were used"

Overwhelmingly an AirPods problem on marketplace listings. What helps: buy sold-by-Amazon.com or from Apple/Best Buy/Target/Walmart, check the separate L/R serials and Find My on arrival, and return within the window if anything is off.
