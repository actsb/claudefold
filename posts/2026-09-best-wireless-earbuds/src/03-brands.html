Brand by brand: what each one is actually good at

Apple — the default for iPhone owners, and now two very different defaults

Lineup: AirPods 5 ($129 / $149 with wireless case, from September 18) — open-fit, no tips, ANC now standard; AirPods Pro 3 ($249, $199 on sale) — sealed tips, Tier-1 ANC, heart rate, hearing features; AirPods 4 ($129 / $179 ANC) — the outgoing model, worth buying only at a deep clearance discount; AirPods Max for over-ear. Strengths: the frictionless pairing and switching between Apple devices that no rival matches, the best transparency mode, the best stock ear tips (Pro 3), the industry's only FDA-cleared hearing-aid feature, and the resale value. Weaknesses: on Android they are ordinary earbuds with no app; the AirPods 5's open fit means 4–5 hours with ANC and no seal for bass; no LDAC/aptX (irrelevant on iPhone). Owner complaints: counterfeits and "renewed" units sold as new by third-party sellers (the single most common AirPods complaint on Amazon), and a wave of AirPods Pro 3 static/crackle reports that firmware addressed for most owners. Buy Apple if you have an iPhone and either hate ear tips (AirPods 5) or want the best all-round pair for iOS (Pro 3).

Sony — the best all-rounder, on any phone

Lineup: WF-1000XM6 ($329.99) flagship; WF-1000XM5 (now discounted, still excellent); WF-C710N / C510 budget; LinkBuds open-ring and LinkBuds Fit. Strengths: the XM6 is the most complete pair on sale — Tier-1 ANC, natural detailed sound, the biggest mic improvement of any 2026 flagship, 9+ measured hours, LDAC and LC3, and every feature works identically on iPhone, Android and Windows. Sony's Headphones Connect app is the deepest in the business (Adaptive Sound Control that changes ANC by location, Speak-to-Chat, 360 Reality Audio, find-my-earbuds). Weaknesses: the price; a bulky case; fewer ear-tip options than rivals; touch controls that some owners find twitchy. Owner complaints: touch-control mis-taps, the case size, and XM5 owners disappointed the XM6 didn't change more. Buy Sony if you want one pair that is excellent at everything and you might change phones.

Bose — silence, refined

Lineup: QuietComfort Ultra Earbuds (2nd Gen) ($299, often $249); QuietComfort Earbuds (the cheaper 2024 model, ~$179); Ultra Open Earbuds ($299) for open-ear. Strengths: the deepest noise cancelling measured by any lab we read, the most comfortable "bulb" fit for many ears, and a second generation that fixed the first's gaps — wireless charging, better mics, Bluetooth 5.4 and LE Audio, and refined ANC behaviour. Immersive Audio is the best head-tracked spatial mode outside Apple. Weaknesses: six hours of battery; a large case; sound that is pleasant rather than revealing. Owner complaints: battery, occasional app connectivity quirks, and the first-gen's missing wireless charging (fixed). Buy Bose if the flight, the open office or the subway is the whole reason you're buying.

Samsung — superb, if you own a Galaxy

Lineup: Galaxy Buds4 Pro ($250) and Galaxy Buds4 ($180), both 2026; Buds3 Pro / Buds3 discounted; Buds FE budget. Strengths: "incredible" sound (Android Authority), some of the most comfortable buds on the market, ANC "close to AirPods, a little off Sony," a two-way speaker design, and the tightest Galaxy integration yet — Galaxy AI live translation and interpreter mode, seamless switching across Galaxy phones, tablets and watches. Amazon best-seller status shows how many Galaxy owners default to them. Weaknesses: six hours with ANC (4.5–5 with everything on) is short for a 2026 Pro; the headline features are locked to Samsung phones; no app on iPhone. SoundGuys called the launch software "a bit of a fixer-upper" — expect updates. Buy Samsung if you have a Galaxy phone and you'll keep it.

Google — the Pixel companion, quietly excellent

Lineup: Pixel Buds Pro 2 ($229, refreshed colours and features in 2026, often ~$189); Pixel Buds 2a ($130). Strengths: 8 hours with ANC on the Pro 2, Tier-2 ANC that the 2a nearly matches for $100 less, Gemini conversation in your ear, Find My Device, and a small, light design that suits smaller ears. Google skipped a Pro 3 this year, which means the Pro 2 will be discounted deeply this holiday season. Weaknesses: no app on iPhone; sound is good rather than great; a hard-to-open case is a recurring gripe. Buy Google if you have a Pixel, or any Android phone and want the best value premium pair.

Beats — Apple's chip, a hook, and a heart-rate sensor

Lineup: Powerbeats Pro 2 ($249.99, frequent $180–$200 sales); Studio Buds+; Beats Fit Pro. Strengths: the Powerbeats Pro 2 use Apple's H2 chip (so they behave like AirPods on an iPhone) but, unlike AirPods, ship an Android app; the ear hooks are the most secure fit in this guide; the heart-rate sensor is accurate enough that DC Rainmaker was impressed; 8 hours with ANC. Weaknesses: bulky case, and several reviewers hit pairing glitches, especially on iOS, at launch. Buy Beats if you train hard and want AirPods-style integration without the fall-out.

Nothing — design-led value

Lineup: Ear (3) ($179, sales to $149), Ear (a) ($79), Ear (3a) new for 2026. Strengths: the most distinctive design in the category, a premium metal case on the Ear (3), LDAC and Bluetooth 5.4, 6 hours with ANC (10 off), good stem-squeeze controls, and a polished app. Weaknesses: Tier-3 ANC and transparency; hi-res streaming hits battery hard; the "Super Mic" on the Ear (3) underwhelmed. Buy Nothing if you want a pair that looks like nothing else and you don't need flagship ANC.

Technics and Sennheiser — the audiophile pairs

Technics EAH-AZ100 ($299): "engagingly musical" sound, excellent comfort for many ears, great controls, 7.5 hours with ANC and 25 with the case — and a mediocre fit for some ears plus "surprisingly poor call quality." Sennheiser Momentum True Wireless 4 ($299.95): the best-sounding earbuds most reviewers have heard, 7.5 hours and 30 with the case, aptX Lossless — with average ANC and lacklustre mics. Both are for people who listen more than they call. Buy them if sound quality is the whole point; buy Sony if you want 90% of the sound with far better everything else.

Soundcore (Anker), EarFun, JLab — the budget tier that deserves the name

These three are the budget brands with real engineering, real apps, real firmware updates and real support. Soundcore Liberty 4 NC (~$80): LDAC, wireless charging, good hybrid ANC, 8 hours — SoundGuys' repeat pick under $100. EarFun Air Pro 4+: the new top pick in several roundups, with a claimed 12 hours in the buds. Soundcore P31i: an Amazon best-seller that adds real-time translation and 50-hour total playtime for under $60. JLab Go Pop ANC: the cheapest ANC that isn't a lie. Weaknesses: Tier-3 ANC, mics that fail in wind, and cases that feel their price. Buy them if you lose earbuds, share them with kids, or simply refuse to spend $200.

Shokz — open-ear done right

Lineup: OpenFit 2 ($179.95, seen at $139.95), OpenFit 2+ ($199.95, adds Dolby Audio and wireless charging), OpenFit Air (budget), OpenRun bone-conduction. Strengths: 11 hours per charge, 48 with the case, IP55, a hook that survives trail runs, and air-conduction sound that is a different world from bone conduction. TechRadar's verdict: "better than Bose" for the price. Weaknesses: no isolation by design, sound leaks at high volume, and no ANC. Buy Shokz if you run or cycle on roads, or you want to hear your kids and your office.
