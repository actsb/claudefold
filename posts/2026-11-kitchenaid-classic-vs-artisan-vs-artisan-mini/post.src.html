How do the Classic, Artisan, Mini and bowl-lift compare, spec by spec?

The Classic and Artisan are nearly the same machine in different clothes; the Mini is a smaller, weaker motor in a lighter body; the bowl-lift is a different design with more power and height.

The number on the box is the bowl's size, not what the beater can work with. America's Test Kitchen measured the working capacity of each bowl in its October 2026 test and found the 4.5-quart Classic holds slightly more than the 5-quart Artisan, because the Artisan's taller, narrower bowl leaves more dead space under the beater. The Mini's 3.5-quart bowl worked like 1.9 quarts, which is why ATK says it cannot fit double batches. KitchenAid's own figures say the same in cookies: up to 9 dozen for the 4.5- and 5-quart tilt-heads, up to 5 dozen for the Mini.

The questions people ask before buying

  • Will it fit under my cabinets? A tilt-head is about 14 inches tall head-down (the Mini about 12.3), but you pull it forward to lift the head; the bowl-lift is about 16.5 inches and needs no tilt room (KitchenAid). One Best Buy answer suggests half an inch more than the mixer's height.

How do you set up and use a KitchenAid mixer so it lasts?

Set the beater height with the dime test on day one and keep yeast dough on speed 2: those two habits head off most of the bouncing, overheating and stripped gears owners report.

  1. Check the model plate. Make sure the mixer says K45SS, KSM150, KSM3316 or KSM55, and that the box was not opened before. Walmart reviewers report "new" units that looked used.
  2. Do the dime test. KitchenAid's help page: put a dime in the empty stainless bowl, attach the flat beater and run it on speed 1. It should nudge the dime about ½ to 1 inch per sweep; if it drags it constantly the beater is too low, if it never touches it the beater is too high. Never do this in a glass or ceramic bowl. (One older KitchenAid page reportedly says about ¼ inch; either way, the beater should graze the dime, not grind it.)
  3. Turn the screw a little at a time. On a tilt-head the screw is in the hinge; the manual says clockwise lowers the beater and counterclockwise raises it. KitchenAid's help page advises no more than ⅛ to ¼ turn at a time; over-adjust and the head may not lock.
  4. Knead on speed 2 only. "Do not exceed Speed 2 when preparing yeast doughs as this may cause damage to the mixer," says KitchenAid's tilt-head manual; the Mini's manual adds not to use speed 1 for dough either. Knead about 2 minutes after the dough clings to the hook, then judge by feel.
  5. Keep within the flour limit. KitchenAid's international tilt-head instructions give 7 cups of all-purpose flour for the 4.5-quart and 8 cups for the 5-quart, 6 cups of whole wheat for either; we could not open the US manual to confirm it, so stay under those and split stiff doughs.
  6. Let it cool. If it stops or the head feels hot, unplug it and wait about 30 minutes, the manual's advice; back-to-back bread batches are the usual cause.

What's the catch with a KitchenAid tilt-head?

It is a superb cake and cookie mixer that tolerates bread rather than loves it, and it is heavy enough that many owners never move it.

Every tilt-head in this guide hinges its motor head over the bowl, and stiff dough pushes back on that hinge. That is the bouncing, walking and heat owners report, and why ATK, Food Network and KitchenAid itself all point serious bread bakers to bowl-lifts or other brands. The plastic worm gear that strips under strain is a feature, not a flaw: it is cheaper to replace than a motor.

  • Weight: 20 to 22 lb for the Classic and Artisan and 27.5 lb for the bowl-lift. If it has to live in a cupboard, the Mini's 16 lb or a hand mixer may get used more.
  • Usable space: none of the bowls hold their label's quarts of batter; plan on about two-thirds of the box figure for the tilt-heads, from ATK's measurements.
  • Prices are a moving target: our lines rest on past sales, and a deal announced for Cyber Monday could beat them, or not come at all.

Quick answers

KitchenAid Classic vs Artisan: what is the difference?

The Artisan has a 5-quart polished bowl with a handle, a pouring shield, a 325 W motor and more colours; the Classic has a 4.5-quart brushed bowl without a handle and a 275 W motor. America's Test Kitchen measured 3.25 quarts of usable space in the Classic and 3 in the Artisan (October 2026), and both make up to 9 dozen cookies a batch per KitchenAid.

Is the KitchenAid Artisan worth $70 more than the Classic?

Only if you value the bowl handle, the pouring shield and the colour choice. ATK found similar results from both, and Food Network and CNN Underscored still name the Artisan best overall. At under $300, last year's Cyber Monday price at Target, the gap gets small enough to pay.

Is the KitchenAid Artisan Mini worth it?

For a small kitchen and small batches, yes; otherwise no. ATK named it the best small mixer but measured 1.9 quarts of usable bowl and found it cannot fit double batches, and at $329.99 (KitchenAid.com, November 19, 2026) it costs more than the Classic.

Which KitchenAid mixer is best for bread dough?

A bowl-lift. KitchenAid itself recommends one for dense whole-grain bread and double batches, and the 5.5-quart KSM55 was $299.99 at Target on November 27, 2026. A Classic or Artisan handles one or two loaves at speed 2 with rests between batches.

What is a good Black Friday price for a KitchenAid Artisan?

Under $350 is fair and under $300 is good. It has sat at $349.99 at Target and KitchenAid.com since early November 2026; Target's 15% in-cart offer took it to $297.49 on November 30, 2025, and Lowe's sold it at $299 on December 26, 2025.

What is the KitchenAid KSM96 or KSM97?

Store models with the Classic's 4.5-quart body: the KSM96 at Target has a 300 W motor, and the Deluxe KSM97 at Walmart and KitchenAid.com adds a bowl with a handle. Both sold at $279.99 this week against a higher "reg" price than the Classic's.

Will a KitchenAid tilt-head fit under my kitchen cabinets?

Usually, head down: KitchenAid gives about 14 inches for the Classic and Artisan and about 12.3 inches for the Mini. You have to pull it forward to lift the head, and KitchenAid gives no height with the head raised, so measure before you buy.

Why is my KitchenAid mixer leaking oil?

KitchenAid says the food-grade grease in the gearbox separates over time, more in warm weather, with rare use or after the mixer was stored on its side, and that a few drops are not a concern. Run it empty on speed 10 for two minutes and wipe it; a full fix is a regrease at a service centre.

How long is the KitchenAid stand mixer warranty?

One year in the US for new stand mixers, with a replacement shipped to you and a prepaid label for the old one; registration is optional, but keep the receipt. KitchenAid's certified refurbished stand mixers also get one year.

Has any KitchenAid stand mixer been recalled?

No. The CPSC database shows no stand mixer recall and no KitchenAid recall of any product from 2023 to 2026, checked November 27, 2026. A viral claim about lead in KitchenAid beaters was rated false by USA TODAY.
